# Pricing and Packaging

How to price and package property / construction / architecture media.

## Two-tier model

- **Solo direct retainer — ~$1,500/mo all-in.** Low-friction entry. Undercuts incumbents visibly. Direct line to Hunter, fixed deliverables. The easy first yes.
- **10x agency packages — the upsell.** Marketing videos, photos, account management, lead-gen ads. Priced higher, sold once the relationship is proven. Don't lead with this; grow into it.

## Pricing approaches

- **Per-project** — good for one-off shoots (a single property, a hero film). Clear scope, clear number.
- **Monthly retainer** — the goal. Predictable revenue, compounding relationship, less selling per dollar earned.
- **Day-rate** — fallback for ad-hoc work; caps your upside, use sparingly.
- **Value-based over hourly.** Price the outcome (listings sold, leads generated, feed that converts), never the hours. Hourly punishes speed.

## Packaging tactics

- **Tiered good / better / best.** Three options anchor the middle and let the client self-select.
- **Bundle** shoot + edit + delivery into one named deliverable. Don't itemise the labour.
- **Name the deliverables clearly** — "16 cut-for-the-hook reels", not "video content". Counts and nouns the client can hold you to.

## Undercut + shortfall guarantee

From [[proposal-playbook]]: undercut the incumbent out loud (say the saving), then back fixed deliverables with a shortfall-carry-over guarantee — a month that falls short carries over free. Turns the promise into a mechanism.

## Anchoring

Anchor against the incumbent's current spend, not against zero. "$1,500 vs the $2,000 you pay now, for more output" reframes price as a saving.

## Scope control

- Define rounds/revisions up front (e.g. 2 rounds). Extra work = separately quoted.
- 90-day term, no lock-in beyond it, no setup fee — low risk for a burned prospect.
- Moving-target scope kills margin. Fix it in writing.

## Don't under-price AI-accelerated work

Speed from the [[ai-toolkit]] is your margin, not a discount. Price the outcome, not the effort. The client pays for the finished result and the judgement behind it — not the minutes the tool saved you.
