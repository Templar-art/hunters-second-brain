# Strobe Lighting

## Why strobe
- Clean, neutral, known-colour light source. Kills mixed-lighting cast (tungsten + LED + daylight fighting each other).
- Controls contrast — fills shadow without blowing windows.
- Adds punch and dimensionality that natural-light bracketing alone can't.
- The backbone of **flambient** (flash + ambient blend). See [[exposure-blending]].

## Gear
- **Speedlights** — light, cheap, battery, plenty of power for bounce interiors.
- **Monolights / battery strobes** (e.g. Godox AD200/AD300/AD400 Pro) — more power, faster recycle, consistent output for whole-house speed.
- Light stands, umbrella or softbox for shaped fill; often just **bare bounce** off ceiling/wall.
- Radio triggers (Godox X system etc.) for off-camera control.

## Core interior technique
- **Bounce** off the ceiling or back wall for soft, even, wraparound fill — never bare flash at the room.
- **Flambient**: shoot one flash frame + your ambient/bracket frames from the same tripod position, then blend the flash frame in for clean colour and shadow control.
- **Multiple pops**: fire into different zones (far corner, hallway, adjacent room), one frame each, then composite the lit areas together for even coverage.
- **Feather** the flash — aim the edge of the beam at the subject, not the hotspot, to avoid bright patches on ceilings and walls.

## Gels for white-balance matching
- Match the flash to the dominant ambient so they agree, then correct globally.
- **CTO / CTS** — warm flash to match tungsten ambient.
- **CTB** — cool flash for daylight-balanced scenes.
- **Plus/minus green** — match fluorescent / cheap LED spikes.
- Get flash and ambient onto the same colour, then set one WB in post. See [[interior-photography]].

## Balancing strobe with ambient
- **Drag the shutter** — slow shutter keeps window views, lamp glow, and room ambience alive; flash sets the key light.
- Aperture/ISO fix the ambient exposure; flash power fixes the fill. They're separate controls.
- Goal: looks naturally lit, not flashed.

## When strobe beats pure natural-light bracketing
- Deep or dark rooms with little window light.
- Ugly mixed sources you can't fix in post.
- Twilight interiors (hold the window view + light the room).
- When you need **consistent colour across a whole house, fast**.

## Technical notes
- Manual flash power (not TTL) for repeatable, consistent frames.
- Watch sync speed (~1/200s typical); use **HSS** only when you must shoot above it (bright exteriors, wide apertures).
- Off-camera via triggers is the standard — on-camera bounce only for speed runs.
