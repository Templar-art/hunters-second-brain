# Architectural Composition

Framing buildings and interiors so they read clean, deep, and intentional.

## Perspective: one-point vs two-point

- **One-point** — camera square-on to a wall/facade, sensor plane parallel. Symmetrical, formal, graphic. Use for: hero facades, corridor/hallway shots, symmetrical rooms, feature walls. Only one vanishing point; verticals and one axis stay parallel.
- **Two-point** — shoot the corner at three-quarter. Two walls visible, converging to two vanishing points. Shows depth, volume, and how a space wraps. Use for: most interiors, exterior hero angles, showing a room's true size.
- Decide per subject: symmetry and order → one-point; space and dimension → two-point.

## Keep the camera level

- Verticals must stay vertical. Tilting up/down makes walls converge (keystone). See [[perspective-correction]].
- Use the electronic/bubble level. Level side-to-side AND front-to-back.
- Level in-camera; don't rely solely on post — correcting stretches and crops the frame.

## Order in the frame

- **Rule of thirds** for asymmetric/two-point shots — place the corner, key furniture, or horizon off-centre.
- **Centered symmetry** for one-point — commit fully; a slightly-off symmetrical shot looks like a mistake.
- **Leading lines** — floors, benches, ceilings, paths, handrails draw the eye in.
- **Layering** — build foreground / mid / background depth (e.g. bench > island > window view).

## Interiors specifics

- Shoot into a **corner** to show two walls — instantly communicates room size and layout.
- Camera height **chest height, ~1.2–1.5 m**. High enough to see over furniture surfaces, low enough to keep ceiling and a natural eyeline. Too high flattens; too low exaggerates floor.
- Give rooms **breathing room** — include a doorway, a slice of adjoining space, negative space. Don't jam the lens into a corner.
- **Clutter** — remove before shooting, not after. Anything in frame you'd clone out, move it now. Watch reflections in glass/TVs/splashbacks for the photographer and gear.

## Exteriors specifics

- Frame to **separate the building from sky and neighbours** — step to an angle where rooflines don't merge with power lines, trees, or the next house.
- Leave sky room for a replacement/interest, but don't drown the subject.
- Watch the base: cars, bins, wires. Reframe or plan a clone-out.
