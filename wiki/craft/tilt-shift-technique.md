# Tilt-Shift Technique

The tilt-shift (perspective-control) lens — the pro architecture tool. Corrects verticals optically, in-camera, before post.

## Rise / Fall (shift up/down)
- Camera stays dead level; shift the lens up to include a building top, or down for a floor.
- Verticals stay parallel — no keystoning, no post-stretch, no lost resolution. The cleanest fix vs software [[perspective-correction]].
- Level the camera first (built-in level / bubble), then dial in shift to frame.

## Shift sideways
- Shoot glass/mirrors around your own reflection while keeping the camera square to the surface.
- Parallax-free frames for [[panorama-stitching]] — shift L/C/R from a fixed body.

## Tilt
- Tilts the plane of focus (Scheimpflug) — align sharpness to a receding plane (facade, tabletop) for front-to-back sharpness without stopping down.
- Or the "miniature/toy" fake-shallow look. Less used in real estate; handy for detail.

## Handling
- Meter and LOCK exposure BEFORE shifting — metering changes as the lens shifts (vignetting shifts the reading). Use manual exposure.
- Manual focus always — no autofocus.
- Sturdy tripod + geared head for precise, repeatable movements.
- Watch vignetting and soft corners at extreme shift — back off if edges degrade.

## Typical focal lengths
- 17mm — wide interiors, tight exteriors.
- 24mm — general interiors + exteriors, the workhorse.
