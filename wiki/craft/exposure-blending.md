# Exposure Blending

Getting clean windows, lit interiors, and visible exteriors in one final frame — the core interiors problem.

## Bracketing

- Shoot **3–7 frames, ~2 EV apart**, covering shadows through blown highlights (window/exterior).
- **Tripod mandatory.** Electronic shutter or 2s timer / remote to avoid vibration; lock mirror on DSLRs.
- **Vary shutter speed only.** Fix aperture, fix focus, fix ISO, fix white balance across the whole bracket.
- Shoot **RAW**.

## Flambient (flash + ambient)

- The real-estate standard for natural interiors.
- Shoot an **ambient bracket** (for window pulls and natural mood) plus a **flash frame** (bounce flash off ceiling/wall for clean, neutral, colour-accurate base light).
- Blend: flash frame gives clean shadows and true colour; ambient frames restore window views, lamp glow, and warmth.
- Solves **mixed lighting** (tungsten + daylight + LED) because the flash overrides colour-cast ambient in the base layer.

## Window pulls

- Expose one frame correctly for the **exterior** (much darker exposure).
- **Mask** that exterior into the blown-out window of the base frame. Now inside and the view outside both read correctly.

## Manual blending vs HDR

- Prefer **manual luminosity-mask blending** for real estate — natural, believable, controllable.
- Avoid one-click **HDR** — the halos, grunge, and over-cooked local contrast scream "processed" and cheapen the property.
- Build the shot: clean base (flash or mid exposure) → paint in windows → paint in shadow detail → warmth from ambient.

## Ghosting

- Moving trees, curtains, ceiling fans, water, and people **ghost** between frames.
- Shoot fast between frames, or take the moving element from a single frame and mask it in cleanly.
