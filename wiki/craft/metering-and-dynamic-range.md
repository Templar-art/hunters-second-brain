# Metering & Dynamic Range

Reading and exposing high-DR architectural scenes — bright windows against dark interiors.

## Read the range, not the picture
- Judge exposure by the HISTOGRAM, never the LCD image (its brightness lies).
- Highlight alert / zebras / blinkies find clipped windows and sky.
- Spot-check the brightest window and the darkest shadow — that spread tells you if one frame holds it or you must bracket.

## Exposing the base
- ETTR (expose to the right): push the base exposure as bright as possible without clipping recoverable highlights — maximises shadow detail and lowers noise.
- Protect highlights in RAW: you can lift shadows far more than you can recover blown highlights. When unsure, err darker on the window.
- Base ISO (100) for maximum dynamic range.

## Deciding the bracket
- Set bracket spread from the actual scene range: enough frames so the darkest exposure holds window/sky detail and the brightest opens deepest shadows.
- Typical interior: 3–5 frames at ~2 EV steps; wide window-to-shadow gaps need more. Feeds [[exposure-blending]].

## Metering modes
- Evaluative/matrix for a starting exposure.
- Spot-meter the bright window to read exactly how far it clips.

## Mirrorless advantages
- Live histogram + exposure preview before the shot.
- Zebras show clipping in the EVF in real time — dial exposure by eye.

## One frame vs bracket
- Single exposure holds it when the range fits the sensor (~overcast, blinds drawn, dim even light).
- Bracket when the window/sky is far brighter than the interior — most daytime interiors.
