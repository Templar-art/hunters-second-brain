# Real Estate vs Architectural

The craft and positioning split between volume real-estate photography and high-end architectural/editorial work. Same camera, different jobs.

## Real estate
- Sells the **listing** — gets the property leased/sold fast.
- Fast, high-volume, agent-driven.
- Quick turnaround (often next-day).
- Portal-spec deliverables. See [[deliverables-and-export]].
- Bright, clean, inviting look; flambient/flash for even light.
- Standard coverage, standard angles. See [[shot-list-and-planning]].
- Priced per-property / by volume; margin from throughput and efficiency.

## Architectural / editorial
- Sells the **design** and the maker's craft.
- Client is the architect, builder, designer, or a publication.
- Slow, precise, heavily planned and styled.
- Waits for the right light. See [[light-and-timing]].
- Tilt-shift for true verticals. See [[tilt-shift-technique]].
- Detail + abstract coverage — materials, junctions, form. See [[architectural-details]].
- Fewer but flawless images.
- Higher day-rate + licensing; margin from craft and rights, not volume.

## How they differ
| | Real estate | Architectural |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Agent | Architect/builder/publication |
| Goal | Sell the listing | Show the design |
| Pace | Fast | Slow, considered |
| Shot list | Standard, broad | Curated, hero-led |
| Styling | Light declutter | Detailed, art-directed |
| Light | Any/flash | Waits for the shot |
| Volume | Many images | Few, perfect |
| Pricing | Per-property | Day-rate + licence |

See [[shot-list-and-planning]] and [[pricing-and-packaging]].

## Where 10x plays
Both. Volume RE keeps cash flow and agent relationships; architectural/editorial builds the brand and commands rate. Use RE work as the on-ramp.

## Moving a relationship up
- Do standout work on a builder's/architect's RE listing.
- Show the difference — offer a twilight hero or a styled detail set as a taste.
- Reframe from "listing photos" to "brand/portfolio media" for their marketing and awards submissions.
- Introduce licensing and a day-rate model as the work gets more considered.
- Target builders/architects who compete on design, not price.
