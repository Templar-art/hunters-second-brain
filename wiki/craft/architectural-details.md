# Architectural Details

Detail, abstract/graphic, and black-and-white shots — beyond the wide hero.

## Why they matter
- Sell craftsmanship, materials, and design intent up close.
- Pad and pace a gallery; give the set rhythm.
- Strong social/editorial content — the frames that travel.

## Detail shots
- Cladding, junctions, joinery, textures, thresholds, hardware, stair/handrail meetings, reveals.
- Use a standard/short-tele ~35–85mm for compression and clean isolation (ref [[photo-gear-and-settings]]) — not the ultra-wide.
- Get square to the surface; keep the plane sharp.

## Abstract / graphic
- Isolate line, rhythm, repetition, shadow, negative space.
- Fill the frame. Use symmetry deliberately.
- Look up and along facades for pattern; crop tight.

## Black & white
- Convert when form/line/light/tone carry the image better than colour.
- Best subjects: strong directional light, high contrast, graphic geometry.
- Shoot RAW colour, convert in post — keep the channels.
- Watch sky-vs-building tonal separation: darken sky via red/orange channel in the B&W mix, or use a polariser at capture.

## Light for detail
- Raking side light reveals texture and relief (ref [[light-and-timing]]). Flat light kills materials.

## Consistency
- Keep detail frames on the same grade as the wide set (ref [[color-grading]]) so the gallery reads as one body of work.
