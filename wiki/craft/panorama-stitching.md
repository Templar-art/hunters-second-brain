# Panorama Stitching

Combine overlapping frames for more width/height without ultra-wide distortion, or for huge print resolution.

## Why
- Tight rooms need width, but ultra-wide lenses distort and exaggerate room size — stitch a normal/moderate lens for natural perspective and honest proportions.
- Massive resolution for large prints and detailed crops.
- Cover a facade or atrium that no single lens frames cleanly.

## Methods
**(a) Rotational pano** — rotate camera on tripod around the lens's no-parallax point (nodal point), not the tripod head.
- Overlap frames ~30%.
- Manual and fixed: exposure, focus, WB. Level the head.
- Best when there's no critical near foreground.

**(b) Shift-lens pano** — with a [[tilt-shift-technique]] lens, shift left / centre / right from a fixed camera.
- Parallax-free — camera body never moves, so no ghosting.
- Keeps verticals dead straight — the cleanest architectural stitch. Best for interiors/exteriors.

**Vertical panos** — shift/tilt up-centre-down for tall buildings, stairwells, atriums.

## Software
- Lightroom Photo Merge > Panorama (Perspective / Cylindrical / Spherical projections; Boundary Warp to fill edges).
- PTGui for hard sets, control points, big commercial stitches.

## Pitfalls
- Parallax ghosting on near objects if you don't rotate about the nodal point — shift-lens avoids it entirely.
- Moving people, cars, curtains, water = ghosts. Time the frames.
- Rolling shutter warps moving elements.
- Boundary Warp bends straight lines — check verticals, crop instead if it distorts.
- Sometimes a single well-composed wide frame is simply cleaner — don't stitch for the sake of it.
