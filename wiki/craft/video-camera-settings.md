# Video Camera Settings

## 180-degree shutter rule
- Shutter ≈ 1/(2×fps). Locks natural motion blur so movement reads cinematically.
- 24fps → 1/50. 25fps → 1/50. 30fps → 1/60. 60fps → 1/120. 120fps → 1/250.
- Too fast = staccato/gatey motion. Too slow = smeared. Hold the rule; change exposure with ND, not shutter.

## Frame rates
- 24 / 25fps — standard cinematic base. Match project/region (25 in AU broadcast).
- 50 / 60fps — smoothness and mild slow-mo; conform to 24/25 for silky slow moves.
- 120fps — heavy slow-mo (water, dust, hero reveals). Needs more light.
- Pick per shot to serve the edit; don't shoot everything high and hope.

## Resolution & codec
- 4K minimum. Deliver 4K or downsample to 1080 for extra sharpness/noise reduction.
- 10-bit 4:2:2 for grading headroom (banding-free skies, clean sky replacements).
- High bitrate; ALL-I over Long-GOP where storage allows — cleaner grade, easier edit.

## Log / flat profile
- Shoot log (S-Log, C-Log, V-Log, etc.) or a flat profile for maximum dynamic range.
- Expose to protect highlights — architecture blows out windows/skies fast; hold them.
- Shoot at the sensor's native/dual-ISO base ISO for cleanest signal; step to second base if dark.
- Rate log ~1–1.5 stops brighter than the metered "correct" to lift shadows out of noise (ETTR discipline).

## Exposure tools
- Variable ND is the primary exposure control — keep shutter and ISO fixed, dial ND for aperture.
- Avoid stacking heavy variable ND at extreme end (cross/X-polarization banding); use fixed ND if needed.
- Monitor exposure with histogram, false color, zebras and waveform — not the LCD brightness.
- Zebras ~94–100 to guard highlight clipping; false color for precise skin/facade placement.

## Discipline
- White balance manual and fixed per setup/lighting — never auto. Match across a scene.
- Focus manual or reliable AF; disable hunting/face-jitter. Pull focus deliberately on reveals.
- Lock one picture profile per shoot so every clip matches in grade — no mixing log flavors.
