# Board Committee BTS — Edit Plan

_10x client job — company context: [[overview]]._

> Studio shoot BTS for a board/committee. Goal: make serious people feel **human, likeable, and cool** while showing 10x makes premium media look effortless. Personality first, polish second.

## The one rule
**Personality leads, process supports.** Most corporate BTS fails because it's 90% gear and 10% people. Flip it. The lighting/setup shots are the *connective tissue* between human moments — not the main event. If a cut doesn't show either a person's character or a genuinely cool visual, it earns its place only as a quick transition.

## Selects pass first (do this before cutting)
Scrub all footage and pull two bins:
- **PERSONALITY** — laughs, reactions, someone nailing a take, a quiet confident look, banter between members, getting mic'd up and joking, the "ok I'm ready" moment. Anything with a real human spark.
- **COOL** — best camera moves, the light kicking on, slider reveals, a striking portrait on the monitor, lens flares, macro detail, the "studio looks expensive" frames.

The edit is built by **alternating** from these two bins. That rhythm is what makes it feel alive instead of a slideshow.

> Protect the talent: cut anything unflattering (mid-blink, awkward, caught off-guard badly). They're the client's leadership.

## Structure (~25–35s vertical, can extend for 16:9)

**1. Cold open — a COOL frame + a PERSONALITY beat back to back (0–3s)**
Open on your single most cinematic shot (light switching on, slider across the set, sharp portrait snapping into focus on the monitor). Immediately cut to a person reacting/laughing. Hook = "this looks expensive" + "these people are likeable." Both in 3 seconds.

**2. Build — alternate COOL ↔ PERSONALITY on the beat (3–18s)**
- Light stand going up → member joking while getting mic'd
- Gimbal/slider move → you/Kris dialing it in, focused
- Macro detail (lens, collar, monitor) → a confident look to camera
- Keep it moving, cut on music hits. This is the personality showcase — front-load your best human moments here, not at the end.

**3. The "rolling" moment (18–24s)**
The actual take. Member delivering to camera, calm and commanding. Let one beat breathe slightly longer here — the only place you slow down.

**4. Reveal — match-cut to the finished portrait (24–28s)**
Cut from your BTS angle straight to the **graded hero portrait/frame**, ideally same framing. The payoff and the quality flex.

**5. Button (28–32s)**
Final clean hero frame holds → subtle 10x logo sting. End on confidence.

## Craft notes
- **Pacing:** controlled and confident, not frantic. Board-level dignity. Smooth moves, clean cuts on the beat — energy comes from rhythm, not whip-pans.
- **Diegetic audio under the music:** "rolling," the shutter, a light clicking on, a genuine laugh left audible for half a second. This is what sells *real* personality vs. staged.
- **Grade:** BTS slightly rawer than the hero portrait so the reveal pops. Light, warm, premium — keep skin tones flattering.
- **Detail inserts:** lens, catchlight in the eye, hands adjusting mic/collar, the framed portrait on the monitor. Texture between wider shots.

## Music
Pull from the **Corporate** vibe tag in the library. Refined build, restrained — land the reveal match-cut on a soft swell, not a hard drop. No aggressive energy; it clashes with board tone.

## Exports
- **9:16** primary — LinkedIn/Reels (this audience lives on LinkedIn). Reframe deliberately, punch in for vertical.
- **16:9** secondary — website / longer cut, can extend the build section.
- Captions on the muted-friendly version.

## Where it slots
Post the polished portraits/committee film first, then this BTS as the **"how we made your leadership look this good"** follow-up. Doubles as a 10x sales asset for prospective corporate clients.
