01 Logo System

Master Logo

The CVA Architects logo is monochrome and appears only in black or white. Always use the official logo files. Do not alter, redraw, or typeset the logo in any program.

Primary: Black

Use on white or light backgrounds. Minimum contrast requirement: background must be below 30% opacity tint.

Inverted: White

Use on dark backgrounds or over imagery at 40%+ opacity. Ensures mark remains clear and high contrast.

Small Mark: Wordmark Only

Remove "ARCHITECTS" at small sizes. Use for favicons, social avatars, and tight spaces where the full logo becomes illegible.

01.2 Logo Clear Space

Clear Space Rules

The "x" value references the width of the "A" in the logo. Always maintain clear space to keep the mark clean and uncluttered.

0.25x above 0.25x below 0.5x 0.5x
✓
Minimum Clear Space0.25x top and bottom; 0.5x left and right, where x equals the width of the "A" letterform.
✓
Extra bufferKeep all text, graphics, and other logos at least 0.5x outside the marked clear space zone.
✕
Page edgesNever place the logo too close to page edges or trim lines. Apply the same clear space rules.
✕
Busy backgroundsAvoid placing the logo over complex imagery that reduces legibility, even when clear space is maintained.

01.3 Logo Restrictions

Logo Don'ts

Never present the CVA logo in any of these variations. If unsure, contact the CVA Architects marketing team for approval.

Don't change the logo colour

Don't add shadows or effects

Don't stretch horizontally

Don't stretch vertically

Don't rotate or angle the logo

Don't use tints or reduced opacity

02.1 Colour Application

Colour Usage Rules

The palette is intentionally minimal. Restraint preserves the refined architectural quality of the brand and keeps the focus on the work.

✓
Base layersUse Charcoal and White as the base for all marketing materials.
✓
Supporting greysUse grey scale sparingly for structure only: backgrounds, dividers, captions.
✓
Light, minimal layoutsKeep layouts predominantly light so project photography remains the visual hero.
✓
Strong contrastMaintain strong contrast between text and background for readability across web, print, and social.
✕
No accent coloursDo not introduce additional accent or brand colours without marketing team approval.
✕
No colour fillsDo not use grey tones as feature or hero colours. They are structural only.
✕
No gradientsThe CVA palette is flat and monochrome. Gradients undermine the clean architectural identity.
✕
No tinted logosNever print the logo in grey tints. Use solid black or solid white only.

04 Tone of Voice

How CVA Speaks

CVA speaks the same way it designs: thoughtful, considered, and tailored to the people they work with. Every word should have a purpose.

01

Calm and Clear

Speak with calm, clear, direct language. Avoid heavy jargon and over-explaining. Keep language simple, precise, and free from architectural terminology unless clearly explained.

02

Client-First

Focus on the client's life and experience before the architecture. Talk about "your home", "the way you live", and "what matters to you". Never push a fixed CVA style.

03

Confident, Not Showy

Let the quality of the work and thinking speak for itself. The tone is professional and assured, never arrogant. Guide rather than push. Help clients make informed decisions.

04

No Filler

Every sentence should have a purpose and add to the client's understanding. No hype, no filler. If removing a sentence doesn't change the meaning, remove it.

05

Spatial Language

Describe spaces in terms of light, proportion, outlook, and flow, so people can imagine how their home will feel, not just how it will look.

06

Collaborative

Treat architecture as a collaborative process. Invite questions and conversation. Acknowledge constraints, outline options, and make it clear you're working together toward the best outcome.

Voice in Practice

✓ On brand

"We designed the living area around the afternoon light. The north-facing corner draws the sun in and keeps the space warm year-round without relying on heating."

✕ Off brand

"Our award-winning passive solar design methodology optimises thermal mass and fenestration ratios for maximum year-round comfort outcomes."

✓ On brand

"Tell us how you use your home: morning routines, how often you entertain, where the kids spend most of their time. That's where we start."

✕ Off brand

"Our comprehensive client brief process leverages cutting-edge design thinking to deliver bespoke architectural solutions tailored to your lifestyle parameters."

04.1 Communication

Communication Principles

Whether presenting a concept, writing copy, or sending an email. Every communication should make decisions easier for the client.

⬜

Clarity

Lead with clarity. Keep messages simple, direct, and easy to act on. Cut through noise. Avoid dense technical language and long explanations. Use clear, direct wording that helps clients understand key choices and trade-offs.

◎

Client-led

Always link design ideas back to everyday experience. Each project is shaped around the client's individual nature, routines, and aspirations. Our language reflects that: we talk about "your home" and "what matters to you".

◈

Confidence

We bring experience and strong design thinking, expressed in a grounded way. Professional and assured, never arrogant. Give confident recommendations instead of sitting on the fence.

⬡

Collaborative

Treat architecture as a collaborative process between client, architect, and builder. Our writing invites questions and conversation. We acknowledge constraints and make it clear we are working together toward the best outcome.

○

Everyday Experience

We talk about homes as places to live, not objects to admire. We emphasise natural light, ventilation, connection to outdoors, and how spaces feel throughout the day and across the seasons.

□

Explain the Why

Our aim is to make decisions easier. Explain the "why" behind each recommendation and relate it back to the client's lifestyle, budget, and priorities. Help clients move forward with confidence.

05 Imagery

Imagery Guidelines

Project photography and renders are the heart of the CVA brand. The minimal monochrome palette exists to frame and elevate the work, not to compete with it.

✓ Approved Image Types

  • High-quality architectural renders of CVA designs
  • Photography of completed CVA projects
  • Team portraits and behind-the-scenes studio content
  • In-progress site photography documenting CVA builds
  • Detail shots of materials, finishes, and spatial moments

✕ Prohibited Image Types

  • Stock photography of any kind
  • Generic architecture shots from other practices
  • Imagery sourced from other firms or photo libraries
  • Heavily filtered or heavily edited photography
  • Renders or images that misrepresent actual CVA work
