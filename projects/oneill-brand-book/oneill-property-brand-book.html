The brand in one page03 / 23
Position and principles

A family name,
not a franchise

O’Neill Property is a Northern Gold Coast agency built around one belief: property is personal. The name on the board is the name the family answers to, and that is the reason the standard has to hold from the first appraisal to the last signature.

The brand should feel established without being old-fashioned, sophisticated without being pretentious, and thoroughly professional without losing its warmth.

The promise

Listen first, give you the straight version, and run the campaign properly to the end.

Who it is for

Owners and buyers on the northern Gold Coast, from a first apartment to a home on the water. The same standard at every price point.

What it is not

A franchise template with a local name dropped into it. Nothing in this book should be reachable by editing someone else’s.

The logo system05 / 23
Clear space and minimum size

Give it the
height of the O

The exclusion zone on every side is the cap height of the O in the wordmark. Nothing enters it: no type, no rule, no photograph edge, no other logo, no portal badge.

  • Minimum sizes. Primary 40 mm wide in print, 150 px on screen. Secondary 28 mm, 110 px. Icon 8 mm, 32 px. Below those the counters in the O and the apostrophe start to fill in, and on a signboard at speed it stops reading as a name.
  • On a vehicle or a building fascia, set the clear space from the physical panel edge, not from the artwork bounding box. A mark that sits hard against a shut line reads as a sticker.
Clear space and minimum size06 / 23
Colourways

Four finishes,
chosen by ground

Pick the version that gives the mark the most contrast against what is behind it. Never tint the artwork to make a fifth.

  • Over photography, use Stone or white and place the mark on the quietest part of the frame. If no part of the frame is quiet enough, the mark goes on a solid band beside the picture rather than being given a drop shadow.
  • The app icon is not settled. Stone on Basalt and Sage Grey on Basalt are both nominated for it in this edition. See spread 22.

How much
of each

Half of everything is Sage Grey. It is the field the brand is built on, not an accent applied to a white one, and it is the colour people will use when they describe the identity to someone else.

This book is set to its own rule. Of twenty-three spreads, nine are Sage Grey, seven are Stone and seven are Basalt, two of those Basalt sheets given over to a photograph rather than to type, and Deep Sage appears only as rules and markers.

Measured, not assumed

WCAG 2.1. Body copy needs 4.5:1. Large text, meaning 24 px and above or 19 px bold, needs 3:1.

  • Sage Grey on Basalt6.1 : 1  ·  all text
  • Basalt on Stone8.2 : 1  ·  all text
  • Stone on Basalt8.2 : 1  ·  all text
  • Basalt on Sage Grey6.1 : 1  ·  all text
  • Deep Sage on Stone2.2 : 1  ·  do not set type
  • Stone on Sage Grey1.3 : 1  ·  do not set type

The sage rule. No sage in this palette can carry body copy: not Sage Grey, not Deep Sage. If words have to be read, set them in Basalt, or put them on Basalt.

Balance and legibility10 / 23
Typography

Typography

Aa
Display

Sinhala MN

Headlines, campaign statements, addresses on a board, the moments that carry the name. One weight. Never below 18 px, where its contrast breaks down and it stops looking premium.

Aa
Text

Inter

Everything that has to work hard: body copy, navigation, agent details, captions, forms, signage information. Weights 300, 400, 500 and 600. Nothing heavier.

Two families, never a third. Where the display face is unavailable, substitute a restrained high-contrast serif and keep Inter as the working sans. Which serif is still an open decision: see spread 22.

One display size per layout. Two competing headline sizes read as a template rather than a design.

Measure runs to about 65 characters. Never justify, never hyphenate, never track body copy.

Tracking. The wordmark is fixed and never adjusted. Eyebrows .24em, captions .12em, both uppercase only. Display sits at zero to .01em, a serif needs almost none. Body copy is never tracked.

Caps. Reserved for lockups, eyebrows and captions. A paragraph in all caps stops being read and starts being scanned, which is the opposite of what listing copy needs.

Headlines. Short, with space around them. If a headline needs a third line at display size, the headline is too long, not the type too big.

Campaign language14 / 23
Signage

Read it at
fifty kilometres

A board has about two seconds of a driver’s attention. One message, one mark, one number. Everything else belongs on the phone they will pick up afterwards.

The rules
  • One campaign word. Own It, Preparing It, Lease It or Achieved. Never two on the same board.
  • The mark sits on Basalt. A dark head band reads from further away than a mark floating on sage, and it stops the board disappearing against a hedge.
  • The agent is the smallest element. Name, then number, at the foot. A face on a board is optional; a legible phone number is not.
  • Sage carries display type only. Basalt on Sage Grey measures 6.1:1 and is fine at any size. Nothing lighter than Basalt goes on sage.
  • Directional arrows point, they do not decorate. One chevron, on the side it points to, at the size of the campaign word.
Signage15 / 23
Print and stationery

The same system,
smaller

Nothing new is invented at A4 or at card size. The same field, the same mark, the same one campaign word, set to the same ratio.

Stock and finish
  • Uncoated, always. 350 gsm for cards, 170 for flyers. Gloss undoes the whole palette: these are chalky, natural colours and a shine reads as budget.
  • Stone is the paper. Specify a warm uncoated stock rather than printing a near-white flat. It saves ink and it looks better than the ink would.
  • Ask for a rich black. Basalt as a flat single black will look grey and patchy across a large solid.
  • No foil, no spot gloss, no bevel. If a piece needs lifting, lift it with paper weight or an edge paint in Basalt, never with shine.
  • One display size per piece. The campaign word is the only thing set large. The address is the next size down, the agent smaller again.
Print and stationery16 / 23
Photography

Let the light
do the work

Natural light, honest colour, architectural framing. The property is the hero. If a viewer notices the photography before the house, the shoot has failed.

Do
  • Shoot early or late. Long shadows and warm light suit the palette; midday sun blows out the water and flattens the render.
  • Square the verticals. A leaning doorframe is the fastest way to make a premium home look like a rental listing.
  • Leave the room breathing. Fewer props, no staged towels folded into swans, nothing that will not be there on inspection day.
  • Keep colour honest. Grass is not emerald, timber is not orange, and the sky over the Broadwater is not turquoise.
Do not
  • Composite a sunset that was not there, or drop a sky in that does not match the shadows on the ground.
  • Run a filter, a vignette, or a heavy graduated wash across the frame.
  • Set type across the middle of a photograph. Type sits on a solid field beside the picture, or on the quietest corner of it.
  • Use a stock image of a house that is not the house. Ever.
Type on photography

Where Stone type must sit on an image, the image gets a measured scrim, not a guess. Stone needs 4.5:1 against the pixels actually behind it, and a flat sage wash cannot deliver that at any opacity.

Every scrim on the website was measured against the real frame. Those ratios are recorded in the page files, not in this book.

Prefer the field to the scrim. A headline on a solid Basalt, Sage Grey or Stone panel beside the picture always beats a headline fighting the picture.

Non-negotiables

When in doubt,
take something
away

This brand is built on restraint. Almost every way it can go wrong is an addition: one more colour, one more typeface, one more badge, one more adjective.

The test

Is the property the hero?

If the answer is the agency, start again.

Never
  • Gold, metallics, gradients or glossy black.
  • A third typeface, or a script anywhere.
  • Sage as a full page flood.
  • Superlatives: stunning, breathtaking, must see, once in a lifetime.
  • A dash used as punctuation. Rewrite the sentence instead.
Always
  • Australian English.
  • The clear space, even when it is inconvenient.
  • One campaign word per piece.
  • Measured contrast wherever type meets a photograph.
  • The plain word beside the campaign word where status must be instant.
Where a supplier pushes back

Portals, franchisor templates and sign fabricators will all ask for a compromise at some point. Grant the smallest one that solves their problem, write down what was granted and why, and never let it become the new default.

Where this book is silent

Nothing here is a licence to invent. If a piece needs something this book does not cover, the answer is the nearest rule it does cover, applied with less rather than more.

Open decisions and exceptions

Not settled
in this edition

Six items. Each one is either a decision nobody has made yet or a correction that has been decided and not yet shipped. None of them are guessed at elsewhere in this book.

Decided, not yet shipped

The Basalt value on the website

The master artwork is #3A3A3A. The site ships #2D2D2D. The artwork wins. Until the token is changed, the two do not match and any contrast measured against #2D2D2D has to be taken again.

Open decision

The display face substitute

Sinhala MN is available on Apple devices only. A restrained high-contrast serif has to be nominated for Windows, for Android and for any printer that cannot embed the original. Until it is, the CSS in this file falls back to Georgia and then Times: a rendering fallback, not a nomination.

Open decision

Pantone, paint and CMYK

References are still to be selected against a physical swatch book, under the light the sign or the paint will actually live in. No CMYK build is published in this edition: the printer sets one and proofs it, and Basalt across a large field is specified as a rich black.

Pending reprint

The retired domain

The business card and the photo board still carry oneillgroup.com.au. It comes off at the next print run, not by overprinting, and it comes out of the site at the same time.

Contradiction to resolve

The app icon colourway

Spread 07 nominates Stone on Basalt and Sage Grey on Basalt for the app icon. Both cannot be the default. Pick one, and leave the other to the social avatar.

Recorded elsewhere

The measured scrim ratios

Every scrim on the website was measured against its real frame, but the ratios live in the page files rather than in this book. Anyone setting type on a new image measures that image, and records the number where the next person will find it.

Open decisions and exceptions22 / 23
Quick reference

Before it
goes out

Nothing new on this page. Every line is a rule from an earlier spread, in the order a piece of work usually breaks.

The mark and the field
  • One of the three supplied lockups, unmodified.
  • Clear space on all four sides is the cap height of the O.
  • At or above minimum size: 40 mm / 150 px primary, 28 mm / 110 px secondary, 8 mm / 32 px icon.
  • Colourway chosen for contrast against the ground behind it.
  • Sage Grey is carrying the piece, not accenting it.
  • Deep Sage is doing graphic work only.
Type and words
  • Two families only: Sinhala MN for display, Inter for everything else.
  • One display size in the layout.
  • No type set in any sage, at any size.
  • Body copy measures 4.5:1 against its ground; large text 3:1.
  • One campaign word, with the plain status word beside it where clarity is required.
  • Australian English, no superlatives, no dash as punctuation.
Image and production
  • Natural light, squared verticals, honest colour, no replaced sky.
  • No type across the middle of a photograph; measured scrim or a solid field.
  • Uncoated stock, Stone as the paper, rich black for large Basalt.
  • No gloss, foil, gradient, bevel or shadow anywhere.
  • oneillgroup.com.au does not appear.
  • The property, not the agency, is the hero.
