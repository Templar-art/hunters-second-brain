# LRGR Than Life Apparel: Creative and Production Plan (v2)

Prepared by 10x Visuals for Larger Than Life Apparel, 18 October 2026. This revises the client proposal (`LRGR Video Concepts.pdf`) against the original brief in `raw/lrgr/`. It is an internal producer document. The client version is `LRGR Video Concepts v2.pdf`.

> **Constraint (Hunter, 18 Oct):** everything must be filmable in one session. The 30-wear series What Goes First? was replaced by The Lift, and In The Bag replaced it in the six (packed at arrival, unpacked at wrap).
>
> **Status:** the executions for The Drawer Hat and In The Bag have not had a second review pass. Check their participant prompts before the shoot.

## 1. Creative diagnosis

The proposal has the right rules and the wrong slate. “Proof over promises. We film the result, whatever it is.” is the correct spine, and the What it shows / What it doesn't / Keep it fair boxes are more rigorous than most proposals carry. The problem is that the six were ranked by how easy they are to film. As a result they collapse onto one set and one takeaway, and the loose scripts quietly break the proposal's own rules.

### Strongest
- **The Drawer Hat** has the best mechanism in the document. It has a real object, a back-story the viewer wants to hear and an outcome nobody knows. Putting the LRGR hat on the table at 0s gives away the ending.
- **The Tug Count** is the only actual game. It has rules set in advance, an audible click, a score and an unknown result.
- **Mirror Says Yes** has the best turn: approval in the mirror, then the side view. It is built on a photo that can't really be taken “without warning” on a set.
- **The Sample I Sent Back** is a genuine spot-the-difference with a decision behind it, if the sample exists.
- **30 Wears. One Hat.** is the right series, but its Ep. 0 has nothing at stake.
- The four strongest mechanisms (03, 05, 08, 11) all sit outside the recommended six.
- Lines worth keeping: “Alright. Why'd you stop wearing this one?”, “Every time he touches it, I click.” and “Three things I check on any hat. Doesn't matter whose.”

### Repetitive
- Nine of the 12 use a side profile as their mechanism or their payoff.
- Four of the recommended six are on one plain-wall profile set (the proposal specifies “a plain grey or white wall”): Same Head. Same Camera. Two Profiles., Fits Around. Doesn't Sit Down., One Hat. Six Heads. and Who It's Not For. The first two use the same wearer back to back. In a feed that's four near-identical thumbnails, which is exactly the “six variations of the same fit comparison” the brief rules out.
- Fits Around and Same Head are one video split in two.
- “Check the side” is the takeaway of Same Head, Mirror Says Yes, Three Checks Before You Buy and The Side Check Booth. “Depth, not size” runs through Fits Around, Every Part, What It's For and Three Checks.
- Even the concepts meant to be different end on the profile: Every Part (“On the head, side view”) and Brand New vs Mine (“both on the same head in profile”).
- Drop Night and Every Colour, One Take both show product with no question attached. Drop Night announces one colourway and a date. Every Colour runs the range and ends on Shak's head.

### Too scripted
- **Participant verdicts are written in advance, including LRGR wins.** This is the most serious problem in the document. Examples:
  - Same Head: “I'd wear this one. The other one feels like it's sitting on top of my head.”
  - One Hat. Six Heads.: “Sits right. Doesn't feel like it's going to fly off.” and the planted dissent “Bit much on top for me, to be honest.”
  - Who It's Not For: “I like it sitting higher. Always have.”
  - The Side Check Booth: “Yeah, okay. That's better.”
- **Results are written before the test:**
  - “Pull it down, and it rides straight back up.”
  - “Drops a bit. But now it's loose, and it's still sitting high.”
  - “Notch 4 → Notch 6”
  - The integrity box line: “loosening didn't bring that cap down meaningfully”
  - 30 Wears: “Brim's flat and clean. Sweatband's fresh. Strap's never been touched.”
- **Hooks read as if a marketer wrote them:**
  - “This fits his head. It just doesn't sit on it.” (an antithesis built to echo the cover line)
  - “If yours sits like the left one, it's not your size. It's the depth.”
  - “Forget the logo for a sec. This is the bit that matters.”
  - “Curved like this so it frames your face.”
  - “Snapback, so you can actually dial it in.”
  - “Mirror says yes. Now check the side.”
  - “Mirror's lying to you. Check this.”
  - “Honestly? If you like it sitting up top…” (performed candour)
  - “New one. Same fit. See you [day].”
- **Shak is used as a presenter.**
  - He points while the wearer turns 0°/90°/180°.
  - He speaks for the wearer (“He doesn't want that.”) and scripts his own generosity (“And that's fine. Wear that one.”).
  - He reads a feature list.
  - Who It's Not For ends on the pitch it claims to avoid.
  - His real views are parked in a one-hour “Shak's chair” block recorded after the action, so nothing he says can shape what he reacts to.

### Missing
- **Blinding.** Nobody, on camera or watching, is ever unsure which hat is LRGR. Blinding is the cheapest way to get tension and fairness at the same time.
- **Shak committing before he knows.** No concept has him make a guess, a call or a bet he can visibly lose. Being wrong on camera is the most credible personality beat available, and it costs nothing.
- **What annoys him, and what he'd change on his own hat.** The brief names the first. No concept is built on either. 07 buries “If you had to cut one thing” in its off-camera questions.
- **Real-world use.** The two concepts with real-world use (The Tug Count and 30 Wears) are either outside the six or only baselined on the day. On the shoot day, real-world use is 30 optional minutes outside.
- **Range.** There is no fast loop and no 60–90 second conversation. Almost every structure is A, then B, then the takeaway. There is no running score outside The Tug Count, no prediction followed by a result and no cold open.
- **A product film with a reason to exist.** Drop Night is a stock trailer with no idea tied to the brand.

### Claims and assumptions to fix

| Where | Text | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fits Around | “If your hat sits high, it's crown depth, not size. Loosening won't fix it.” | Too absolute. A cap can sit high because it's too small even at its last notch, or because of crown taper, a stiff front panel, head shape or hair. | Test loosening on a real hat and report what it did on that head. |
| Fits Around | “Same snug fit, deeper crown.” | Notches aren't a shared unit across two brands' straps. “Deeper” is unmeasured, and depth gets all the credit. | Set every hat the way the wearer likes it. Any depth figure needs a stated measuring method. |
| Fits Around | “a wearer where the difference actually shows (tested before rolling)” | This is casting for the result, and Same Head then reuses the same wearer. | Recruit with a neutral question. Disclose any pre-screening. |
| One Hat. Six Heads. / Recommendation | “Find the head closest to yours. That's roughly how it'll sit on you.” / “Real data across head sizes.” | Head circumference doesn't establish head shape or fit. Four to six recruited heads aren't a representative sample. “58cm · notch 5 · ‘Sits right’” is invented. | Claim only what was filmed: whether the strap closed on each person. A head measurement is a rough guide to whether it will close, not a guarantee. The tape goes above the brows and ears, the band sits on a different line, and hair and head shape also affect it. |
| The Tug Count | “Makes comfort and fit something you can count and watch.” | It counts fidgeting, not comfort. Two “similar days” aren't the same conditions. | Run it on one day in alternating blocks, labelled informal. |
| 30 Wears / Brand New vs Mine | “Brim's flat and clean.” / “How this hat has actually aged under real use.” | “Flat” contradicts the curved brim in the public product descriptions. One hat with an unverified history can't speak for the model. | Define and log each wear. Compare against a sealed twin. |
| Every Part / Recommendation | “the way a maker would” / “Shak talking like a maker” | Implies LRGR manufactures the hats. Where they are made is unconfirmed. | Describe only decisions Shak can evidence. |
| The Sample I Sent Back | “Everyone reckoned I was being picky.” / “The shape came from specific decisions, not a catalogue.” | Invents history and other people's reactions, and implies a custom pattern. | Use Shak's documented account, or don't make the video. |
| The Big Idea | “From the front almost every hat looks fine.” / “it looks off in the photos their mates take” | The research lists “looks fine from the front but awkward from the side” as one frustration some people raised. “Almost every hat” overgeneralises it, and the mates' photos aren't in the research. Mirror Says Yes rests on the second. | Treat it as a hypothesis and test it on camera. |
| Throughout | “Starts a series people follow”, “Gives every drop a moment people wait for”, “Shak's honesty is the content”, “Shak's eye for fit comes through naturally” | Engagement predictions, and an on-camera personality nobody has confirmed. | Say what the video does, not how people will react. |

### Schedule and access
- **The day doesn't hold.** It is 7.5 hours on paper (8:30 to 4:00). By our estimate, as written it runs to roughly 12–13.5 hours once the rest is counted: load-in and building two sets +1.5h, briefings and releases for four to six participants +0.5h, moving camera and lights between the tabletop and the profile set +0.5h, hat changes, re-marks, overlay checks and the extra takes natural conversation needs +0.5–2h, a lunch longer than the 30-minute gap +0.25h, buffer +0.5h and pack-down +0.75h.
  - One camera body can't stay “locked and never touched” while also shooting the 85mm notch inserts and “a wide that proves the camera didn't move”.
  - One lav can't cover the wearer-led pieces.
  - The kit lists talent but no crew roles. Nobody is assigned to shoot, run sound, ask questions, log notches or manage releases.
  - To fit a day of about ten hours, the proposal's slate needs a second camera and named crew, or fewer concepts.
- **Access is assumed, not confirmed:**
  - an embroidery machine (Stitch by Stitch)
  - a market stall, the organiser's permission and releases from the public (The Side Check Booth)
  - a rejected sample (The Sample I Sent Back)
  - a worn hat with a known history (Brand New vs Mine)
  - real drawer hats (The Drawer Hat)
  - four to six people across a range of head sizes (One Hat. Six Heads.)

## 3. The final concept slate

### How the slate works
- **Two concepts are shape-led:** Red or Blue? (on heads, blind) and Shape First (on stands, no fit claim). The Drawer Hat's loosening check is a conditional beat, not a mechanism. No concept argues crown depth.
- **The comparison cap is bought by the 10x producer, not Shak or anyone from LRGR,** to a rule written down before the shop visit: the curved-brim snapback the shop says is its best seller within about $10 of the LRGR price (confirm the current price and currency first), from a named retailer, with the receipt kept. Shak doesn't see it before the shoot. Red or Blue? Part 2 says the rule on screen, and Part 2 and The Tug Count both state the exact product and the price paid. If the cap isn't named, state its style, its price and how it was chosen. The producer also buys the Ours Or Not? decoys, to their own written spec.
- **Measurements appear only where a concept uses one,** with the method shown.
- **Every hat is set the way its wearer likes it, on camera.**
- **Participants get neutral prompts, never lines.** Shak's opening lines are starting points. He says them his way, or not at all.
- **Blinding hides logos, not shapes.** Anyone who knows the A-frame may still recognise it, so blind wearers and recounters are people who have never seen LRGR hats or LRGR content.
- **Any test LRGR or Shak can lose needs a publishing sign-off before the slate locks.** That covers all six recommended concepts. No sign-off, no test.
- **Small tests are labelled as small.** One person, one day, informal. Any link to the crew or to LRGR is disclosed on screen.

### 1. Red or Blue?
*Built from 02 Same Head. Same Camera. Two Profiles. (made blind), with 11 Mirror Says Yes's turn as its middle. Absorbs the “check the side” takeaway of 12 Three Checks Before You Buy and The Side Check Booth.*

- **Pillar:** Side by Side Comparison
- **Viewer insight or question:** With the logos hidden, which hat would you wear? And does seeing the side change the answer the front gave?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A blind pick with a turn, across two posts.
  - Both hats get identical grey tape over the front panel, plus a red or a blue dot.
  - The viewer and the wearer pick from the front first. Then the locked side and back views land, and the wearer is asked again.
  - Part 1 resolves the wearer's turn and ends on the viewers' vote. Part 2 takes the tape off.
- **Exact first two seconds:** A stacked 9:16 split, front-on: the same face twice, in two caps with matching grey tape over the fronts, a red dot on one tape and a blue dot on the other. On screen: “Red or blue?” No voice.
- **Suggested opening line:** None from Shak. The first words heard are the producer's prompt to the wearer, off camera: “Which one would you actually wear out?”
- **What happens:**
  - The producer tapes both hats and tosses a coin for the order. Shak stays out of the wearer's eyeline for both picks.
  - On a wide shot, the wearer sets each strap the way they like it, which shows nobody misadjusted either hat.
  - **Mirror round:** the wearer tries both hats in a mirror, front only. Prompt: “Which one would you wear out? Why?”
  - **Locked round:** the wearer stands on floor marks with their eyes on a target. Each hat is shot at 0°, 90° and 180°, then the first hat goes back on (A-B-A) to catch any hair or order effect. An onion-skin overlay on the monitor re-matches the head position.
  - The wearer sees the side and back frames on the monitor. The same prompt, word for word: “Which one would you wear out? Why?”
  - **Part 1:** the front split (the viewer picks), the wearer's mirror pick, then the side and back angles landing at about 8–10s as the turn, each held for at least 2s. Then the wearer's second pick and reason (“Mirror: red. Side: ___”). It ends on “Which would you wear?” as a poll or a comment vote.
  - **Tape-off:** filmed after The Tug Count, so the wearer stays blind through both. Shak takes the tape off on the profile set and responds to the pick, unscripted. Prompt: “What do you make of that?”
  - **Part 2:** the tape-off and the brand, Shak's response, the vote result, the A-B-A check, and the comparison cap: the rule it was bought by, the exact product and the price paid (or, if it isn't named, its style, price and how it was chosen). On screen: “One head, two hats. The vote is opinion, not a fit test.”
- **Payoff:** Part 1: whether the side changed the wearer's pick. Part 2: which one was LRGR, and how Shak takes a pick that may not be his. If the wearer and the vote both land on the other hat, that's the post.
- **Product role:** One of two unlabelled options. It earns its result or it doesn't.
- **Why someone would watch:** You pick before the turn and before the brand. The logos are hidden, so the brand doesn't steer the pick, though anyone who already knows the A-frame's shape may still recognise it. It tests the proposal's central idea (“Show the side, not the front”) instead of asserting it, and it might not hold. Part 2 is also where Shak responds to someone whose preference may differ from his.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - The comparison cap, bought by the producer to the written rule, and whether it's named in Part 2.
  - Colours close enough not to give LRGR away.
  - Whether the raised front embroidery shows through the tape. Disclose it if it does.
  - A wearer who has never seen LRGR hats or LRGR content (not Shak, not the LRGR helper).
  - Sign-off to publish if LRGR loses.
  - Whether the account will run a two-part post.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - No suitable wearer: a 10x crew member who hasn't worked on LRGR or handled its hats, disclosed on screen.
    - No two-parter: one video with a “pause and pick” card before the reveal.
    - Colours don't match: grade both halves black and white and say why on screen.
- **Production difficulty:** Medium. It needs a locked set, a continuity sheet and a monitor or tethered laptop with an overlay function. Recommended: a 50–85mm full-frame-equivalent lens from further back, so the front view doesn't exaggerate the brim, with the zoom ring taped.

### 2. Ours Or Not?
*New.*

- **Pillar:** Side by Side Comparison
- **Viewer insight or question:** Could the person behind this hat pick it out without looking? And what is he actually feeling for?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A feel box, with the viewer one step ahead of Shak.
  - The cardboard box has its back cut away towards the camera. Shak's hands come in through two holes on the far side.
  - A hat slides in. The viewer sees it, and an on-screen tag gives them the answer before Shak calls “ours” or “not ours”.
  - A coin toss decides each round and hats can repeat, so he can't count his way to the answer.
  - Six rounds that get harder on a fixed schedule, scored against luck. Six from six by guessing is 1 in 64. Five or better is about 1 in 9.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Front-on: a plain box on a table, its open back facing the lens. Two hands push in through holes on the far side as a cap slides in from the left. A small tag above it reads “LRGR” or “NOT”.
- **Suggested opening line:** “Mix 'em up. I've just got to say if it's one of ours.”
- **What happens:**
  - **Rules card:** every front badge and every inside label goes under an identical square of gaffer tape. Rounds 1–2: the whole hat. Rounds 3–4: the front panel is off limits. Rounds 5–6: strap, sweatband and brim only.
  - **The pool:** two or three LRGR hats and three caps from other makers, bought by the producer to a written spec. They match the A-frame's confirmed features (curved brim, snapback strap), at least two are high-crown or A-frame-style, and one has raised embroidery. Match structure only once LRGR's construction is confirmed. Shak never handles the decoys beforehand.
  - **Loading:** the producer tosses the coin and loads the box. The loading is shown once on a wide shot, so it's clearly not Shak doing it. The order is logged off camera.
  - **Rounds:** Shak talks through what he's checking, and the score updates.
  - **End:** the score next to the luck figure, then the round that fooled him or the detail he leaned on every time.
- **Payoff:** The score against chance, and the one detail his hands relied on, found blind rather than claimed. A miss gets shown.
- **Product role:** It's the thing he's trying to find. What his hands rely on becomes the product point.
- **Why someone would watch:** You know the answer while he doesn't, so every hesitation and wrong call lands. The founder can fail on his own channel, and the fixed schedule means the last rounds are the hardest whether he's winning or not. What he feels for is something you can check by hand in a shop.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - Shak agrees that a miss goes out.
  - LRGR hats on hand. Colour doesn't matter, because it can't be felt.
  - Decoys bought to spec. They aren't named and get no verdict. The only conclusion drawn is whether he could tell.
  - Anything he says about construction is presented as what he feels, and checked before it goes in a caption.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - If Shak won't be tested, the participant plays after the tape-off, having handled one LRGR hat for a minute.
    - If he goes six from six, that's the result, and the schedule means he did it with only the strap, sweatband and brim in the last two rounds.
- **Production difficulty:** Low. One table, the main camera and a phone on the wide.

### 3. The One I Knocked Back
*Built from 08 The Sample I Sent Back, with a participant's honest answer added.*

- **Pillar:** Side by Side Comparison (with a Built For Us decision behind it)
- **Viewer insight or question:** Can you even see the thing he was fussy about? And would a buyer have cared?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A participant spots the difference, then gives an honest answer.
  - Two near-identical hats, with no markings.
  - A participant tries to find what changed, and the viewer looks along with them.
  - Shak shows the difference, with the evidence.
  - Then he asks “Would you have noticed?” and has to respond to whatever comes back.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Close on the participant's hands turning both hats over on a plain surface, with no tape and no markings. Off screen, Shak: “One of these I sent back.”
- **Suggested opening line:** (Shak, off screen) “One of these I sent back.”
- **What happens:**
  - The participant handles both hats, tries them on if that helps, and guesses. Prompt: “See it?”
  - Shak points out the difference, with matching macros of both hats.
  - His account, drawn out by prompts: “What was wrong with it? How'd you spot it? What did you ask for?”
  - Any dated photo, email or spec note goes on screen.
  - “Would you have noticed?” The participant answers. Then Shak responds: why it mattered to him, and whether he'd do it again. Cost or delay comes up only if it's documented.
- **Payoff:** The difference, proof that it happened, and the clash or agreement between his standard and a buyer's answer. A flat “no” stays in, and his response to it is the ending.
- **Product role:** The final hat is the answer to the spot-the-difference.
- **Why someone would watch:** You look for it alongside the participant, then watch a founder defend being fussy to someone who might not care.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - That a real rejected sample or a documented revision exists, and what the change actually was.
  - Documentation for any cost or delay claim.
  - Whether the supplier can be mentioned.
  - Nothing said about where or how the hats are made unless verified.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - Dated photos only: run it from the photos and say so.
    - Nothing real: drop it. Never reconstruct a sample.
- **Production difficulty:** Low, if the sample exists.

### 4. The Tug Count
*Built from 05 The Tug Count, restructured.*

- **Pillar:** The Hat Test
- **Viewer insight or question:** How often do you reach up to fix your cap without noticing? The research describes people who keep pulling their caps down. This counts it for one person instead of claiming it.
- **Core creative mechanism:** A running tally where the viewer knows which hat is which, and the wearer and the official counter don't.
  - Four 15-minute blocks of something the wearer genuinely does, filmed on one locked wide in the order A-B-B-A, so neither hat always gets the later slot. A coin toss decides which hat is A.
  - Both hats stay taped, tagged “1” and “2”.
  - The LRGR helper clicks live, for the sound and the picture. The helper knows which hat is which, so the live count isn't the official one.
  - Afterwards, someone who has never seen LRGR hats, this brief or the hypothesis recounts from the footage. That's the official number.
  - The viewer sees the hats labelled from the first block. There's no brand reveal.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Captured live in block 1 on a second phone locked over the clicker's shoulder: the tally counter sharp in the foreground, the wearer soft behind. A hand goes up to the hat and the thumb clicks, loud in the mix. If block 1 doesn't give a clean moment, open on the locked wide with the click audible and a counter overlay. No re-enacted tugs.
- **Suggested opening line:** (Shak to the wearer, at the start of block 1) “Every time you touch it, that's a click.”
- **What happens:**
  - **Rules card first:** a hand to the hat that moves or re-seats it counts. A scratch that doesn't move it doesn't count, and neither does putting it on. The card also sets the draw margin before filming (for example, within [x] clicks over the hour).
  - Shak gives the wearer the rule, then leaves for the tabletop.
  - **Hats:** the two from Red or Blue?, still taped. The red and blue dots are covered with “1” and “2” tags (uncovered again before the tape-off), and a fresh coin toss sets the order. The wearer sets each one their way and swaps at the same spot every block, with the helper handing over the next hat.
  - **Activity:** whatever the wearer really does that holds one locked frame at the location, out of sight and earshot of the tabletop: a gaming or desk session, say, or hoops if there's a court that holds one frame. It's chosen with them, not for them.
  - Between blocks, one check-in: “Anything you've noticed with this one?”
  - **Edit:** labels on the hats for the viewer from block 1 (“LRGR” and the comparison cap's name, or its style and price), a running tally, then the four block counts with the live count next to the blind recount. On screen: “The live count isn't blind. The blind recount is the official number.” and “Same wearer picked [hat] in Red or Blue? earlier that day.”
  - **Posting:** after Red or Blue? Part 2, which is where the brand is revealed.
- **Payoff:** Four raw numbers against the margin set in advance, whichever way they fall. A difference inside the margin is a draw. On screen: “One person, one hour, informal.”
- **Product role:** LRGR against the comparison cap, in the same hour, under the same rules.
- **Why someone would watch:** It's a scoreboard you can count along with. You know which hat is which, and the wearer and the person who did the official count don't. It also puts the wearer's Red or Blue? pick next to what their hands actually did.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - The wearer (not Shak, with any link to LRGR disclosed) and their activity.
  - A recounter who has never seen LRGR hats, LRGR footage, this brief or the hypothesis. Not a 10x editor who works on the account.
  - The count rule and draw margin, agreed in writing before filming.
  - Sign-off to publish any result.
  - A space at the location that holds one locked frame, out of sight and earshot of the tabletop, with conditions that hold steady across all four blocks.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - A crew member who hasn't worked on LRGR as the wearer, disclosed.
    - An indoor desk or phone session if the space won't hold a frame.
    - If conditions change mid-session, stop and say so rather than compare the blocks.
    - If the wearer's real activity is somewhere else, or there's no second rig or third crew member, it runs in sequence on the main camera in the same session (adds about 1.25 hours; Can't Unsee It and the buffer go first) and its status changes from “finish on shoot day”. Use a wearer who hasn't seen the Red or Blue? reveal, or say on screen that the wearer knew which hat was which.
- **Production difficulty:** Medium. About 75 minutes on a locked camera, plus an hour of recounting in post. It runs on a second camera, or a phone on a tripod, so the main camera keeps shooting.

### 5. In The Bag
*New.*

- **Core creative mechanism:** A before-and-after through one real bag, compared with an overlay wipe.
  - Two new hats (LRGR and a second copy of the comparison cap) are shot on a gridded board from fixed positions.
  - The bag's owner packs both their normal way. Halfway through, they swap the two hats' places.
  - At the end, the hats come out onto the same marks, and the edit wipes the after frame over the before.
  - Ten minutes later, there's one more frame, with the hats untouched.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Overhead: two caps on a gridded cutting mat beside the owner's open bag. The owner's hand picks one up and packs it into their own bag the way they normally do.
- **Suggested opening line:** (to the bag's owner) “Pack them how you normally would.”

  - The packing and the swap are filmed and left undirected.
  - **Unpack:** the hats go straight onto the marks, then the wipe, then the ten-minute frame.

  - Sign-off to publish a bad result.
  - No rehearsal off camera. Testing first and filming only a good run is selection bias.
  - No steaming or washing to fix a dent unless the care instructions are confirmed.
  - On screen: “One bag, one shoot day, one way of packing.” It is not a durability claim.
  - **Fallback:** a crew member's bag, disclosed.
- **Production difficulty:** Low. Twenty minutes of stills at each end; the bag does the rest.

### 6. The Lift
*Replaces What Goes First?, which needed 30 wears over several weeks and can't be filmed in one session. It sits inside The Tug Count's block swaps, so it costs no extra wearer time.*

- **Pillar:** The Hat Test
- **Viewer insight or question:** What does a cap leave behind after you've had it on for a while? A band mark across the forehead, flattened hair, nothing at all? And does it differ between two caps on the same head?
- **Core creative mechanism:** Four lifts, one at the end of each Tug Count block.
  - At each swap the wearer stands on the same floor mark, facing the same locked close-up camera, and lifts the cap straight up.
  - The camera holds on the forehead and hairline for three seconds. The cap then goes face-up on a marked tray for a close-up of its sweatband.
  - The four lifts run in order (A, B, B, A), so the cap worn first is also worn last and the order effect is visible.
  - The viewer knows which cap is LRGR. The wearer doesn't, because they're still blind from Red or Blue?.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Tight on a forehead and the edge of a brim. Two hands lift the cap straight up and out of frame. Nothing is said.
- **Suggested opening line:** None from Shak. The first words are the producer's prompt to the wearer: “How's your head feel?”
- **What happens:**
  - Lift 1 (cap A after 15 minutes): hold on the forehead, then the sweatband on the tray. The wearer answers the prompt in their own words.
  - Lifts 2 and 3 (cap B, then B again): same framing, same hold, same prompt.
  - Lift 4 (cap A again): the last lift. The edit lays all four forehead frames side by side.
  - Shak sees the four frames for the first time after the tape-off and says what he notices, unscripted.
- **Payoff:** Four frames side by side: a mark, flattened hair or nothing, after the same 15 minutes on the same head. Both caps may leave the same mark, or the LRGR one may leave more. Either result stays in.
- **Product role:** One of two caps worn under the same conditions on the same head.
- **Why someone would watch:** Everyone knows the moment you take a cap off and check your hair. It's a two-second reveal repeated four times, with the order flipping halfway.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - The wearer's consent to forehead and hairline close-ups.
  - No hair product, sweat-wiping or mirror check between the lift and the shot. The producer calls the lift before the wearer touches their head.
  - Sign-off to publish if the LRGR cap leaves the bigger mark.
  - On screen: “One person, 15 minutes per cap. Not a comfort test.” Activity, temperature and sweat all vary between blocks.
  - **Fallback:** if The Tug Count runs in sequence instead of in parallel, The Lift still happens at each swap.
- **Production difficulty:** Low. About two minutes per swap on the second rig, inside The Tug Count hour.

### 7. The Drawer Hat
*Built from 03 The Drawer Hat. Absorbs 01's loosening test and 12's checks.*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us
- **Viewer insight or question:** Why does a hat you paid for end up in the drawer? Is it the hat, or the way it's being worn?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A specific call before the evidence, then one fix.
  - The owner puts on a hat they stopped wearing. Shak looks it over and writes one specific guess on a card before they say a word.
  - The card flips, and the owner gives the real reason.
  - Shak gets one fix without swapping the hat: the strap, how it's seated, the angle, or the brim (with permission).
  - The owner decides where it goes: back on their head or back in the drawer.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Mid-shot: the owner sits in their old cap, worn the way they used to wear it. Beside them, Shak writes on a card angled away from them. No LRGR hat on the table.
- **Suggested opening line:** “Don't tell me why yet.”
- **What happens:**
  - The hat arrives as it is. In the lean version it stays in the owner's bag, and nobody talks about their own caps until this video rolls.
  - The owner wears it the way they used to. Shak walks around them, asks before touching anything, and checks it the way he'd check any hat (“Doesn't matter whose”). Close inserts show whatever he's actually looking at.
  - **Guess rule, on screen:** one specific sentence about where it sits, what it does or what it clashes with. A category word like “fit” or “size” counts as a miss.
  - Prompts to the owner: “Alright. Why'd you stop wearing this one? How long have you had it? When did you last wear it?”
  - The card flips: hit or miss.
  - **One fix.** If loosening is the obvious move, he tries it, and the profile set's locked camera and floor marks record what it actually does, before and after.
  - The owner checks in a mirror or on their phone. Prompt: “Would you wear it out now?”
  - An LRGR hat comes out only if the owner asks to try one. It's a try-on, not the fix, and whatever they say about it stays in.
- **Payoff:** Whether Shak called it, and the owner's decision. “Keep your hat, wear it like this” is a valid ending, and maybe the most believable one the brand can post.
- **Product role:** Indirect. What's on show is Shak's eye, not his hat. The A-frame appears only if the owner asks to try it.
- **Why someone would watch:** You guess along with him, the owner's reason might be yours, and he can be wrong in front of them. It's the slate's long conversation, where the talk is the point.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - Owners with a hat they genuinely stopped wearing, whose reason Shak hasn't heard. Start asking a week out. In the lean version the participant brings it, so ask when booking them. Disclose any crew on screen.
  - Releases.
  - Whether other brands' logos can show. If not, frame around them, and never knock the brand.
  - Permission to adjust or reshape the hat, with nothing that damages it.
  - That Shak is happy to commit to a guess on camera, and signs off on publishing a miss, a “keep your hat” ending, or the owner not liking the LRGR hat.
  - Lean version: if the owner asks to try the LRGR hat, say on screen that they wore it earlier in the shoot. Expanded version: use an owner who hasn't worn it.
  - **Fallback:** the daily-hat episode. The participant brings their everyday cap and one thing they don't love about how it sits, and the same guess-then-one-fix mechanism runs. Never stage a drawer hat.
- **Production difficulty:** Low per episode: a table, two chairs, the main camera plus a locked phone on the wide, two lavs, and the Red or Blue? profile set left standing for the before-and-after. Sourcing the hats is the hard part.

### 8. Shak's Bet
*Replaces 04 Who It's Not For. Built on its buried question, “Ever thought about a shallower version?”*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us
- **Viewer insight or question:** Does the person behind this hat know who it's not for? And what does he say when someone wants something different?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A hidden bet, then a live disagreement.
  - Three people, each in their own everyday cap, chat with Shak one at a time.
  - They step out. Shak puts a coin under one of three upturned cups at their seats, for the person he reckons won't want the LRGR hat, and gives his reason to camera in one sentence.
  - They come back. All three try the hat, set it their way and give a verdict. The cup is lifted only after the last verdict.
  - Shak asks whoever's least keen “If we did one for you, what's different?”, then has to say whether he'd do it.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Three people sit down in a row, each in a different cap of their own, each behind an upturned cup. Nobody has spoken yet.
- **Suggested opening line:** “One of you won't want this. I've already picked who.”
- **What happens:**
  - Prompts to each person: “What do you normally wear? How do you like it to sit? What do you hate in a cap?”
  - The bet, made while they're out of the room, with his one-sentence reason.
  - Try-ons in random order, so the person he bet on isn't saved for last. Each person checks however they normally would. No locked profile.
  - Prompt during each try-on: “Would you wear this out? What would you change?”
  - The cup comes up: hit or miss.
  - To the least keen person: “If we did one for you, what's different?” Shak can ask why, push back or concede, then gives his real answer on whether he'd do it. “Maybe” stays “maybe”.
- **Payoff:** Hit or miss, the reasons people actually gave, and a founder answering someone who wants something else. It ends on the limit or the decision, never on a pitch.
- **Product role:** The hat is judged by three people nobody chose for liking it.
- **Why someone would watch:** A founder bets against his own product and might be wrong, then has to deal with someone who doesn't want what he sells.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - Three people with genuinely different cap habits, found with a neutral question (“How do you like a cap to sit?”) and not cast for the result.
  - Shak's real position on other shapes. Talk it through beforehand so he isn't ambushed, but don't rehearse it.
  - No hint of a future product unless it's real.
  - Sign-off to publish any outcome.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - Two participants (a 50/50 bet, which is weaker), or crew members, disclosed.
    - With nobody available, Shak says to camera, with two hats in hand, who the shape doesn't suit. Weaker, but honest.
- **Production difficulty:** Low–medium. Three releases and four voices: two lavs plus a boom recommended.

### 9. Will It Do Up?
*Replaces 09 One Hat. Six Heads.*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us
- **Viewer insight or question:** Will it even do up on my head? And does head size tell you where you'll actually set the strap? (The fit discussions behind the research come from threads about large heads. That's a creative lead, not audience data.)
- **Core creative mechanism:** The tape's call against the person's own choice.
  - **The ladder:** the hat's inside circumference is measured at every notch on camera, with the method shown, and the figures are stacked into a ladder.
  - **The call:** each person's head is measured, and the ladder calls the notch closest to that measurement. The call goes on screen for the viewer. The person doesn't hear it.
  - **The result:** the person sets the strap how they like it. A call counts only if it's the exact notch they choose.
  - **Score:** “The tape called [x] of [n].”
  - **Last notch:** whoever's nearest the end of the range tries the last notch.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Extreme close-up on the back of a head: two thumbs pressing a snapback's last peg towards its hole. On screen: “Last notch.” Cut before it closes. If nobody is near the end of the range: the ladder building one notch at a time, the tape laid round the inside of the sweatband and each figure stacking on screen.
- **Suggested opening line:** “Don't put it on yet. Tape first.”
- **What happens:**
  - **The ladder:** the tape goes round the inside of the sweatband on the same line every time, with each figure rounded to the nearest half centimetre.
  - **Each person:**
    - Their head is measured above the eyebrows and ears, with the method shown the first time.
    - The producer logs the tape's call before the hat goes on.
    - They set the strap until they're happy. Prompt: “How's that compared with what you normally wear?”
    - The call and the result go side by side, and the score updates. On a miss, Shak says what he sees (where it's sitting, how they like it), as his read, not a measurement.
  - **The last-notch attempt.** Nothing is forced. If it doesn't close comfortably, that's the result.
- **Payoff:** “The tape called [x] of [n]”, the range in centimetres, and what the score showed on these heads, whichever way it went. The closing line is written in the edit from what was filmed. The end card lists each person's rounded head size, their notch and their own words, under “Rough guide only. Same size doesn't mean same shape.”
- **Product role:** The hat's real adjustment range, measured on camera instead of stated.
- **Why someone would watch:** You guess along with the tape, you get a real number for a practical question, and there's a moment of suspense at the last notch.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - One consistent way of counting notches.
  - Whether the hats come in one size or several.
  - The measuring method.
  - Releases that cover showing head measurements.
  - Nobody cast as a joke for their head size, and nobody chosen for how the hat looks on them.
  - Claims stop at whether the strap closed on each person filmed. A head measurement is a rough guide to whether it will close, not a guarantee: the tape goes above the brows and ears, the band sits on a different line, and hair and head shape also affect it.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - On a lean shoot, run it as a recurring segment: one or two heads per shoot, with the score carried across episodes. Crew heads are fine if disclosed.
    - If nobody's near the end of the range, drop the last-notch moment and open on the ladder.
- **Production difficulty:** Medium with four to six people. Low as a recurring one- or two-head segment.

### 10. Mark It Up
*Replaces 07 Every Part, What It's For. Its buried question, “If you had to cut one thing, what would it be, and what wouldn't you touch?”, becomes the mechanism.*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us
- **Viewer insight or question:** Would the founder change anything about his own hat? And would someone who's worn it pick the same things?
- **Core creative mechanism:** Three calls each, then a second opinion.
  - Shak pulls a hat from a stock box without looking.
  - He gets three strips of tape in total, in any mix: orange for “I'd change this”, green for “not touching it”. He chooses where they go, so he has to decide what matters. One sentence per strip.
  - Then the wearer marks up the LRGR hat they wore today, with the tape removed, under the same three-strip limit and out of his sight.
  - Both hats go side by side.
- **Exact first two seconds:** Macro: the first strip of tape going onto a hat on a turntable, Shak's thumb pressing it down. No timer, no voice.
- **Suggested opening line:** “Three strips. Orange I'd change, green I'm not touching.”
- **What happens:**
  - A random pull from the stock box, shown on a wide shot so it's clearly not a hand-picked hat.
  - Shak looks the hat over and places his three strips. If he stalls: “What wouldn't you touch? Why?”
  - Each green strip's reason is his own preference or a documented decision, never a claim about what the part does (no “frames your face”, no “dial it in”).
  - Macro and loupe inserts of the three marked spots only.
  - The wearer marks up their hat. Prompt: “You've worn it today. What would you change? What would you leave?”
  - Both hats side by side, top-down and then on the turntable. Prompt to Shak: “What did they mark that you didn't? Agree or not?”
  - If one of his orange strips marks something he's actually changing, he says so, but only if that's a real decision.
- **Payoff:** Two marked-up hats in one frame, three strips each, showing where the founder and a wearer agree and where they don't. How many of his three are orange is the honest verdict.
- **Product role:** The hat itself, at the three spots that matter most to two people who've handled it.
- **Why someone would watch:** A founder marking his own product as flawed is uncommon, three strips force him to choose, the opinion reads in a single frame, and the second opinion gives the video a turn.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - Shak has genuine “would change” answers (check in the pre-interview).
  - The client is comfortable with an orange strip reading as a flaw.
  - No reasons are given about materials or how the hat is made unless they're confirmed.
  - Tape colours that contrast with the colourway.
  - **Fallbacks:**
    - If Shak has no genuine changes, run it wearer-led: the participant marks it up and Shak responds.
    - The wearer-led version still puts orange tape on the hat. If the client isn't comfortable with that, or neither has anything real, In The Bag takes the slot, decided at the pre-interview. “Nothing to change” is a humble-brag, so it never posts.
- **Production difficulty:** Low.

### 11. Can't Unsee It
*New. Built on what annoys Shak. The brief names this, and no proposal concept is built on it.*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us
- **Viewer insight or question:** What's the thing on a cap that the person behind a hat brand can't stop seeing?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A ten-second spot-it loop that only the viewer plays.
  - One held frame of a cap with the logo covered, and two silent seconds to look.
  - Then Shak's thumb lands on the detail, and he says why it bugs him.
  - LRGR's own hats are in the pool, covered like the rest. If an A-frame has one of his pet hates, that episode runs like any other.
- **Exact first two seconds:** A locked close-up of a cap on a plain surface, its logo under plain tape. No hands, no voice. On screen: “Spot it?”
- **Suggested opening line:** None set. After the pause he names the detail his own way.
- **What happens:**
  - Hold, pause, thumb, then one line on why. It ends on his reason.
  - One pet hate per episode, batch-shot four to six at a time on the tabletop.
  - Caps from several makers, bought retail, plus LRGR's own. Logos covered, stickers removed, brands never named. Skip any cap whose shape alone gives the brand away, so it doesn't turn into a dig.
- **Payoff:** The reveal and his reason. You go and check your own cap.
- **Product role:** Shak's eye. The A-frame is one more cap in the rotation, not the answer.
- **Why someone would watch:** A tiny game with the answer inside five seconds, built to loop. It leaves you with something to look for next time you're buying.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - Shak's real pet hates. Pre-interview question: “What's something on a cap that bugs you that most people wouldn't notice?”
  - Caps that genuinely show each one.
  - **Status:** a bonus only, shot if the day allows and only if the pre-interview turns up real pet hates. If he doesn't have them, drop it. Never invent them for him.
- **Production difficulty:** Low. About 30 minutes for a batch on the tabletop setup.

### 12. Shape First
*Replaces Drop Night and Every Colour, One Take.*

- **Pillar:** Streetwear Built For Us (drop and range film)
- **Viewer insight or question:** What's the new one?
- **Core creative mechanism:** A backlit row of identical hat silhouettes on stands. The shape is the constant, and the hero.
  - A front light clicks on one hat at a time, in release order.
  - The last silhouette stays dark a beat longer. That's the new one.
  - Each drop adds a stand, so the film becomes a running record of the range.
  - The hats are on stands, not heads, so the film makes no fit claim.
- **Exact first two seconds:** A white backlit wall, a row of black hat silhouettes on stands, and silence. A switch clicks, and the first hat lights up in colour.
- **Suggested opening line:** None at the open; the switch clicks carry it. Shak's only line comes at the end, in his own words: which one's new, plus his real reason for the colour, if he has one.
- **What happens:**
  - Click-reveals of half a second to a second each, in release order.
  - The last hat is held dark, then lit and held.
  - An end card with drop details appears only if a real date exists. No “Same fit” and no other product line unless it's confirmed.
- **Payoff:** The new colour, revealed in line with everything before it, plus one honest line.
- **Product role:** The whole film.
- **Why someone would watch:** The pattern sets up the wait for the last reveal, and at 8–15 seconds it's made to replay. Instead of a stock trailer, it leads with the shape the public product descriptions emphasise (the deep crown and curved brim) and keeps it constant, so colour is the only thing that changes.
- **What needs confirming:**
  - That every colourway uses the same pattern. If not, identical silhouettes would mislead, so show the real differences.
  - The release order and the range on hand.
  - A real drop date, if one is shown.
  - **Fallback:** if no drop is scheduled, shoot “the range so far” with no new one. Record the stand spacing and light positions on a setup sheet, and add the next hat at the next drop.
- **Production difficulty:** Medium. It needs a backlit set, a flagged front light for each hat (or one hat lit per take and cut together), and a colour-accurate grade.

### Why these six work together
- **One side-profile video, not four.** Red or Blue? carries “check the side” once, done blind, and puts it to the test instead of asserting it. The other five reach the product through touch, behaviour, critique, conversation and time. The Drawer Hat uses the profile set only if loosening turns out to be its one fix.
- **In three of them, Shak commits before he knows the answer:** Ours Or Not?, The Drawer Hat and In The Bag (he calls which cap comes out worse before it's packed). In Mark It Up, he marks his own hat before a wearer marks theirs. In Red or Blue? Part 2, he responds to a pick that may not be his.

  That's how his personality comes through without anyone having to assume what it is: being right, being wrong, and how he takes it.
- **Most of them can go against LRGR or against Shak on camera.**
  - Red or Blue? and The Tug Count can both land on the other hat.
  - Ours Or Not? can beat him.
  - Mark It Up puts his own product's flaws in frame.
  - The Drawer Hat can end on “keep your hat”.
  - In The Bag can dent the LRGR hat and not the other one, and his call can be wrong.

  That is the proposal's own rule (“If the LRGR hat doesn't come out ahead, it stays in”), built into the structures instead of written in a box.
- **Six different first frames, and only two on a tabletop.** Two faces in taped caps, a feel box, a clicker over a shoulder, a tape macro, an owner in their old cap, and two people holding cards beside a box. The two tabletop openings (the feel box and the tape macro) look nothing alike.
- **Six different structures:** a blind pick with a turn across two posts; scored rounds against chance on a fixed escalation; a running tally in alternating blocks with no reveal; a three-strip audit with a second opinion; a guess, a conversation and one fix; and a before-and-after wipe across the whole day. Lengths run from 20 seconds to a 90-second conversation. Two are quick (Ours Or Not?, In The Bag), two build (The Tug Count, Red or Blue?), one is talk (The Drawer Hat) and one is hands-on opinion (Mark It Up).
- **Six different takeaways:** whether the side changes the pick, the detail to check by hand, how often one person fixed each hat, what the founder would change, whether it's the hat at all, and what wears first.
- **They feed each other on the day.**
  - **Hats.** The 10x producer buys the comparison cap and the three Ours Or Not? decoys once, to the written rules, and Shak doesn't see them before the shoot. The comparison cap serves Red or Blue? and The Tug Count. The decoys go in the Ours Or Not? box, which runs at the same time as The Tug Count. Buy a second copy of the comparison cap for In The Bag.     - **Posting order:** Red or Blue? Part 1 → Ours Or Not? → Red or Blue? Part 2 → In The Bag → The Tug Count → Mark It Up → The Drawer Hat. Put a post from outside this shoot (Can't Unsee It if it's made, or the account's usual posts) between The Tug Count, Mark It Up and The Drawer Hat, so this person's videos never run back to back. The Tug Count lands after the reveal.

## 5. Shoot-ready execution

### Red or Blue?

*Status: filming finishes on the shoot day. The picks are filmed first and the tape-off after The Tug Count. Part 2 can't be edited until Part 1's vote closes, because it needs the count. Nothing else has to wait.*

#### A. The idea

Someone who has never seen an LRGR hat picks between two caps with every logo taped over. They pick twice and get the same question both times. The first pick is front on, in a mirror. The second comes after they see locked side and back frames of both hats, stacked on a laptop screen. Part 1 shows both picks and asks the viewer the same question as a vote. Part 2 recaps the picks and the vote, then films Shak hearing the picks for the first time, peeling the tape himself and responding. It also discloses the comparison cap and how it was bought.

The video tests the proposal's line, "Show the side, not the front," on one head instead of stating it. If seeing the side changes nothing, that is the result, and we publish it.

**Viewer takeaway**
- **Part 1:** whether seeing the side and back changed one person's pick, and which hat the viewer would pick.
- **Part 2:** which hat was LRGR, whether the wearer and the viewers agreed, and how the founder takes an answer that may not go his way.

#### B. Opening

**Part 1**
- **Shot:** a stacked 9:16 split made from the locked 0° frames of each hat's first pass.
  - The same face in the same position in both halves, eyes on the target beside the lens. The taped patches and coloured dots are clearly visible.
  - The red-dot hat is always in the top half and the blue-dot hat in the bottom, in every split in both parts.
  - A thin neutral divider separates the halves. Each half has a small red or blue chip in its top corner, so the halves can still be told apart in the side and back frames, where the dots don't show.
- **Action:** none. The frame is still for about 2s.
- **Dialogue:** at about 1s, the producer's prompt from the mirror round, off camera: "Which one would you wear? Why?" These are the exact words on the prompt card, used in both rounds.
- **On screen:** "Red or blue?" centred on the divider from frame one. Burned-in captions for the prompt.

**Part 2**
- **Shot:** close on the two taped hats on the stool. Shak's thumb and finger pinch the corner of the tape on one of them. Cut before the tape lifts and any logo shows. This is live footage from the start of the real peel, not a re-enactment.
- **Dialogue:** room audio only, as recorded.
- **On screen:** "Red or blue? Part 2".

#### D. Essential shot list

**Main camera, locked on the profile set**

Settings (recommendations):
- An 85mm full-frame-equivalent prime, or a zoom taped at 70–85mm, about 2.5–3m from the wearer.
- 4K landscape, which leaves room to crop each half of the stacked split.
- Manual focus, exposure, white balance, shutter and ISO.
- Start around f/5.6. At 90°, check on the monitor at 100% that the brim tip and the back of the crown are both sharp. If not, go to f/8 and add light. Don't move the camera.
- **Framing check before anything is recorded:** after the straps are set, the wearer stands on the 0° marks for about 30s in each hat. This isn't for the cut, and nothing is shown to them.
  - The shooter sets the lens at the wearer's eye line and the headroom for whichever crown is taller, then sets focus.
  - Log the camera height, distance and focus distance. From here on, nothing moves.

1. **0° front, on each pass (A, B, then A again).** Head and shoulders. Feet on the 0° marks, eyes on the target beside the lens, chin level. Hold 5s. Shows the taped front and where the brim sits against the brow.
2. **90° side, on each pass.** The wearer turns to their right on the floor marks, so their left side faces the lens, every pass. Eyes on the side target. Hold 5s. Shows the crown height on the head, where the brim sits, the gap or contact above the ear, and the back of the band.
3. **180° back, on each pass.** Eyes on the target on the wall. Hold 5s. Shows the strap, the closure and how far the back comes down at the nape.
4. **The second pick.** The wearer, hat off, on the 0° marks, looking at the laptop beside the lens and answering. Same locked frame.

**Second rig: a second body or a phone on a tripod**

5. **Taping and coin tosses, before the wearer arrives.** Shot overhead or at 45°, with both hats in frame. The producer calls each toss before flipping:
   - Toss 1: "Heads, LRGR gets the red dot."
   - Toss 2: "Heads, red goes first."
6. **Both taped hats side by side, close.** Shows the matching patches and dots. If the raised embroidery shows through the tape on either hat, this shot is where we disclose it.
7. **Strap setting, wide.** The wearer sets each strap themselves. The producer's hands stay away from the hats.
8. **Mirror round, over the shoulder.** The reflection face on, the swaps, the pick and the reason. Angle the rig so it doesn't appear in the mirror.
9. **Continuous wide of the locked round.** The main camera, tripod, floor marks and wearer in one frame, rolling through all three passes. Shows that the camera never moved and that every hat change happened at the mirror.
10. **Over the shoulder at the laptop.** The side pair, then the back pair, with the wearer's head and shoulder in frame.

**Tape-off, after The Tug Count**

11. **Two-shot.** Shak and the wearer on either side of the stool with both taped hats on it. The laptop with the side split sits on a second stool beside them, screen towards Shak. The profile wall is behind them.
    - Recommended: the main camera on the second tripod, rotated vertical, with a 35–50mm full-frame-equivalent lens.
    - Roll before anyone sits down.
12. **Peel insert, live.** A phone clamped or on a mini tripod at the stool, close enough to read a logo as the tape lifts. Brief Shak beforehand to peel with each hat resting on the stool, brim towards the phone. Each hat only gets one real peel, so both cameras are rolling before he touches the tape.
13. **Comparison cap disclosure, after the tape-off.** The receipt (personal details covered) and the cap's tag or product name, flat on a table. Shot after the tape-off because the tag shows the brand.

**Optional extras**
- A top-down of both taped hats on a neutral board, at matched scale.
- The wearer's hands seating each hat at the mirror.
- A single on Shak's face while the wearer tells the picks, if there's a third operator.
- **After the vote closes:** one phone take of Shak answering "What do you make of the vote?"
  - Only use it if he hasn't been following the count. Ask him first, and if he has been, say so on screen.
  - If used, it goes straight after the vote line at 2–8s, labelled "Recorded [date], after the vote closed".
  - It doesn't change the status above.

#### E. Direction for Shak

- **During the picks:**
  - He stays out of the room and out of earshot, and doesn't see the laptop.
  - Nobody tells him the picks.
  - He doesn't see the comparison cap before the shoot.
  - At The Tug Count briefing, he doesn't handle or name either hat.
- **At the tape-off:**
  - He talks to the wearer, not the lens.
  - He hears the picks with the tape still on and can ask why.
  - He peels the tape himself, then responds.
  - After that, he looks at the side split on the laptop. It's his first look at the frames.
- **Tone:** no performance. He talks to the wearer the way he would to anyone trying on his hats.
  - If the pick isn't his hat, he says what he actually thinks. He can agree, disagree, ask more or concede.
  - No recovery pitch, and he doesn't explain the result with product features the video can't show.
  - He talks about how each hat sat on this person, as filmed. Nothing about the other brand beyond that.
- **Recognising his own hat:** he may recognise it under the tape. If he does, saying so on camera is fine.
- **Starting points.** He says these his own way, or not at all:
  - "Which one'd you go for?"
  - "What was it about that one?"
  - "What'd you see from the side?"
- **Off-camera questions from the producer, after the peel:**
  - "What do you make of that?"
  - "Did you know which one was yours before the tape came off?"
  - (At the laptop) "What do you notice about how each one sits on them?"
  - "Would you have picked the same, on their head?"
  - "Seeing that, is there anything you'd want to change on yours?"
- **If he names a change he'd make:** it stays as his opinion. It is never captioned as a planned product change unless LRGR confirms one.

#### F. Participant prompts

The producer reads these from a card, the same way every time. No reactions to answers: no nods, no "nice", no "interesting".

- **At booking:**
  - Ask "Do you wear caps?"
  - Describe it as a shoot for a cap brand.
  - Agree any fee or thank-you at booking, and make clear it doesn't depend on which hat they pick.
- **On the day, before anything else:**
  - The release names LRGR.
  - Ask them not to look the brand up, and to keep their phone away until the tape-off.
- **On-camera pre-check (for the record, not for the cut):**
  - "Have you ever seen, worn or followed LRGR?"
  - "Do you know anyone involved with the brand or with 10x?"
- **Briefing, said once:** "Two rounds. First the mirror, front on only. Then we film you front, side and back, and show you the side and back on screen. I'll ask the same question both times. Same answer or a different one, both are fine. One of these is the brand's and one isn't. We don't know what you'll pick, and either answer's useful."
- **Straps:** "Set each one how you'd wear it. Take your time."
- **Framing check:** "Stand on the marks for a sec. We're just setting the camera."
- **Mirror round:**
  - "Face the mirror straight on for this round."
  - Then: "Which one would you wear? Why?"
  - The only follow-up is "Anything else?"
- **Locked passes:**
  - "Put it on how you'd wear it."
  - "Feet on the marks, eyes on the dot."
  - "Next mark."
- **While the splits are built:** "Take a break. We'll call you back in about fifteen."
- **At the laptop:**
  - "Which one would you wear? Why?", word for word.
  - The only follow-up is "Anything else?"
- **After both rounds:** "How do you normally like a cap to sit?"
- **Before The Tug Count:** "Keep your picks to yourself till the tape comes off, Shak included."
- **At the tape-off:**
  - If Shak doesn't ask himself: "Tell Shak which one you picked each time, and why."
  - After the peel: "Does knowing which is which change anything for you?"
- **Never ask:**
  - "Which looks better?"
  - "Can you see the difference?"
  - "See how that one sits higher?"
  - "Do you like ours?"
  - For a second take of a pick.

#### G. Payoff and ending

- **Part 1:**
  - The payoff is "Mirror: [colour] · Side and back: ___", one person's pick before and after seeing the side and back.
  - It ends on the vote. That's a real question, not a call to action, because the count goes into Part 2.
- **Part 2:**
  - The payoff is three answers landing together: which hat was LRGR, whether the wearer and the vote agreed, and Shak responding to picks he's hearing for the first time.
  - It ends on a person, never on a product link: either the wearer's answer about whether knowing changes anything, or Shak's last line.
- **If the wearer and the vote both land on the other hat:** that's the ending, with Shak's response.
- **Part 2 caption, plain facts only:** "LRGR A-frame [colourway], $[price]. Comparison: [product], [retailer], $[price paid], bought [date] by 10x Visuals to this rule, written before the shop visit: [rule]. Logos taped; our producer knew which was which. One wearer; the vote is opinion."
- **Once Part 2 is up:** pin a comment on Part 1 pointing to it.

#### I. Honest execution

**What it demonstrates**
- Which of two specific hats one person chose with the logos hidden: first front on in a mirror, then after seeing locked side and back frames.
- How those two hats sat on that one head, each set the way the wearer likes it, in the same light and frame.
- Whether seeing the side and back changed their pick.
- How viewers who saw the same frames voted.
- How Shak responded, live, to picks he hadn't heard.

**What it does not demonstrate**
- How either hat fits or sits on anyone else.
- Comfort, since each hat was worn for minutes.
- Durability.
- That a deeper crown is better.
- That the side matters more than the front for everyone.
- That the vote is representative:
  - It's self-selected: whoever chose to vote on the brand's own posts.
  - It isn't blind for anyone who already knows the A-frame's shape.
  - Viewers voted after seeing the wearer's picks.
- That the comparison cap stands in for "other caps".

**Keeping it fair**
- **The comparison cap:**
  - Bought by the producer, not Shak or anyone from LRGR, to a rule written before the shop visit.
  - The receipt is kept, and the details are disclosed in Part 2 and the caption.
  - Both hats are new and unshaped. Any visible colour difference is disclosed.
- **Blinding:**
  - Identical patches from one template, with all branding covered, inside labels included.
  - Filmed coin tosses decide the dot colours and the hat order.
  - The producer knew which hat was which. The fixed prompts, read from a card with no reactions, limit that influence, and the caption discloses it.
  - Any embroidery bump visible through the tape is disclosed.
  - Until the tape-off, nobody names or points out either hat where the wearer can hear, and the crew say nothing about the frames.
- **The wearer is in charge of fit:**
  - They set the straps and seat every hat themselves.
  - Nobody else touches a hat on their head.
  - Nobody re-seats a hat to match a frame.
  - No one else wears either test hat before the wearer does.
- **Locked capture:**
  - Camera, lens, light and exposure don't change between passes.
  - The overlay matches the head (eye line, ear, jaw, shoulders), never the hat.
  - The A-B-A order catches any hair or order effect.
  - Every hold is the same length.
- **Edit:**
  - Each split uses the frame 2s after the wearer's feet are on the mark and eyes on the target. If that frame has a blink, use the nearest frame without one. A frame is never chosen for which hat looks better.
  - The laptop splits and the published splits use the same frames.
  - Crop, scale and grade values are copied exactly from one half to the other.
  - Red stays on top throughout.
  - No guide lines, zooms or slow motion on either hat.
  - No measurements or strap numbers on screen. Strap holes can't be compared across two brands' straps.
- **Wording:**
  - "Mirror: red · Side and back: blue" is one person changing their preference, not proof.
  - No on-screen copy says deeper, shallower, better or worse.
  - The vote is called a vote. On screen it's "Logos taped", never "blind test".

#### J. Contingency

- **The visible difference is weak:**
  - Don't zoom one half, push in, add guide lines or slow anything down. Keep both halves identical and let the picks carry it.
  - If the wearer says the frames look the same, "Side and back: couldn't split them" is the Part 1 result.
  - Part 2 then rests on the reveal, the vote and Shak's response.
- **The wearer prefers the comparison cap, in either round:**
  - That's the post. Nothing is reshot with another wearer, and the video isn't dropped.
  - Part 2 ends on Shak's response to it. His written sign-off is already in hand.
- **Seeing the side doesn't change the pick** ("Mirror: red · Side and back: red"):
  - Part 1 still pays off, because the viewer was asked "Your pick?" at the side split before the answer landed.
  - Part 2 can say it plainly: "Seeing the side didn't change [Name]'s mind."
- **The wearer can't choose, or says there's no difference:** keep it in. On screen: "Side and back: no preference."
- **The wearer's reason is the colour or the dot, not the fit:**
  - Keep it in, word for word.
  - Part 2 adds: "The colours weren't an exact match."
- **The wearer guesses which hat is LRGR, or the embroidery shows through:**
  - Keep going, and disclose it in Part 2: "[Name] guessed which was ours" or "The raised logo showed through the tape".
  - If they guessed, don't call their pick unbranded or blind anywhere.
- **The A-B-A repeat doesn't match the first pass:**
  - In the edit, overlay the first and third side frames of the hat that went on first at 50% opacity. Compare the shift at the brim tip and at the band above the ear with the difference between the two hats at the same points.
  - If the repeat moved by half or more of the difference between the hats, Part 2 says on screen: "It sat differently second time round. Too close to call from these frames."
  - Set this threshold before the shoot.
- **The vote and the wearer disagree, or the vote is small:**
  - Show both picks and the raw counts, whatever they are.
  - Below the minimum count agreed before posting, the vote becomes a one-line mention in the recap rather than a full card.
- **Shak explains the result with product claims we can't verify, or slips into a pitch:**
  - Those lines aren't used.
  - The producer moves to the next off-camera question. No retake of his reaction.
- **The shop's staff can't name a best seller:** use the fallback written into the rule (closest price). The caption says which rule applied.
- **The colours don't match closely enough on screen:**
  - Grade both halves black and white in both parts, with "Shown in black and white: the colours weren't a close match."
  - The wearer saw the hats in colour. If colour comes up in their reasons, it stays in.
- **No two-parter:** one video of about 50s, with no vote, in this order:
  1. The front split.
  2. The mirror pick.
  3. The side and back splits.
  4. The second pick.
  5. A 2s "Pause and pick" card.
  6. The tape-off with the picks and the peel, with the chips relabelled.
  7. Shak's response.
  8. The comparison card.
  9. The A-B-A strip, with the "One head, two hats" line.
- **No suitable wearer:** a 10x crew member who hasn't worked on LRGR, wasn't present for the taping and hasn't handled the hats. Disclose this on screen.
- **The wearer cancels on the day:**
  - Keep the hats taped and stored together, and keep the key unchanged.
  - Rebook the wearer or a replacement. The status changes to "finished at a later session".
- **No spare LRGR hat for the tape test:** test on a cap with similar fabric, and use the paper layer on both hats.
- **The Tug Count runs in sequence instead of in parallel:**
  - The tape-off still comes after it, so the wearer stays blind. Everything is still filmed in the one session.
- **No overlay monitor:** mark the eye line and the top of the ear from the first pass with fine tape on the monitor screen, and match each later pass to those marks.
- **The session runs over:**
  - Cut the optional extras first, then the buffer.
  - Never cut the A-repeat, the mirror round, the split-building break, or the time the wearer takes to choose.

### Ours Or Not?

*Side by Side Comparison · about 55s (50–60s) · Finish on shoot day · Filmed at the tabletop during The Tug Count hour, with Shak, the 10x producer and the 10x shooter only.*

#### A. The idea

Shak puts his hands into a box he can't see into and calls each cap "ours" or "not ours". Before each round the producer tosses a coin. Heads loads one of three LRGR A-frames. Tails loads one of three caps from other makers, which 10x bought new to a written spec. The camera looks in through the open back of the box. A tag on the box shows the viewer the answer before Shak makes his call.

There are six rounds, and he gets less of the hat each time:
- Rounds 1–2: the whole hat, except a covered patch over the front badge.
- Rounds 3–4: no front panel.
- Rounds 5–6: only the strap, sweatband and brim.

He isn't told any answers until all six are called. Then he guesses his own score before he hears it.

**Viewer takeaway:** the viewer plays along already knowing each answer. They watch whether the founder can pick out his own hat as the clues are taken away. They come away with his score next to what pure guessing would get. They also learn which part of the hat, if any, gave his own away. That part is a difference he could feel. It doesn't mean one cap is better. He can fail, and a fail still posts.

#### B. Opening

- **Shot:** the main camera is locked, front-on to the open back of the box, and framed 9:16.
  - A plain cardboard box lined with matte grey sits in the lower half of the frame. Shak's face and shoulders are above it.
  - There's a cap inside, and Shak's hands are already on it through two curtained holes.
  - A tag card is clipped to the top edge of the box on the camera side, facing the lens. It reads **LRGR** or **NOT**, whichever round 1's coin gave.
- **Action:** the first frame is mid-handling: his fingers are moving over the crown. He doesn't know what the viewer knows. There's no load shot here. The load appears in the wide at 2s.
- **Dialogue:** use his first think-aloud line from round 1, as it comes. It should describe the cap, not judge it. If nothing usable lands in the first two seconds, open on the hand and cardboard sound. Don't give him an opening line.
- **On-screen text:**
  - Top: "Ours or not? Hands only."
  - From about 1s, a small arrow pointing to the physical tag: "The answer. He can't see it."
  - From 2s: a small "ROUND 1/6 · whole hat" at top left, and six empty tally boxes sitting just above the bottom UI zone.
  - The tag is a physical card in the shot. It is never added as a graphic.

#### D. Essential shot list

**Essential**
1. **Main camera, locked, in one continuous take from the rules read to Shak's first look at the pool.**
   - Set it front-on to the open back, with the lens at about the height of the inside of the box and a slight down-tilt. Start about 1.2–1.5m back (a recommendation; set it in the dry run).

   - Recommended lens: 35–50mm full-frame equivalent, at around f/5.6–f/8, so the cap and his face both stay sharp. Check on the monitor.
   - If the body allows, shoot vertical at 4K (on an L-bracket or a rotated cage). That leaves room for up to a 2x crop into the hands for rounds 3–6 at 1080×1920. If it can only shoot horizontal, use its highest resolution and compose inside the centre 9:16 column.
   - Before rolling, check that the camera has no clip-length limit and no overheating cut-off. Have card and battery for at least 40 minutes. Split files are fine as long as there's no gap.

2. **Phone wide, locked, rolling for the same span.**

     - Shak turned away during the load.

3. **The blank log sheet and the rules card,** top-down on the phone, before round 1.

#### E. Direction for Shak

- **What he's doing:** playing a game he can lose, not presenting.
- **Who he's talking to:** the producer, just left of the lens. Not the camera.
- **Tone:** thinking out loud. It's fine to be unsure and fine to change his mind before the call. No performed confidence and no performed doubt.
- **Describe, don't rate.** He talks about what he can feel, such as stiff, soft, an edge, a seam, a shape or the snap. Not whether it's good. How his comments are handled:
  - No rating of any cap, good or bad, goes on screen as text or on the end card.
  - A line running down an LRGR hat stays in.
  - A line running down a cap that turns out to be a decoy is trimmed back to his call. Those caps aren't named and can't answer back.
  - A line praising a cap that turns out to be LRGR stays as his words only. It never becomes a caption.
- **Materials and how the hats are made:** he says what he feels.
  - If he names a material or a process for an LRGR hat, it's cut unless LRGR confirms it in writing before posting.
  - Nothing he says about a decoy's materials goes out.
- **Other brands:** if he names the brand he thinks a decoy is, trim the name and keep the call.
- **If crown depth is what he goes off:** that's fine. It's a difference he could feel, not a ranking, and no caption calls the deeper hat better.
- **His rules.** The producer reads these to him on camera before round 1:
  - Turn the chair away on "Turn away" and face back on "Go".
  - Don't look at the tub or the tag.
  - Thirty seconds a round. The producer says "Ten" with ten seconds left, then "Call it".
  - One call per round. Don't ask whether he got it.
  - What he can touch:
    - Rounds 1–2: anything except the patch over the front badge.
    - Rounds 3–4: nothing on the front panel.
    - Rounds 5–6: only the strap, sweatband and brim.
  - He can open and close the strap. The producer resets it afterwards.
  - The same hat can come back. Recognising one from an earlier round tells him nothing about which pile it came from.
- **After the reveal:** he reacts however he reacts. Nobody is fishing for a particular response.

**Off-camera questions**
- **Before round 1 (optional):** "What do you reckon you'll be feeling for?" This lets the edit check whether he actually used it.
- **During the rounds:** "What's telling you that?" / "What are you feeling for?" / "Ours or not?" (when time is up)
- **After round 6, before any answers:** "How many out of six do you reckon?" / "Which one were you least sure about?"
- **After the reveal:**
  - "Which one got you? What did it feel like?"
  - "What were you actually going off? Was it the same thing every round?"
  - "Did that change once the front was off limits?"
  - "Could anyone feel that, or only someone who knows the hat?"

#### F. Participant prompts

**None in the main version.** It's Shak and the producer only.
- **The producer says only:** the rules read, "Turn away", "Go", "Ten", "Call it", "Logged", the read-out and the questions in E.
- **The shooter says only:** "Void" (see I).

**Fallback, if Shak won't be tested.**
- **Who plays:** one person already on set who:
  - hasn't seen or handled the decoys.
  - has signed a release.
  - ideally doesn't own an LRGR hat.
- **When and how:** at the end of the day, run by the producer exactly as above. Shak stays out of the room until the reveal.

The prompts:
- **Before round 1:** "This one's LRGR. Have a feel of it for a minute." Use the Red or Blue? hat, which stays out of the pool.
- **Each round:** "LRGR or not?" / "What are you going off?"
- **Before the reveal:** "How many do you reckon you got?"
- **After the reveal:** "Which one got you? What did it feel like?"

Nobody hints at what an LRGR hat feels like. The caption then says the player held one LRGR hat for a minute beforehand.

#### G. Payoff and ending

**Payoff:** three numbers in quick order: his guess at his score, his real score, and what guessing gets. The ending then depends on the result:
- **He missed at least one:** end on the round that fooled him, called back from the earlier footage with the tag visible, and his answer to "Which one got you?"
- **Six from six:** end on the detail macro and his answer to "What were you going off?" The end card notes that rounds 5–6 allowed only the strap, sweatband and brim.
- **He scored around three:** end on what he thought he was feeling for, over the pool lineup. "Couldn't tell by hand" is a result.

**No CTA.**

**Caption, factual:** "Every round was decided by a coin toss, and the tag was set before Shak's hands went in. The other three caps were bought new by 10x Visuals, who filmed this, to a written spec ([list which parts of the spec they met]), and they aren't named. Six rounds, one go. It's his own hat, so this tests whether he can tell them apart by hand, not which cap is better."

**Luck card wording.** These odds hold whatever Shak does. A fresh coin toss decides every round, so a blind guess is right half the time:

| Score | On screen |
|---|---|
| 6/6 | Guessing gets 6 from 6 once in 64 goes. |
| 5/6 | Guessing gets 5 or more about 1 in 9 goes. |
| 4/6 | Guessing gets 4 or more about 1 in 3 goes. |
| 3/6 | 3 from 6 is what guessing gets on average. |
| 2/6 | Guessing gets 2 or fewer about 1 in 3 goes. |
| 1/6 | Guessing gets 1 or fewer about 1 in 9 goes. |
| 0/6 | Guessing gets 0 from 6 once in 64 goes. |

#### H. Practical requirements

**People**
- **Shak.**
- **10x producer:**
  - Writes the decoy spec, buys the decoys and preps the pool.
  - Runs the box: the rules read, coin, die, log, loading, tag, stopwatch, prompts and read-out.
  - Wears a lav so the read-out is clean.
- **10x shooter:** runs the main camera, the lavs and the phone wide, and watches the monitor for off-limits touches.
- **The LRGR helper** stays on The Tug Count clicker for the whole hour.

**Products**
- **3 LRGR A-frames,** new and unworn, from regular stock, chosen by the producer, not Shak.
  - If only two are spare, the die maps 1–3 to L1 and 4–6 to L2.
  - Keep them separate from the Red or Blue? / Tug Count hat, the In The Bag hat and the Mark It Up stock box.
  - Any colourway. If the hats come in more than one size, use one size throughout.
- **3 caps from other makers,** bought new and retail by the producer, ideally from three different makers.
  - Keep the receipts.
  - They go straight from the shop into an opaque bag. Shak doesn't see or handle them until the reveal.
- **The decoy spec.** The producer writes and dates it before shopping. Shak signs off the document but never sees the caps.
  - The decoys match the LRGR pool hat on everything the producer can see on it, such as the number of panels, front structure, brim curve, closure, front embroidery and sweatband.
  - Crown depth as close as the shops allow. Measure an LRGR pool hat first, then every decoy the same way. Write the method on the sheet and record all six measurements.
  - Rule out any feature the LRGR hat doesn't have, such as a flat brim, an unstructured crown, a mesh back or a fitted (strapless) closure. Also rule out any cap whose shape alone would identify its brand on camera.
  - Record each price, but don't choose by it and never mention it on screen.
- **Pool prep.** The producer does it at load-in, out of Shak's sight, the same way for every hat:
  - Unpack all six at once. Remove inserts, paper, stickers and hang tags.
  - **Front badge:** cover the front embroidery of every hat with a patch of thick craft foam (about 3mm), cut from one template and gaffed down. Raised embroidery can be felt through gaffer tape alone.
  - **Inside labels:** cover them with gaffer squares cut from one template. Put a square on every hat at every spot where any pool hat has a label, adding dummy squares where a hat has none.
  - **Straps:** set each one as close as its snaps allow to the same inside circumference, measured at the sweatband with a soft tape. Log the snap position and the measurement.
  - Write each hat's ID (L1–L3, N1–N3) in pen on its inside back tape square.
  - Photograph each prepped hat before round 1.

  - Cut two holes about 12cm across on Shak's side, each with a slit black-fabric curtain inside.
  - Line it with matte grey or black card.

#### I. Honest execution

**What it demonstrates:** whether Shak, on this day, under these rules, over six rounds, could tell these three LRGR A-frames from these three caps by touch, and what he said he was feeling for.

**What it doesn't demonstrate:**
- **That LRGR is better made, more comfortable, more durable or a better fit.** Nobody wears a hat in this video.
- **That the part he relied on is a sign of quality, or that other caps don't have it.** Three decoys aren't every other cap.
- **That anyone else could tell.** It's his own hat, so a high score may show familiarity as much as a real difference.
- **Anything about materials or how any of the hats are made.**
- **Anything more than a small test.** Six rounds gives a result, not a finding.

**Keeping it fair**
- **Toss for every round.** Never balance it three and three in advance. The per-round toss is what makes the luck figures hold.
- **The answer is fixed before he touches the hat.**
  - The coin and the log are on the wide, the tag is in the main frame, and nothing is added in post.
  - The log is the record. If the tag and the log disagree, the round is void.
- **Both cameras run one continuous take.** All six rounds appear in the cut, even briefly.
- **No feedback between rounds.**
  - Neither the producer nor the shooter reacts to any call.
  - While Shak handles a hat, the producer watches the stopwatch, not Shak.
  - The coin and die results are never said aloud.
- **The rules are read aloud and on the card before round 1.** The ladder and the 30-second limit don't change mid-game.
- **Every hat is prepped identically by the same person.** When a hat comes out of the box, the producer checks its badge patch, tape squares and strap setting. Anything that moved is reset before the hat goes back in the tub.
- **Voids are shown on screen.**
  - A round is void if he touches an off-limits part, glimpses the hat, or a square lifts or a curtain slips. The shooter is watching the monitor for off-limits touches and says "Void". The round is then re-tossed.
  - If an off-limits touch only turns up in the footage afterwards, the round can't be re-run. It stands, flagged on screen.
- **The decoys are fair and unjudged.** They're bought to a spec written before shopping. They're never named and never given a verdict.
- **One set of six.** There's no second attempt because the first went badly.

**What would make it misleading:**
- A tag added in post, or a round cut from the edit.
- An order balanced in advance.
- Easy decoys, such as flat-brim or unstructured caps.
- A caption that turns what he felt into a product claim, or calls the deeper hat better.
- Calling the score proof.
- A re-run for any reason other than a prep mistake (see J).

#### J. Contingency

- **He can't tell (a score around three).** It posts; the sign-off covers it.
  - End on what he thought he was going off and where it let him down, over the pool lineup.
  - The takeaway becomes that by hand alone, his own hat was hard to pick from these three.
  - No re-run.
- **Well below chance (one or none from six).** That's as unlikely by luck as five or six from six. He may be feeling a real difference and reading it the wrong way round.
  - Ask "What did you think ours would feel like?" and end on his answer.
  - Use the exact odds from G.
- **Six from six.** That's the result.
  - End on the detail.
  - The end card notes that rounds 5–6 allowed only the strap, sweatband and brim, and the caption calls it a small test.
- **The giveaway turns out to be our mistake** (a patch or tape edge, sticker residue, an insert left in, a strap set differently).
  - It only counts if Shak names it as what he went off and the producer finds it on the hats on camera. A low score is never a reason for a re-run.
  - That run doesn't post on its own. Fix the problem on every hat and say so on camera.
  - Run six fresh rounds with fresh tosses, which adds about 15 minutes.
  - Post the re-run, explain why in the edit, and put the first run's score in the caption.
- **The giveaway is a real feature,** such as the snap, the sweatband or the crown depth. That's a legitimate result, even if it's a dull one.
  - Say plainly that his hat is recognisable by that part.
  - Treat it as a difference, not a ranking and not a fit tip.
- **He prefers the feel of a decoy.** It stays in, the cap stays unnamed, and no caption softens it.
- **He names another brand.** Trim the name and keep the call.
- **Shak won't be tested.** Run the fallback in F at the end of the day.
  - Same box, same rules, same odds.
  - The decoys stay hidden from the player until the reveal.
  - It adds about 30 minutes.
- **Decoys can't be bought to spec.** Use caps that match as closely as possible on the confirmed features (curved brim, snapback). The caption lists which parts of the spec they met. Never swap in obviously different caps.
- **The main camera stops mid-game.**
  - Don't load the next round until it's rolling again.
  - The wide covers the gap.
  - Note it on screen: "Main camera restarted after round [n]; the wide kept rolling."
- **The box doesn't read on camera** (the hands block the cap or the inside is too dark). Raise the box LED, widen the back opening, or move hand coverage to the optional inside-top phone. Sort this out in the dry run, not mid-game.
- **The tabletop runs over.** The rounds are never cut or shortened.
  - Drop the optional extras but keep the detail shot.
  - Move Shak's half of Mark It Up later in the day, which adds about half an hour to the schedule.

### The Tug Count
*The Hat Test · Filmed on shoot day, blind recount in post · about 50s · posts after Red or Blue? Part 2*

#### A. The idea
One wearer spends an hour doing something they actually do, split into four 15-minute blocks. They wear the two taped caps from Red or Blue? in A-B-B-A order, and a coin toss on camera decides which cap is A. Each time a hand moves or re-seats the cap, the helper clicks a tally counter. The viewer is told which cap is LRGR from the start. The wearer isn't told, and the official counter doesn't know either: they recount later from muted footage with no idea which cap is which.

The edit plays like a scoreboard. A strip across the top fills one block at a time. A half-time score appears after block 2. The second half runs the same caps the other way round, so the viewer gets a provisional answer in the middle and then sees whether it holds. The video ends on full-time totals, judged against a draw margin Shak wrote and signed before anyone was counted.

**Why someone stops:** a thumb clicking a counter as a hand goes up to a cap is a habit most cap wearers recognise straight away. The question (which cap gets fewer touches?) is set up in about 10 seconds and doesn't resolve until the last card.

**Takeaway:** how often this one person moved or re-seated each of these two caps over two 15-minute blocks each of their usual [activity]. That sits next to the wearer's own guess and the cap they picked in Red or Blue?. The research context mentions people who keep pulling their caps down. This video counts that once, for one person, labelled informal. It is not a verdict on comfort, fit or quality, and on its own it says nothing about crown depth.

#### B. Opening
- **Shot:** Phone B, locked behind the helper's shoulder on the 2x–3x lens. The tally counter is sharp in the foreground in the helper's hand. The wearer is soft about 2m back, partway through the [activity]. This is real session footage at real speed.
- **Action:** The wearer's hand goes up and moves the cap, and the helper's thumb clicks. The moment is chosen by rule: it's the first touch of the session that the blind recount logged, that reads clearly on phone B and that has a real click on it. That will usually fall in block 1, but the label shows whichever block it actually came from.
- **Dialogue:** Nothing for the first two seconds. The click is the only sound. At about 2s, cut to the briefing for Shak giving the rule in his own words. A starting point is "Every time you touch it, that's a click." On the day it was said first; in the edit it comes second. Subtitle what he actually says, not the starting-point line.
- **On-screen text:** "Every click = a hand moved the cap." A small label on the soft cap reads "Block [n]: [LRGR / comparison cap name]".
- **Fallback:** If no logged touch reads cleanly on phone B (no touches, the hand is blocked, or the click is missing or muddy), open on the locked wide at the session's first logged touch. Use the wide's own audio and a tally overlay driven by the recount log. Never re-enact a touch, and never add a click sound or move one to a different moment.

**Edit rules, fixed before editing starts:**
- Lock the cut before the recount comes back. That means the check-ins (in or out), Shak's reaction (in or out) and every card layout. The recount then only adds the numbers, the tally ticks and the real-speed moments chosen by rule.
- Every number on screen comes from the recounter's timestamped log, never from the live clicks.
- Each block gets 4s at the same compression.
- The real-speed moment in each block is that block's first logged touch, shown for 1.5s from the wide. A block with no logged touches gets 1.5s at 07:30, with the tally showing "0".
- Use real click audio only where the helper actually clicked. Never add, copy or move a click.
- Use the first check-in for each cap (after blocks 1 and 2), both or neither, whatever was said. Cut both only for a technical reason, such as the answer being inaudible. Unprompted comments during blocks stay out of the edit and go into the log.
- No music anywhere. Both caps' labels use the same colour and weight throughout.

#### E. Direction for Shak
- **What he does:**
  1. **Before the wearer arrives (about 5 min):** he sets the draw margin with the producer and signs the card on camera. This is his decision on screen: he commits to the terms before anyone knows the count. Any number applies to both caps equally, so it can't favour either.
  2. **The briefing (about 5 min):** he gives the wearer the rule, answers their questions, and leaves for the tabletop.
  3. **Optional:** his first look at the result.
- **Who he's speaking to:** the producer during the signing, and the wearer during the briefing. Never the lens.
- **Tone (direction, not a persona):** plain and quick, like running someone through the rules of a game. No build-up about what it might show.
- **What he doesn't do:**
  - Say which cap is his, or look at, touch or hand over either cap.
  - Predict a result, say "ours", or react to any adjustment the wearer makes.
  - Come back to the space during the hour, ask the helper how it's going, or hear the live count.
  - Overrule the card. The card is the rule. If what he says differs from it, the producer clears it up on camera before block 1.
- **Starting-point lines (in his own words, or none):**
  - "Every time you touch it, that's a click."
  - "Scratch your head, doesn't count. If the hat moves, it counts."
  - "Just do what you'd normally do."
- **Off-camera questions at the signing (from the producer):**
  - "How far apart would they need to be before you'd say one's actually ahead?"
  - "Why that number?"

  If he has no basis for a number, the producer offers the recommended default and he can take it or change it. The default is: it only counts if one cap has at least 3 fewer touches **and** at least a quarter fewer than the higher total. That's a rule of thumb, not a statistical test.
- **Off-camera questions at the briefing (from the producer):**
  - "Can you run them through what counts?"
  - "What if they just scratch their head?"
  - "What if it comes off?"

  These get the edge cases said naturally. Check his answers against the card.
- **Optional reaction:** decide before the recount whether to film it. If you film it, it goes in the same slot (after the full-time card) whichever verdict lands.
  - Prompts: "What do you make of that?" and "Anything there you didn't expect?"
  - Never ask "Happy with that?" or "Why do you reckon yours did better?"
  - It only runs under his sign-off to publish any outcome, which is given before the slate locks.

#### F. Participant prompts
These are for the wearer only. The helper reads every on-camera question from the card, word for word. Any opinion the wearer gives is theirs. None is written for them.

- **At booking or pre-interview (off camera):**
  - "What do you usually do for an hour or so with a cap on?"
  - "Could you do that in one spot for about an hour and a quarter?"
  - "Do you wear anything else on your head while you do it, like headphones?"
  - "Have you seen or worn anything from LRGR before?"
- **At the briefing:**
  - "Set it how you'd normally wear it. Say 'done' when it's right."
  - "Do what you'd normally do. If you want to fix it, fix it."
  - "Any questions about what counts?"
- **After every block (same words every time):** "Anything you've noticed with this one?" One follow-up is allowed: "Like what?"
- **After block 4:**
  - "Which one do you reckon you touched more, if either?" Then: "What makes you say that?"
  - "During the hour, did you have a guess which one was LRGR?"
- **If they ask which cap is which:** "Can't say till after."
- **If they want a cap off mid-block:** "What's up with it?"
- **Never:**
  - Ask which cap is more comfortable or sits better.
  - Say which cap is which, or mention the comparison cap or their earlier pick.
  - Tell them a count.
  - Laugh, nod or comment when they touch the cap.

#### G. Payoff and ending
- **Payoff:** the full-time totals against the margin and the halves rule, both signed before filming. Next to them sit the wearer's guess and their Red or Blue? pick. The count, the guess and the pick might agree, or they might point different ways. Either way, that's the ending.
- **Verdict cards, written and laid out before the recount comes in:**
  - "Fewer touches: LRGR, by [n]"
  - "Fewer touches: [comparison cap], by [n]"
  - "Draw: inside the margin"
  - "Draw: the halves split"

  All four share the same layout, the same disclosures and the same hold time. The edit drops in whichever one happened. Never use "wins", "comfier" or "stays put".
- **Ending:** hold the card, then "One person, one hour, informal." Go out on room tone. No call to action, no question to camera, no music sting.
- **Which pick goes in the closing line:** use the wearer's side-view pick from Red or Blue?. If their mirror pick and side pick differed, state both: "picked [hat] in the mirror, [hat] from the side".
- **Caption template:** "One person, one hour, informal. [First name] did [activity] on [date] in four 15-minute blocks, swapping two caps in A-B-B-A order (a coin toss decided which went first). Both caps had all branding covered the same way and were tagged 1 and 2. Asked afterwards, [first name] [had no guess / guessed right / guessed wrong] about which was LRGR. Counted: any hand to the cap that moved or re-seated it [full rules]; [n] of these were re-seats after something else knocked it. A cap is only called if it's [margin] lower over the hour and lower in both halves, which was set and signed before filming. Otherwise it's a draw. Official count: a recount from muted footage by someone with no LRGR connection who didn't know which cap was which, with [n] unsure moments left out. Live clicker: [role, e.g. LRGR helper]. Comparison cap: [name or style], $[price] paid [at retailer, if named], bought to [the buying rule used for Red or Blue?]. Both caps were worn with tape on them."
- **Posting:** after Red or Blue? Part 2. Put a post from outside this shoot between this one and Mark It Up.

#### I. Honest execution
**What it demonstrates:** how many times one wearer moved or re-seated each of two specific caps over two 15-minute blocks each of one activity on one day. The count was made from footage by someone who didn't know which cap was which, and judged against rules signed before filming.

**What it does not demonstrate:**
- Comfort or fit.
- How either cap behaves on other heads, with other hair, in other activities or in other weather.
- Why any touch happened (habit, sweat, headphones, boredom or the clicking itself).
- Anything about other caps from LRGR or from the comparison brand.
- Anything about crown depth. The two caps differ in more ways than one.
- How either cap behaves as sold. Both were worn with tape on them, which may change how they sit.
- That fewer touches means a better cap for the viewer. A draw means nothing either way.

**Keeping it fair:**
- **Same conditions:** the same wearer, activity, seat, light and day.
- **What A-B-B-A does and doesn't balance:** it evens out a steady drift over the hour, such as warming up, settling in or getting bored. It doesn't balance a start-and-end effect, because cap A gets both the first block and the last. That's why the coin decides which cap is A, and why the halves are judged separately.
- **Every block starts the same way:** cap off, cap on at the mark, set the wearer's way, "done", then the timer. This applies between blocks 2 and 3 too, when it's the same cap.
- **The wearer doesn't know which cap is LRGR:** all branding is covered the same way, and the tagging happens out of their sight. This makes it harder for politeness to the brand to steer them, though it doesn't rule it out. They may still recognise their Red or Blue? pick by shape or feel, which is why the closing line states that pick and the caption reports their answer to the LRGR-guess question.
- **Rules are fixed before filming:** the rules, the margin and the halves rule are written, dated and signed before filming, shown on screen, and never changed once any count is known. The edge cases on the card are:
  - **Counts as one touch:** pulling it down, pushing it up or back, turning it, lifting and re-seating it, adjusting the brim, adjusting the strap while it's worn, or taking it off and putting it back on while carrying on with the activity. Two hands at once is one touch. Continuous fiddling is one touch until the hand leaves the cap. If the cap stays off for more than 60s while they carry on, log the time off and report it on screen.
  - **Doesn't count:** a touch or scratch that doesn't move the cap, the setting before "done", or anything after 15:00.
  - **Knocked by something else and re-seated:** counts, is logged as "knocked", and the total is reported in the caption.
  - **Unsure moments:** logged with a timestamp, left out of the official number, and their total reported in the caption.
  - **Halves rule:** a cap is only called as having fewer touches if it's lower in both halves (block 1 against block 2, and block 3 against block 4) and the hour total clears the margin. A tie in either half is a draw.
  - **Void conditions:** the session is void if the light changes, the rig stops or is bumped, the hands leave frame, or a break runs over two minutes. A void is called from the log and the footage, before anyone sees the recount.
- **What the recounter gets:** four muted clips from the locked wide (plus the side angle, if it's in the pack). Each is trimmed to exactly 15:00 from the timer start, renamed neutrally (K, M, R, T) and given in shuffled order. They also get the rule sheet and a log sheet with a timestamp column and an "unsure" column. The clips are muted because the live clicks are audible on the wide.
- **What the recounter doesn't get:** phone B footage, any audio, the live counts, which tag is which, the fact that one cap is LRGR, this plan, or any LRGR content.
- **What the recounter is told, in full:** "Count every time the person's hand moves or re-seats the cap, using this sheet. Log the time of each. Mark anything you're not sure about." Pausing and rewinding are allowed. A 10x editor can trim and rename the clips, because that's mechanical and doesn't touch the count.
- **The live count stays visible:** the live clicker can probably tell the caps apart, so their count is shown small and labelled "not blind". Any gap between it and the recount stays in.
- **Disclosures:** who the live clicker is, any link between the wearer and LRGR or the crew, whether the wearer guessed the brand, and the comparison cap's identity, price paid and buying rule.
- **One take only:** no rehearsal session and no re-runs, unless the session is void under the card's conditions. In that case the void session is disclosed in the caption. Testing first and only filming a good run is selection bias.
- **Same treatment whichever way it falls:** equal screen time, real-speed moments and check-ins chosen by rule, the cut locked before the numbers arrive, and the same card and caption whatever the result.
- **Being watched changes behaviour:** being watched and hearing the clicks will probably change how often anyone touches a cap. That applies to both caps, so it doesn't favour either one. But these numbers aren't "how often people normally touch their caps".
- **Words:** the edit and caption say "touches that moved the cap" and "fewer touches". Never "comfier", "fits better", "stays put" or "proof".

### Mark It Up

#### A. The idea
Shak pulls one of his own hats out of a stock box without looking. He gets three strips of tape: orange for "I'd change this", green for "I'd leave this". Any mix is allowed, but three is the limit, so he has to choose what matters most on his own product. He puts each strip on, then says one sentence about why.

Later, with Shak out of the room, the participant marks up the LRGR hat they wore through Red or Blue? and The Tug Count, under the same rules. The viewer has already seen Shak's three strips and the wearer hasn't, so the viewer can spot matches before either of them can. At the end, one cover lifts off both hats at once, and Shak and the wearer see each other's strips for the first time on camera.

**Viewer takeaway:**
- What the founder would and wouldn't change on his own hat. The tape shows it, rather than him claiming it.
- How many of his three strips were orange.
- Whether someone who has worn the hat marked the same spots.

The video puts two opinions in one frame. It does not test the hat.

**Status:** finish on shoot day. Any "I'm changing that" line gets checked before posting (see H). Runs about 55–60s.

#### B. Opening
- **Shot:** a close-up of the hat on a manual turntable. Recommended camera setup: main camera with a 90–105mm full-frame-equivalent macro, about a metre away, mounted vertically for 9:16, at its highest resolution so the edit can punch in to a macro-tight frame on the strip. Use the live take of the first strip Shak actually places, whatever its colour and wherever it goes.
- **Action:** Shak's fingers bring a strip into frame and lay it on the hat. His thumb presses it flat and holds for a beat.
- **Dialogue:** none for the first two seconds. Only natural sound: the tape and the press. At about 2s the cut goes to the wide shot, and Shak states the rule in his own words. A starting point: "Three strips. Orange, I'd change it. Green, I'd leave it."
- **On-screen text:**
  - From the first frame: a small ID line, "Shak, LRGR founder". It explains whose hand it is and whose hat it is. The scroll-stop is a founder taping up his own product.
  - From about 2s, a legend at the top of frame in the real tape colours. It stays to the end: "ORANGE = I'd change it · GREEN = I'd leave it".
  - Over the wide of the pull: "Pulled blind. Crew packed the box."
- **Why it works before the explanation:** a hand pressing bright tape onto a new hat is an odd sight, and the ID line says it's the founder doing it. The colour means nothing until the legend arrives at 2s, so the opening works whichever colour goes on first. Never peel a strip off and lay it again to get a "better" first colour.

#### D. Essential shot list
Lenses, rigs and sizes are recommendations. Adjust them to the confirmed kit. Inserts show the marked spots only, with no beauty passes of unmarked parts.

**Essential**
1. **Wide (phone A).** Locked on a tripod at seated eye height, about 45° to Shak's seat, on the main lens (roughly 24–28mm equivalent), vertical. The frame holds the turntable, his hands, his face and the open stock box at the table edge. It rolls without stopping through his whole half:
   - his head turned away during the pull
   - the hat set at the 0° mark
   - all three placements
   - the hat going into the hiding box

   Tape the tripod legs, because the wearer's half uses the same position. For the reveal, widen only if two people don't fit, and log the new mark.
2. **Box contents (phone B, handheld by the producer).** Straight down into the open box from about 60cm, for 3s, right before the pull, with every hat visible. Phone A keeps rolling, so the box on the wide is visibly the same box.
3. **Live placement close (main camera).** 90–105mm macro at about a metre, vertical, highest resolution, so the hat fills most of the frame. It rolls from before strip 1 until after strip 3, and the shooter follows focus as Shak turns the hat. Strip 1 from this take is the cold open.

#### E. Direction for Shak
- **What he's doing:** deciding, not presenting. He's looking over a hat he didn't choose and picking the three things on it that matter most to him.
- **Who he's speaking to:** the producer, sitting just off the main camera, not the lens. At the reveal, the wearer beside him as well. Thinking out loud is fine.
- **Tone:** however he naturally talks while looking over a hat across a table. If he's quiet and deliberate, let it be quiet. No selling and no wrap-up line. There's no clock: he takes as long as he needs, and the edit compresses the wait.
- **The rules, read from a card before rolling.** The wearer hears the same card, word for word: "Three strips, any mix of orange and green. Orange is something you'd change. Green is something you'd leave as it is. All one colour is fine. Strip on first, then tell us why."
- **Brief him once. This is not a rehearsal.**
  - One sentence per strip is enough. If he says more, the edit will cut it.
  - On green strips, say why you'd leave it or what you decided, not what the part does. Leave out materials, how it's made, and anything about it being "better", unless it's confirmed.
  - If he moves a strip because he's changed his mind, that stays in. What we won't do is redo a strip for a better shot.
  - Only mention a change he's making if it's real. No dates, no hints at a future product.
  - He won't see the wearer's hat until the cover lifts, and once it lifts nobody touches a strip. He shouldn't tell the wearer what he marked. At the tape-off and over lunch, he shouldn't talk them through what he likes or would change about the hat.
- **Suggested line, a starting point only:** "Three strips. Orange, I'd change it. Green, I'd leave it." He says it his way, or the legend carries it.
- **Off-camera questions (from the producer):**
  - "What's the first thing you look at?"
  - If he stalls, ask whichever question he hasn't covered yet, so the prompts don't steer the mix: "What wouldn't you touch? Why?" or "What would you change if you could? Why?"
  - For each strip, only if he hasn't said: "Why that one?"
  - At the reveal: "What did they mark that you didn't? Agree or not?"
  - If he agrees with one of their orange strips: "Would you change it, or leave it?"
  - If he says he's changing something: "Is that decided, or a maybe?" A "maybe" stays a "maybe".

#### F. Participant prompts
The producer gives these, with Shak out of the room and out of earshot until the cover lifts.
- The rule card, read word for word as it was read to Shak.
- "There's no right mix. Whatever you put down goes in."
- "You've worn it today. What would you change? What would you leave?"
- "Do you want to put it on again before you decide?"
- For each strip, only if they haven't said: "Why that one?"
- If they can't find a third: "Take your time." If there genuinely isn't one, the strip stays on the card and the video shows that. The same applies to Shak.
- At the reveal: "Anything he marked you'd see differently?"
- Never ask "What did you like about it?" or "Was it comfortable?" Never name a part, and never suggest a colour.

#### G. Payoff and ending
The payoff is the cover lifting: two hats side by side, three strips each, showing where the founder and someone who has worn the hat agree and where they don't. Because the viewer has already seen Shak's strips, the reveal belongs to the two people at the table. Their first look and Shak's response are the new information. The honest number is how many of Shak's three strips are orange, stated plainly in the tally.

The video ends on the top-down hold with the tally. The last sound is the tail of the last real line, or room tone. No end card, no product line, no question to camera. If someone will answer comments, the post caption can carry one plain question, for example "What would your three be on your own cap?" If Shak places zero orange strips, see J.

#### I. Honest execution
- **What it demonstrates:**
  - Which three things Shak would change or keep on a hat he didn't choose, under a three-strip limit, and his stated reasons.
  - What one person who wore the hat on and off for part of one shoot day would change or keep.
  - Where those two views overlap.
- **What it does not demonstrate:**
  - That an orange spot is a defect, or that unmarked parts are fine. Three strips force a choice, so plenty goes unmarked either way.
  - Quality, durability, comfort or fit, for these two people or anyone else.
  - How the hat would fit or sit on anyone else. The wearer's head is not a guide to yours. Nothing here says a deeper crown is better or worse.
  - What buyers in general think. The wearer's view comes from one day's wear, not ownership.
  - That anything will change in a future batch, unless Shak confirms a real decision.
  - **A like-for-like comparison of one hat.** Shak marks a new hat from stock, and the wearer marks one they've worn. If a strip is about something specific to that hat (for example a loose thread or a mark), the caption says "this one", not the model.
- **Keeping it fair:**
  - **A real blind pull:**
    - The box contents are shown.
    - The producer filled the box, not Shak.
    - The pull is on the wide, with Shak's head turned away.
  - **Same conditions:** the same rule card, read word for word to both. The same tape, limit, lens, distance and light for both halves.
  - **Independence:**
    - Shak marks first, while the wearer is in The Tug Count space.
    - His hat is photographed top-down and shut in a box before the wearer's half.
    - He's out of the room while they mark.
    - Neither sees the other's strips until the cover lifts.
    - There's no talk between them about the hat before then.
  - **No redos for a better answer:** strips stay where they're placed. If someone moves one on their own before the reveal, the move is in the cut. The top-down taken before boxing proves Shak's strips didn't move.
  - **Neutral prompts:** the prompts for when someone stalls are balanced. Nobody names a part or suggests a colour for anyone.
  - **Edit:**
    - Every strip placed is shown, each with its reason.
    - The tally gives the real counts.
    - No music cue over orange strips, and no softening line added after them.
    - Captions use the speaker's words, cut but not reworded, with their name on them. They add no part names the speaker didn't use.
    - A reason about fit, comfort or crown depth stays that person's opinion. It's never turned into a label or on-screen claim.
    - A green reason that's a claim about what the part does or what it's made of is not captioned as fact.
  - **Damage from the shoot:**
    - Check the front panel after the tape-off.
    - If the blinding tape or the Tug Count tags left a mark and the wearer marks it, the caption says the mark came from our tape.
    - A mark the shoot caused never reads as a product flaw.
  - **Context:**
    - Show how long the wearer actually wore the hat, from the log.
    - Decide before filming whether their Red or Blue? pick gets mentioned: mention it whichever way they picked, or not at all.

#### J. Contingency
- **If the visible difference is weak:**
  - **A strip marks something you can't see** (how something feels, its stiffness, how the strap sits): film the action that shows it, such as flexing the brim or working the strap. Show the action, don't add a claim. If it still doesn't read on camera, the spoken reason carries that beat over the macro.
  - **The tape doesn't read on camera:** switch both halves to the backup colour pair before rolling, and update the legend. The tape test the day before should catch this.
  - **The inset doesn't read in the edit:** drop it. The reveal top-down and the tally still show the overlap.
  - **The two markups barely differ:** the agreement is the result. Cut the wearer's half to about 9s and give the reveal and responses the time. The top-down frame shows the match.
  - **They're completely different:** that's the turn. Give Shak's response the time.
- **If the participant prefers another hat:**
  - They still mark the LRGR hat they wore, because that's the concept. Three orange strips post as they are, and Shak responds to them.
  - If their reasons mention the comparison cap, keep their words. Add no text naming or judging it.
  - Don't swap in the comparison cap. It's never marked or judged in this video.
  - If they genuinely can't find a third, the unused strip stays on the card and is shown. Nobody is pushed to find a green or an orange.
- **If the expected result doesn't happen:**
  - **Shak puts three green strips on the day:**
    - It posts only if the wearer placed at least one orange and Shak's answer to it stays in. The video is then about that disagreement.
    - If both go all green, it doesn't post. A founder and a connected participant marking nothing to change gives viewers nothing to use, and it reads as praise.
    - Don't reshoot for orange.
    - In The Bag covers the slot if it was a go at the pre-interview. If not, the shoot carries five and the producer flags it the same day.
  - **The wearer goes all green with thin reasons:** don't push for orange and don't prompt again beyond "Why that one?". It posts under the rules above, with their link to LRGR or the crew on screen.
  - **Shak puts three orange strips:** it posts, under LRGR's sign-off. No softening line in the edit.
  - **Shak's "I'm changing that" can't be backed up afterwards:** cut the line, and keep the strip and its reason.
  - **A green reason comes out as a claim** (what the part does or what it's made of): ask once, "Why's that one green for you?" If the only usable sentence is still a claim, run the strip with no reason caption, or confirm the fact with LRGR before posting.
- **Production:**
  - **The live strip 1 is unusable for the cold open:** open on its macro re-press, same strip, same spot.
  - **A strip comes loose in the hiding box:** lift the cover anyway. Add one line on screen, "This strip came loose in the box", and show where it was with the earlier top-down. Don't stick it back on.
  - **Independence breaks** (Shak hears or sees the wearer's strips before the lift): keep filming the reveal, but say so on screen. If the response is clearly shaped by it, cut from the lift straight to the tally.
  - **The tabletop runs over:** Shak's half moves to after the tape-off, with the wearer out of earshot (about 30 minutes extra).
  - **The tape won't hold, or it snags on a part:** use removable paper labels in the same two colours. No pins, and nothing that marks the hat.
- **Decided at the pre-interview, not on the day:**
  - **Shak has nothing genuine to change:** run it wearer-led. The opener becomes the wearer's first strip and Shak's half drops. At the lift, he responds strip by strip, agreeing or not.
  - **LRGR isn't comfortable with orange tape, or neither person has anything real to mark:** In The Bag takes the slot, using the second copy of the comparison cap bought in advance.

### The Drawer Hat

#### A. The idea
Someone brings a cap they bought and then stopped wearing. With the camera already rolling, they take it out of their own bag and put it on the way they used to wear it. Before they say anything, Shak looks it over and writes one specific guess about why it ended up in the drawer. The owner gives their real reason first and then turns the card over. Shak gets one fix on their hat (the strap, how it's seated, the angle, or the brim with permission). He never swaps it for another hat. The owner then decides where it goes: back on their head or back in the bag.

**Viewer takeaway:** a reason a hat stops getting worn that the viewer may recognise, and an answer to whether it was the hat or the way it was worn. What's on show is Shak's eye, not his hat. LRGR appears only if the owner asks to try one. "Keep your hat, wear it like this" is a complete result.

**Length:** 75–90s. It's the slate's long conversation, and the reason and the flip both need room. It can run to about 100s if the owner asks to try an LRGR hat, because that stays in.

#### B. Opening
- **Shot:** Main camera, locked mid two-shot at the corner of the table. The owner sits in their old cap, worn the way they used to wear it. Shak sits beside them, writing on a card tilted away from them. The table holds only the card and the marker. No LRGR hat is anywhere in frame, background included.
- **Action:** The pen is moving. The owner glances across and can't see what he's writing. The camera can't read the card either.
- **Dialogue:** Nothing for the first two seconds. The only sound is the pen on the card, picked up by Shak's lav. The suggested first line is Shak's, said to the owner whenever they start to explain: "Don't tell me why yet." It's a starting point. He says it his own way, or not at all.
- **On-screen text:**
  - 0–2s: "Guessing why they stopped wearing it."
  - Lean version, as a lower third from about 3s: "[Name] has been on set with us all day. Shak hasn't seen this hat or heard why."
- **Cold-open rule:** This is the real writing moment, a few minutes into the take. It is never restaged. If the main camera misses it, open on the locked wide of the same moment. When the edit catches up to the writing, it carries on from the next frame, so no shot plays twice.

#### E. Direction for Shak
- **What he's doing:** Looking at someone else's cap the way he'd look at any cap ("Doesn't matter whose"). He commits to one specific guess in writing, makes one fix, then accepts the owner's call.
- **Who he's speaking to:** The owner only. The viewer is overhearing. No summary to the lens, no wrap-up line.
- **Tone:** Whatever his natural register is. We're not asking for a persona, and nobody has confirmed what his on-camera style is. The aim is a conversation with someone about their own hat.
- **His rules (agree them at the pre-interview, don't rehearse them):**
  - Ask before touching.
  - Don't narrate the guess during the look-over, because that gives it away.
  - One guess, one sentence, specific. Once the card is face down it isn't touched.
  - One fix. If he thinks it can't be fixed, he says so rather than forcing one.
  - The fix is done to their hat. Don't mention LRGR.
  - If the owner asks to try an LRGR hat before deciding on their own, he holds it until after the decision, e.g. "Yeah, after you've decided on yours."
  - No knocking the other brand and no naming it. Anything he says about the cap is his read of how it's sitting, never a claim about its materials or how it was made.
  - If he's wrong, react however he reacts. No retakes of the flip.
  - Let silences sit. Don't fill the gap after "Would you wear it out now?"
- **Starting-point lines** (his words, or none): "Don't tell me why yet." / "Mind if I have a look?" / "Can I try one thing?" / "Would you wear it out now?"
- **Pre-interview questions (a week out, not about this hat):**
  - "When you look at someone else's cap, what do you notice first?"
  - "Is there anything you won't do to someone else's hat?"
  - "Are you happy to be wrong on camera, and for that to go out?"
- **Off-camera questions (the producer asks after the decision, with the owner off set):**
  - "What made you write that?"
  - "What did you see first?"
  - "When they told you why, what went through your head?"
  - "Why that fix and not another one?"
  - "Did it do what you thought it would?"
  - "What would you tell them to check next time they buy a cap, any brand?"

  Use these only as short voiceover where they explain something already on screen. Never use them to add a claim.

#### G. Payoff and ending
There are two payoffs. The first comes mid-video: whether Shak called it, in the owner's own word. The second is the ending: the owner's decision, shown as an action, with the cap staying on as they walk out of frame or going back in the bag.
- No call to action, no "link in bio", no closing line from Shak to camera.
- A "keep your hat, wear it like this" ending is the post as it stands, and nothing gets added to steer it towards LRGR.
- If a try-on happened, the video ends on their verdict and then a one-second wide of where their own cap ended up.
- **Caption:** plain description, e.g. "[Name] brought a cap they'd stopped wearing. Shak got one guess and one fix." Don't generalise from one person.
- "I paid for it and it just lives in the drawer" never appears on screen or in a caption as something a customer said.

| The owner prefers their own cap, or their everyday cap, to the LRGR hat or to the fixed cap | Keep it, every word. | "Keep your hat" or "I'd rather wear my other one" is a valid ending, and maybe the most believable. |
| The owner can't say why they stopped | Follow-ups: "What did you wear instead?" "When did you last reach for it?" "Not sure" stands as their answer, and the owner still judges the card. | The conversation is the content, and the fix and the decision still land. |
| The reason leaked before rolling, or Shak saw the cap | Drop the card. On screen: "Shak had already heard why." Run the reason, the fix and the decision. If the participant has an everyday cap Shak hasn't discussed, run the daily-hat episode with the guess instead. | It loses the guess but keeps the fix and the decision, disclosed. |
| No drawer hat turns up | The daily-hat episode: their everyday cap and one thing they don't love about how it sits, with the same guess and one fix. On screen: "Their everyday cap." | Same mechanism, nothing staged. |
| No cap at all | Don't stage one. Use the hour for Can't Unsee It (if the pre-interview turned up real pet hates) or for pickups. Book a real owner for a short later session. | No video is better than a fake drawer hat. |
| The owner won't let anyone touch the cap, or won't allow brim shaping | Shak talks them through the fix and they do it with their own hands, within whatever they allow. | Their cap, their hands. The fix is still his call. |
| The day runs late | Protect at least 40 minutes. Drop pickups before cutting the take. If less than 40 minutes remain, switch to the daily-hat episode, which runs in about 30 minutes, in the same session. | A rushed conversation is the one thing this concept can't survive. |

### In The Bag

#### A. The idea
Two new caps, the LRGR hat and a second copy of the comparison cap, are shot on a gridded board when the participant arrives. Before anything is packed, Shak writes down which one he reckons comes out worse, and the card is turned face down. The participant packs both into their own everyday bag the way they normally would. The bag stays with them for the whole shoot. At lunch they swap the two caps' places in the bag, filmed on a phone. At wrap the caps come straight out onto the same marks, and the edit wipes each after frame over its before. Ten minutes later there's one more frame with the caps untouched, to show whether anything springs back. Then Shak's card is turned over.

**Viewer takeaway:** whether either cap came out of one real bag after one shoot day looking different, whether it recovered, and whether the founder called it. It's one bag on one day, not a durability claim.

**Length:** 20–30s.

#### B. Opening
- **Shot:** Overhead, locked. A gridded cutting mat with two caps on taped marks, the owner's open bag beside them.
- **Action:** The owner's hand picks up one cap and packs it into the bag the way they normally do. Cut before the second one goes in.
- **Dialogue:** None in the first two seconds. Then the producer, off camera: “Pack them how you normally would.”
- **On-screen text:** “Same bag. All day. Which one comes out worse?”

#### C. Beat-by-beat plan

| Approximate time | Visual/action | Dialogue or talking prompt | Edit/audio note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Overhead: the hand packs the first cap | None | Cold open on the action. Room tone only. |
| 2–6s | Both caps on the grid, top-down, then front-on | None | Label: “Before · [time]”. Hold each frame 1.5s. |
| 6–9s | Insert: Shak writes his call on a card, turns it face down | Producer: “Which one comes out worse?” | Don't show the card. |
| 9–13s | The owner packs both, their way, undirected | Owner, in their own words, if they say anything | Speed-ramp the packing; keep the real order. |
| 13–15s | Phone clip: the lunch swap inside the bag | None | Label: “Swapped places at lunch.” |
| 15–19s | Wrap: the caps come straight out onto the marks | Producer: “Straight onto the marks.” | Label: “After · [time]”. |
| 19–25s | Overlay wipe, after over before, one cap at a time | None | Same crop and grade as the before frames. |
| 25–28s | The ten-minute frame, untouched | None | Label: “10 minutes later.” |
| 28–30s | Shak's card flips | Shak's reaction, unscripted | End on the card and his first line. |

#### D. Essential shot list
**Essential**
- Overhead locked frame of the gridded mat with both caps on taped marks, before and after (recommended: 35mm on a boom arm or overhead rig, height noted on the setup sheet).
- Front-on locked frame of both caps at mat level, before and after, same height and distance.
- The owner packing both caps, wide enough to show the bag and the way it's packed.
- Insert of Shak writing the card and turning it face down. The card's text is never in frame until the flip.
- Phone clip of the lunch swap inside the bag.
- The unpack straight onto the marks, with no reshaping.
- The ten-minute frame from both positions.
- The card flip and Shak's reaction.

**Optional**
- Macro of any crease or dent.
- The owner pushing a dent out by hand, filmed as a separate step and only if they'd normally do it.

#### E. Direction for Shak
Shak isn't in the packing or the unpack. His job is the call at the start and his honest reaction at the end. He talks to the producer, not the lens.
- **Off-camera questions:**
  - “Which one do you reckon comes out worse? Write it down.”
  - (At the flip) “What do you make of that?”
  - “Is that something you'd ever change on the hat?”
- **Starting point for his line, only if he wants it:** “I reckon that one cops it.” Said his way or not at all.

#### F. Participant prompts
- “Pack them how you normally would.”
- (At lunch) “Swap their places in the bag.”
- (At the unpack) “Straight onto the marks. Don't fix anything.”
- “What do you notice?”

Never prompt for a verdict on either cap. Don't tell the owner which cap is LRGR.

#### G. Payoff and ending
The wipe answers the question in a second, the ten-minute frame shows whether it recovered, and Shak's card says whether he called it. End on his first reaction to the card. No call to action.

#### H. Practical requirements
- **People:** the participant (their own everyday bag), Shak for the call and the flip, the producer for prompts and the setup sheet, the shooter.
- **Products:** one new LRGR hat that won't be sold afterwards, and a second copy of the comparison cap bought by the producer to the same written rule.
- **Props:** gridded cutting mat, tape for the marks, an A6 card and marker, a grey card in the before and after frames.
- **Location:** a corner of the tabletop space that can stay set all day without being bumped.
- **Releases:** the participant's release covers their bag appearing on camera.

#### I. Honest execution
- **Demonstrates:** what one shoot day in one real bag, packed one person's way, did to these two caps, and whether it recovered after ten minutes.
- **Does not demonstrate:** durability, how either cap holds up over weeks, or what any other bag or packing style would do.
- **Keeping it fair:**
  - Both caps go into the same bag, packed by the owner without direction.
  - They swap places halfway so neither spends the whole day on the bottom.
  - No rehearsal. Testing first and filming only a good run is selection bias.
  - No reshaping before the after frames, and no steaming or washing unless LRGR's care instructions are confirmed.
  - On screen: “One bag, one shoot day, one way of packing.”

#### J. Contingency
- **No visible change on either cap:** that's the result. Post it as “Nothing happened”, with the wipe and the card flip. It's shorter, and still a real answer.
- **The LRGR hat comes out worse:** it stays in, as agreed in the sign-off.
- **The owner forgets the lunch swap:** say so on screen.
- **The owner's bag isn't suitable or they didn't bring one:** use a crew member's everyday bag, disclosed on screen.
- **No second comparison cap:** swap this slot for Can't Unsee It, only if the pre-interview turned up real pet hates.


## 6. Realistic batch-shoot plan

**Everything is filmed in one session.** Nothing needs a second day or a follow-up shoot. **Assumptions:** none of this is confirmed. Lenses, kit, crew and locations below are recommendations.
- **Crew:**
  - 10x shooter (Hunter).
  - A 10x producer.
  - An LRGR helper.
- **Spaces:** one location with three areas.
  - The locked profile set, which stays standing all day.
  - A tabletop.
  - A Tug Count activity space far enough from the tabletop that the wearer can't hear Shak.
- **Times:** start times assume an 07:30 load-in. Shift the whole schedule if the venue opens later.

### Rules for the day
- **Lock once, never touch.** On the profile set, lock camera height, distance, focal length, aperture, ISO, white balance and light positions before the participant arrives. Write them on the continuity sheet and photograph the set. Every Red or Blue? frame, the Part 2 tape-off and any Drawer Hat before-and-after use those exact settings.
- **Blind stays blind.** The participant never sees an untaped LRGR hat, the box tags or Shak's tabletop work until the tape-off. The producer keeps the key on paper in a pocket, not on the clipboard.
- **Product changes are logged.** Every swap gets the hat ID, tag, strap setting and timecode on the log sheet. Strap settings are set by the wearer and photographed, never by crew.
- **Takes:**
  - Never re-take a reaction or a verdict. If audio or focus fails on a pick, a call or a reveal, keep the take and cover it from the second camera.
  - Repeat only setup lines, inserts and Shak's explanations, and allow 2–3 passes of each.
- **Usable time:** plan for about 60% of each block to be usable footage. The rest goes to setting up the people, resets and conversations that warm up before they get good.

## 7. Client confirmation checklist

### Essential before locking the concepts
1. **Shak signs off that results go out as filmed** in all six: a pick or count that goes against LRGR, a miss in Ours Or Not?, orange tape on his own hat, a dent in the LRGR hat in In The Bag, and a "keep your hat" ending in The Drawer Hat. If he declines one, that slot gets re-planned (section 4) before anything is booked.
2. **Which hat model is the hero for this shoot,** and that six to eight units can be set aside: one for Red or Blue? and The Tug Count, two or three for the Ours Or Not? pool, a stock box for Mark It Up, and one for In The Bag that won't be sold afterwards.
3. **That everything happens in one session.** No follow-up shoots or series. Only Red or Blue? posts in two parts, both filmed on the day.
4. **Whether Shak has genuine "I'd change this" answers** on his own hat, checked in a short pre-interview. If not, Mark It Up becomes wearer-led or swaps for In The Bag.
5. **How Shak prefers to work on camera:** answering someone off camera, talking straight to the lens, or a mix. This changes how the producer runs his prompts.

### Essential before filming
1. **The participant:** someone who wears caps, has never seen LRGR hats or content, is booked through the tape-off, and has any link to LRGR or the crew disclosed. Their real everyday activity for The Tug Count, and whether they own a cap they stopped wearing.
2. **The participant brings their own everyday bag** for In The Bag.
3. **Location:** three areas, with the Tug Count space out of earshot of the tabletop.
4. **Crew:** an LRGR helper for the full day, and a 10x producer.
5. **Care instructions** for the hero hat, so nobody steams or reshapes a dent in a way LRGR wouldn't recommend.
6. **Signed releases** for every participant, including on-screen measurements (Will It Do Up?) and their own caps (The Drawer Hat).
7. **A blind recounter** for The Tug Count with no link to LRGR or 10x's work on the account.

### Helpful but optional
- Whether a real development sample, rejected version or documented revision exists. That unlocks The One I Knocked Back.
- Upcoming drops and whether every colourway shares the same pattern. That unlocks Shape First.
- Shak's real pet hates about how people wear caps, for Can't Unsee It.
