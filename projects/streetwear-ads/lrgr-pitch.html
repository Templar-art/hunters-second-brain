The big idea

Show the side, not the front

Most hat content is filmed from the front, and from the front almost every hat looks fine. The problems people actually describe show up from the side: the hat fits around the head but sits up high, they keep pulling it down, and it looks off in photos their mates take.

So the camera goes where the problem is. A locked-off side profile, the same head, the same light, and the viewer judges the difference for themselves. Shak is the person who shows you what to look for in a hat. He doesn't have to sell it. He points at what he notices, says what bugs him, and is upfront about who the shape isn't for.

Everything here runs on one rule: proof over promises. We film the result, whatever it is.

Lean baseline kit (to confirm)

One camera, a 35mm (or 24–70mm) and an 85mm lens, a tripod, two soft LED panels, a plain grey or off-white wall, a lav mic, a stool with tape marks for 0°/90°/180°, a soft measuring tape and a tally counter. Shak plus one helper, and four to six people with different head sizes.

Before we roll

What we need from you

  • How you're most comfortable on camera: talking straight to the lens, or answering someone off camera
  • Which LRGR hats and colourways go in, and in what sizes
  • The comparison caps, bought from a shop and named on screen, so it's fair
  • 4–6 people with a range of head sizes, happy to sign a release
  • Materials and care instructions, so we only say what's true
  • Whether a real development sample or rejected version exists
  • Whether you've got a genuinely worn LRGR hat (and how long you've had it)
  • Where the hats are made, if it ever comes up
  • Location for the day
  • Who wears the 30-wear hat and keeps the log
Results are filmed, not scriptedIf the LRGR hat doesn't come out ahead in a comparison, we keep it in. That's what makes the rest believable.
Fair comparisonsSame camera, same light, same lens. Every hat is adjusted to how the wearer likes it, and nobody's hat is set up to look bad.
Preference ≠ proof"I like it" and "it measures this" are labelled separately. An informal test is called an informal test.
No claims we can't backNo waterproof, sweat-proof or wash claims, and no "built different", until it's verified.
