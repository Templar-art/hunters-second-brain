# LRGR Than Life Apparel: Short-Form Creative Plan

Client: Larger Than Life Apparel (LRGR Than Life). Face: Shak (founder). Hero product: deep-fitting A-frame hats.
Prepared by 10x Visuals (Hunter) from the creative brief dated 18 October 2026 ([raw/lrgr/LRGR_Claude_Creative_Brief.pdf](../../raw/lrgr/LRGR_Claude_Creative_Brief.pdf)).

## The strongest opportunity

Most hat content shows the hat from the front. The problem buyers describe happens in **side profile**: the hat fits around the head but sits high, so the wearer keeps tugging it down. The front view hides that. A locked-off camera that shows the same head from front, side and back makes crown depth easy to see without any claims. Shak doesn't need to sell. His job is to point at the gap between the brim and the ear, say what he'd change, and say when a deep crown isn't the answer. Every concept below is built so that the viewer can see the result for themselves.

**Lean baseline setup (assumption, confirm with Shak):** one pro camera body, a 35mm or 24–70mm lens and an 85mm, a tripod, 2 soft LED panels, a plain mid-grey or off-white wall, a lav mic, a stool, a rotating stool or tape marks on the floor, a head-height reference mark on the wall, and a soft measuring tape. One indoor location (studio or a clean room) plus one ordinary outdoor spot. Wearers: Shak plus 3–5 friends or customers with different head sizes, all of whom agree to be filmed.

## Deliverable 1: Twelve concepts

Pillars: **HT** = The Hat Test, **BFU** = Streetwear Built For Us, **SxS** = Side by Side Comparison.

### 1. Fits around. Doesn't fit down. (SxS)
- **Frustration:** "It fits, so why does it look wrong?"
- **Core idea:** On one wearer, a shallow-crown cap is fitted correctly, then loosened, then compared in profile with the LRGR A-frame, so it's clear that adjusting changes the circumference but not the depth.
- **Opening (0–2s):** Tight side profile of a cap sitting high, with a visible gap above the ear.
- **Line:** "This fits his head. It just doesn't sit on it."
- **What happens:** The wearer puts on the shallow cap and adjusts it until it's snug. We cut to a fixed side view. The snap is loosened two notches: the hat drops slightly and gets loose. Then the LRGR hat goes on at a snug setting, and we see the same side view again.
- **Conclusion:** Loosening the strap doesn't fix a shallow crown. Crown depth and circumference are separate measurements.
- **Why it suits:** Turns the core fit insight into something you can see, with no hype.
- **Confirm:** The comparison cap (exact model) and a wearer with a head shape where the difference shows. Shoot it before deciding whether it makes the cut.

### 2. Bring the hat you never wear. (BFU)
- **Frustration:** "I bought it and it just sits in the drawer."
- **Core idea:** A real person brings a hat they stopped wearing, explains why, and then tries on an LRGR hat, with the result left open.
- **Opening:** Hands drop a used cap onto a table next to an LRGR box or hat.
- **Line:** "Why'd you stop wearing this one?"
- **What happens:** Shak and the guest talk across a table. The guest puts on their own hat and Shak points out what he notices. The guest tries an LRGR hat and we film front and side. The guest gives an honest verdict, including if they prefer their own.
- **Conclusion:** The reason the hat goes unworn is specific and recognisable, and whether an alternative fixes it is shown, not claimed.
- **Why it suits:** Starts with a genuine frustration, and Shak's personality comes out in what he notices.
- **Confirm:** Real guests with a real unworn hat. No casting of the outcome. Consent and a release.

### 3. Same head. Same camera. Two profiles. (SxS)
- **Question:** "What does a deeper crown actually look like on a person?"
- **Core idea:** A fixed tripod and tape marks capture front, side and back views of one wearer in two hats, shown as a split screen.
- **Opening:** A split screen of the same face, left vs right, both side profiles.
- **Line:** "Same head, same camera, same spot. Just the hat changes."
- **What happens:** The wearer sits on a marked stool and rotates 0°/90°/180° in hat A, then repeats in hat B with the camera, light and lens untouched. Both hats are adjusted to the wearer's preferred fit, not deliberately loose.
- **Conclusion:** A visible difference in how high the hat sits and in its silhouette. The viewer decides which they prefer.
- **Why it suits:** Proof over promises, in its purest form. It's also reusable as a format.
- **Confirm:** The exact comparison product (disclose brand or style), and the model and size of the LRGR hat.

### 4. When I wouldn't recommend ours. (BFU)
- **Question:** "Is a deep A-frame right for me?"
- **Core idea:** Shak names the head shapes and style preferences that the LRGR shape may not suit, and shows one on camera.
- **Opening:** Shak takes the LRGR hat off a wearer and hands them a lower-profile cap.
- **Line:** "Honestly, if you like it sitting up top, ours probably isn't it."
- **What happens:** One wearer who prefers a shallow or low-volume fit tries the LRGR hat. Shak points out where it sits differently from what they want. They switch to a shallower cap, and Shak explains who the LRGR shape is for.
- **Conclusion:** A clear guide to who should buy and who shouldn't.
- **Why it suits:** Honest limits build credibility, and it shows how Shak handles a preference that isn't his.
- **Confirm:** Shak's actual opinion, and a willing wearer with that preference.

### 5. The adjustment count. (HT)
- **Question:** "How often do you actually fix your hat?"
- **Core idea:** Count how often a wearer deliberately repositions each of two hats over the same ordinary 30-minute activity.
- **Opening:** A tally counter clicks as a hand tugs a brim down.
- **Line:** "Every time he touches it, I click."
- **What happens:** Two sessions of the same activity on similar days (a walk, coffee, a drive), one per hat. A second person clicks a counter on every deliberate adjustment, filmed with a wide fixed camera. End on the tally.
- **Conclusion:** An informal comparison on one person, labelled as such.
- **Why it suits:** Makes comfort and fit something you can watch.
- **Confirm:** The activity, the wearer and the criteria for what counts (defined before filming). Capture the result as it falls, even a draw.

### 6. Same hat. Thirty wears later. (HT, recurring)
- **Question:** "What does this look like after I actually wear it?"
- **Core idea:** One LRGR hat, logged on every real wear, inspected at wear 1, 10, 20 and 30.
- **Opening:** A fresh hat on a turntable, with an on-screen "WEAR 0 / 30".
- **Line:** "Day one. I'm wearing this one and nothing else for thirty wears."
- **What happens:** A baseline inspection: brim curve, crown shape, embroidery, sweatband, strap, plus macro shots. Then a phone selfie on each wear day, with a dated log. A repeat inspection at each checkpoint with the same framing.
- **Conclusion:** Real wear evidence, whatever it shows.
- **Why it suits:** Builds a series with continuity, and the brand can stand behind the outcome.
- **Confirm:** Who wears it, and a wear log (date, activity, conditions). No washing unless the care instructions are confirmed.

### 7. What are you getting for fifty bucks? (BFU)
- **Question:** "Why pay this much for a cap?"
- **Core idea:** Shak takes apart, detail by detail, what each part of the hat does for fit, look or use.
- **Opening:** A macro of the inside of the crown, with a thumb pressing the structure.
- **Line:** "Forget the logo for a sec. This is the bit that matters."
- **What happens:** Close-ups of the crown depth (measured with a tape from the brim to the top), the brim curve, the snapback, the 3D embroidery and the sweatband. Each detail gets one sentence on why it's there.
- **Conclusion:** A specific breakdown of what the money buys, with no luxury language.
- **Why it suits:** It's about value, not prestige, and it doesn't attack competitors.
- **Confirm:** Current price and currency, materials, and whether the measurements are accurate.

### 8. The detail I was being annoying about. (BFU)
- **Question:** "Who actually decided this?"
- **Core idea:** Shak tells the story of one real decision made during development, using the sample or reference behind it.
- **Opening:** Two near-identical samples side by side, one marked with tape or a pen.
- **Line:** "Sent this back. Everyone thought I was being picky."
- **What happens:** Shak shows the rejected sample against the final one, points at the difference, explains why it mattered and what it cost (time or money, if Shak's happy to say).
- **Conclusion:** The product shape came from specific choices.
- **Why it suits:** Personality through standards, with a concrete story rather than a founder's journey.
- **Confirm:** Only viable if a real sample, revision or reference exists. Skip it if not.

### 9. One hat. Different heads. No pretending. (SxS / reference)
- **Question:** "Will it look like that on me?"
- **Core idea:** The same LRGR model on 4–6 people, logging head circumference, strap setting and an honest preference from each.
- **Opening:** A grid of 4 side profiles in the same hat.
- **Line:** "Same hat, five heads. Not everyone liked it."
- **What happens:** Each wearer is measured and puts the hat on. We film fixed front and side views and log the strap notch. Each gives a one-line verdict, mixed opinions included.
- **Conclusion:** A fit reference across head sizes that buyers can compare with themselves.
- **Why it suits:** An honest fit guide, not a reaction montage.
- **Confirm:** Wearers covering a range of sizes, and consent to use their measurements on screen.

### 10. New versus my own worn one. (SxS)
- **Question:** "Will it hold its shape?"
- **Core idea:** A new LRGR hat next to Shak's genuinely worn one of the same model.
- **Opening:** Two hats on a table, one crisp and one obviously used.
- **Line:** "This one's new. This one I've worn for [X months]."
- **What happens:** Side-by-side macros of the brim curve, crown shape, sweatband, embroidery and strap wear. Then both on the same head in profile.
- **Conclusion:** How this particular hat has aged under one owner's use.
- **Why it suits:** Real ownership evidence, using an asset Shak already has.
- **Confirm:** That the worn hat is genuinely the same model, and its real age and use. No staged wear.

### 11. Front looks fine. Turn around. (HT)
- **Frustration:** "Looks fine in the mirror, then I see a side photo."
- **Core idea:** Recreate the mirror-versus-candid gap. A wearer approves a hat from the front, then sees an unposed side photo taken by a friend.
- **Opening:** A front-on mirror shot of the wearer nodding at their reflection.
- **Line:** "Mirror says yes. Now check the side."
- **What happens:** A mirror shot of hat A, then a hard cut to a side photo from a friend's phone. Repeat with the LRGR hat. Both are shown at the same angle.
- **Conclusion:** Check fit from the side before buying.
- **Why it suits:** A buying tip first, product second.
- **Confirm:** The comparison hat and a willing wearer.

### 12. How to tell if a hat's too shallow. (BFU, tip)
- **Question:** "How do I know before I buy?"
- **Core idea:** Shak's 3-check routine for any hat (brim line versus eyebrow, gap above the ear, pull-down test), shown on hats of different depths.
- **Opening:** A finger traces the line from brim to ear on a side profile.
- **Line:** "Three things I check on any hat. Doesn't matter whose."
- **What happens:** Each check is demonstrated on a shallow cap and an LRGR hat. The video ends with "neither is wrong, depends how you like it".
- **Conclusion:** A practical checklist they can use in-store.
- **Why it suits:** Positions Shak as the person who shows you what to look for.
- **Confirm:** That Shak actually uses these checks. Rewrite them in Shak's own words.

## Deliverable 3: Shoot-ready execution plans

### Plan 1: Same Head. Same Camera. Two Profiles.

**A. Summary:** One wearer, one locked camera, two hats, three angles. The viewer sees how each hat sits from the side and back, and decides for themselves. Takeaway: judge a hat from the side, not just the front.

**B. Opening:** Split screen, left and right side profiles of the same wearer. Line (Shak, VO or on camera): "Same head, same camera, same spot. Only thing that changes is the hat." On-screen text: "Same head. Two hats." with small labels "Hat A: [brand/style]" and "LRGR A-Frame".

**C. Beats (~30–40s)**
| Time | See | Hear | Establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | Split side profiles | Opening line | The comparison |
| 2–7s | Wide: the tripod and tape marks, the wearer sitting | "Tripod hasn't moved. He's adjusted both how he likes." | Fairness |
| 7–17s | Front view A vs B, then side A vs B | Shak notes the brim line and the gap above the ear | The difference |
| 17–25s | Back view A vs B | Notes on the strap and how far down the back sits | Full picture |
| 25–32s | Wearer's honest pick | Wearer: preference + reason | Personal preference, kept separate |
| 32–38s | Shak | "Which side looks like how you want it to sit?" | Real question |

**D. Shots**
- Essential: locked 35mm medium close-up at head height, wearer at 0°, 90° and 180° on tape marks, in both hats. A wide of the setup (proves the camera stayed put). Both hats being adjusted on camera to the wearer's preference.
- Optional: an 85mm macro of each crown off-head, and a top-down of both hats side by side.

**E. Shak's prompts:** "What's the first thing you notice from the side?" / "Where's each one sitting relative to his ears?" / "Would you wear the other one? When?" / "Who'd prefer hat A?"

**F. Production:** One wearer (Shak or a friend), one LRGR A-frame, one comparison cap bought retail, a rotating stool or tape marks, a grey wall, 2 LEDs. Lighting and white balance stay fixed for the whole sequence.

**G. Edit:** A vertical split (top and bottom in 9:16) or left and right panels. Keep scale identical, so no cropping one more than the other. A thin guide line at the eyebrow height is optional, and the same on both. Calm pacing, with each angle held for 2s or more. Continuity is critical: same exposure and same grade on both halves.

**H. Ending:** The audience question "Which one sits how you like it?", which is genuine because both are valid.

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* how two specific hats sit on one specific head. *Doesn't show:* comfort, durability, or a result for every head. *Risks:* misadjusting the comparison hat, mismatched lens or crop, picking a comparison chosen to look bad. Disclose the comparison hat's style. If the wearer prefers hat A, keep it in.

### Plan 2: Fits Around. Doesn't Fit Down.

**A. Summary:** Shows why "loosen it" doesn't fix a hat that sits high. Takeaway: circumference and depth are different things, so check depth.

**B. Opening:** A tight side profile with the gap above the ear. Line: "This fits his head. It just doesn't sit on it." Text: "Fits around ≠ fits down".

**C. Beats (~25–35s)**
| Time | See | Hear | Establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Profile gap | Opening line | Problem |
| 3–9s | The strap snapped snug, wearer pulling the hat down and it riding back up | "Snug all the way round." | Fits around |
| 9–16s | Strap loosened two notches, same profile | "Loosen it, it drops a bit, but now it's loose." | The usual fix doesn't work |
| 16–25s | Back to snug. Swap to the LRGR hat at a snug setting, same profile | "Same snug fit, deeper crown." | The depth difference |
| 25–30s | A/B side freeze | Shak: "If yours sits like the left one, it's depth, not size." | Takeaway |

**D. Shots:** Essential: locked side-profile 35mm, a close-up of the strap notches being changed (85mm), the hat being pulled down and riding back up. Optional: a tape measuring crown depth on both hats.

**E. Prompts:** "What do people usually do when it sits high?" / "Does loosening help? Why not?" / "Who does a shallow crown actually work for?"

**F. Production:** Same set as Plan 1 (shoot back to back). One wearer whose head shows the gap with the shallow cap; test this before rolling. Shallow cap from retail.

**G. Edit:** Notch overlay (e.g. "Notch 4 → Notch 6"). Optional matching guide line above the ear. Keep the profile framing identical in every beat.

**H. Ending:** A buying takeaway: "Check depth, not just size."

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* on this head, loosening didn't lower that cap much. *Doesn't show:* every shallow cap, every head. *Risks:* overtightening the comparison, or not disclosing it as one person. Don't overlay any "deeper fit for everyone" text.

### Plan 3: One Hat. Different Heads. No Pretending.

**A. Summary:** A fit reference: the same LRGR model on 4–6 people with their head measurement and strap notch on screen, plus honest verdicts. Takeaway: find the head most like yours.

**B. Opening:** A 2×2 grid of side profiles. Line: "Same hat, five heads. Not everyone was into it." Text: "1 hat · 5 heads · real opinions".

**C. Beats (~40–60s)**
| Time | See | Hear | Establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Grid | Opening line | Format |
| 3–8s per wearer | Tape around the head → hat on → front + side | Wearer's one-liner | Data + preference |
| Last 8s | Grid again, with labels | Shak: what he noticed across them | Pattern, no overclaim |

**D. Shots:** Essential: per wearer, a tape-measure close-up, a strap-notch close-up, locked front and side views, and a verdict line. Optional: a group shot and Shak adjusting a hat on someone.

**E. Prompts (to wearers):** "How do you usually like a hat to sit?" / "Better or worse than what you normally wear?" (to Shak) / "Anyone surprise you?" / "Who did it not suit, and why?"

**F. Production:** 4–6 wearers with a range of circumferences, one LRGR model in an adjustable size, a tape, the same set. Releases that cover use of measurements on screen.

**G. Edit:** A lower-third card per wearer: "58cm · notch 5 · 'Sits right'". Keep the negative verdicts in, and don't put them last as a punchline. Identical framing per wearer.

**H. Ending:** "Comment your head size and I'll tell you who's closest" is a genuine reply opportunity only if Shak will actually answer. Otherwise end on the grid.

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* how one model sat on these people. *Doesn't show:* a universal fit, or comfort over time. *Risks:* cutting the negative verdicts, or choosing only wearers it flatters.

### Plan 4: When I Wouldn't Recommend Ours.

**A. Summary:** Shak openly names who the LRGR shape isn't for and demonstrates it. Takeaway: buyers can self-select, and they trust the rest of what Shak says.

**B. Opening:** Shak lifts the LRGR hat off a wearer and hands them a shallower cap. Line: "Honestly, if you like it sitting up top, ours probably isn't it." No text needed beyond captions.

**C. Beats (~20–30s)**
| Time | See | Hear | Establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | The swap | Opening line | Honesty |
| 3–12s | Wearer in the LRGR hat, side view; Shak points | "See how it comes down over the ears? He doesn't want that." | Limitation |
| 12–20s | Shallow cap, side view | Wearer: why they prefer it | A preference respected |
| 20–28s | Shak to camera | "If you've always found hats sit too high, that's who ours is for." | Who it IS for |

**D. Shots:** Essential: the swap, both side profiles, Shak talking at medium close-up. Optional: Shak's reaction while the wearer explains.

**E. Prompts:** "Who's walked away from your hats and why?" / "What would you tell a mate who likes it sitting high?" / "Did you ever consider making a shallower one?"

**F. Production:** One wearer who genuinely prefers a shallow or low-volume fit, plus one shallow cap. Same set.

**G. Edit:** Minimal cuts, and let Shak's tone carry it. No music sting or "gotcha" edit.

**H. Ending:** A buying takeaway: who it's for.

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* Shak's own view of where the product fits. *Risks:* staging the "honesty" as a humble-brag. Keep the limitation real.

### Plan 5: What Are You Getting For Fifty Bucks?

**A. Summary:** A macro tabletop breakdown of each part of the hat and what it does. Takeaway: what the price pays for, in plain terms.

**B. Opening:** A macro of a thumb pressing the crown structure from the inside. Line: "Forget the logo for a sec. This is the bit that matters." Text: "[Confirmed price] hat. What's it doing?"

**C. Beats (~35–45s):** crown depth with the tape (0–8s) → brim curve and its shape (8–15s) → snapback, notches and closure (15–22s) → 3D embroidery and why it's raised, a look claim only (22–30s) → sweatband (30–36s) → on the head, side view (36–42s).

**D. Shots:** Essential: an 85mm or macro of each detail on a neutral surface, a tape reading in focus, one on-head side profile. Optional: a slider push-ins and a top-down layout of the parts.

**E. Prompts:** "Why that depth?" / "What did you change the brim for?" / "What's the 3D embroidery for: look, durability, both?" / "What would you cut if you had to make it cheaper?"

**F. Production:** One new hat, a tape, a tabletop setup with soft side light. **Confirm the price, currency and materials first.**

**G. Edit:** One label per detail ("Crown depth: [X]cm"). Hold each macro long enough to read it. No "premium" or "luxury" overlays.

**H. Ending:** A product action: "That's the A-frame. Link's there if it's your shape."

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* features and what they're intended to do. *Doesn't show:* durability or performance. *Risks:* claiming embroidery proves quality, inventing materials, or comparing prices with competitors.

### Plan 6: Same Hat. Thirty Wears Later. (Episode 0 at the shoot)

**A. Summary:** Start a documented wear series. Today's video is the baseline inspection and the rules. Takeaway: "We'll show you, not tell you."

**B. Opening:** A fresh hat turning on a turntable. Text: "WEAR 0 / 30". Line: "Wearing this one, nothing else, thirty times. Here's what it looks like now."

**C. Beats, ep 0 (~25s):** turntable (0–4s) → baseline macros of the brim, sweatband, embroidery and strap (4–16s) → the rules: one wearer, logged wears, no special care (16–22s) → "Check back at 10" (22–25s).

**D. Shots:** Essential: a turntable 360° shot, macros of each inspection point on a **marked, repeatable framing** (note the lens, distance and light for the reshoots), a side profile on the head. Optional: a top-down shot.

**E. Prompts:** "What do you expect to go first?" / "What would count as it failing for you?"

**F. Production:** A dedicated hat (mark it inside), a wear log (a shared note: date, activity, weather, hours), and a framing reference photo for every checkpoint.

**G. Edit:** A consistent wear counter and identical framing across episodes. Later episodes use an A/B wipe from wear 0 to wear N.

**H. Ending:** A genuine unresolved test to revisit.

**I. Integrity:** *Shows:* one hat's real wear. *Risks:* swapping in a fresh hat, skipping logged wears, cleaning it without confirmed care instructions, or presenting it as a lab test.

## Deliverable 4: Batch-shoot plan

**Assumed baseline (to confirm):** one shoot day, an indoor neutral set + table + a small outdoor spot, Shak + 1 helper (or Hunter) + 4–6 wearers called in waves.

| Block | Setup | Concepts | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. 08:30–10:00 | Tabletop, macro, side light | 5 (details), 6 (baseline macros + turntable) | Shak only | No wearers needed yet. Lock and record the framing for #6 |
| 2. 10:00–12:00 | Locked profile set: grey wall, tape marks, tripod | 1, then 3 | 1 wearer | Same camera position for both. Check exposure and white balance once, then don't touch |
| 3. 12:30–14:30 | Same set unchanged | 9 (wearers in waves), 4 (wearer who prefers shallow) | 4–6 wearers | The fixed framing carries over, so no reset |
| 4. 14:30–15:30 | Medium close-up of Shak | Talking prompts for all six, pickups, #6 rules | Shak | Interview-style, prompts read off-camera |
| 5. 15:30–16:00 | Outdoor | Optional B-roll of the hat being worn naturally, #6 wear 1 selfie | Shak | Optional coverage only |

**Finished in one session:** 1, 3, 4, 7 (if the price is confirmed), 9.
**Needs existing evidence:** #10 (a genuinely worn hat), #8 (a real development sample), #2 (real guests with a real unworn hat).
**Starts now, finishes later:** #6 (30 logged wears, check-ins at 10/20/30), #5 adjustment count (two scheduled activity sessions).

### Must confirm before the shoot
- [ ] Shak's comfort on camera: talking to camera or interview style
- [ ] Current hat price and currency; materials; care instructions
- [ ] The comparison caps, with exact models (bought retail, disclosed on screen)
- [ ] Wearers and head-size range, plus signed releases (including measurements on screen)
- [ ] Location and the camera kit Shak actually has
- [ ] Whether a real development sample or a genuinely worn hat exists (it decides #8 and #10)
- [ ] Where it's made, if mentioned (don't imply Australian manufacture unless verified)

### Claims rules for every edit
- No waterproof, sweat, heat or wash claims.
- Personal preference and measurement are labelled separately. One person's test = "one person, one test".
- No "built different", "more than a brand", "elevate".
- Results are captured, not scripted. If the LRGR hat loses a comparison, that's still content.
