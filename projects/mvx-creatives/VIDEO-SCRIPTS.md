## How these are built, and why it's modular

Each campaign gets **3 hooks + 1 body + 2 CTAs**, shot as separate takes and assembled in post. That yields **6 cuts per campaign** from 6 takes, because the body and CTAs are reused across every hook.

That structure exists for a reason beyond efficiency. The revised kill rules in [BUILD.md](../mvx-meta-rebuild/BUILD.md) cut on **link CTR at day 7**, and CTR is almost entirely a function of the first three seconds. So the hook is the only variable worth isolating: hold the body constant, vary the hook, and a CTR difference between cuts means something. Vary hook *and* body together and a losing ad tells you nothing about which half lost.

**The 20 seconds:**

| Time | Element | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Hook** | Stop the scroll. Must read with sound off. |
| 0:03–0:15 | **Body** | Who, what, where, and the one differentiator. |
| 0:15–0:20 | **CTA** | One instruction. |

**Sound-off rule:** most of this gets watched muted, so every hook line is also burned-in text on frame. Captions on the whole 20s, brand green on the CTA only and never more than ~10% of the frame, per [[brand-system]].

**⚠️ Two extra hooks per campaign are listed as optional.** The five-ad-set structure in BUILD.md wants five creatives, one per ad set. Three hooks fills three. Filming two more is maybe four minutes of extra shoot time on Monday and it fills the structure exactly — but three is what was briefed, so they're marked optional, not assumed.

# Campaign 1 — General Electrical Work

## The angle, and why it isn't the old one

The paused General Electrician campaign sold **symptoms** — something's broken, call us. That's an emergency frame, emergencies go to Google where people are already searching, and Meta only caught the cheap end: $200-clean hunters and one bloke who'd bought his own unit online. 3 leads at $89 over 30 days.

Same service, different frame: **the list**. Every homeowner has a running tally of small electrical jobs they've been putting off — the fan that stopped, the loose powerpoint, the downlight out since Christmas. That's a *planned* purchase sitting in someone's head for months, which is exactly the behaviour Meta is good at reaching, and it bundles several small jobs into one visit worth actually attending.

It also plays the real differentiator: one licensed trade doing the whole list in one visit.

## Hooks — 0:00–0:03

**Hook 1 — the list** *(lead with this one)*
> "Everyone's got a list. The fan that stopped working, the powerpoint hanging out of the wall, the downlight that's been out since Christmas."

On-screen: `EVERYONE'S GOT A LIST`

**Hook 2 — one visit**
> "If you're getting an electrician out anyway, you may as well get the whole list done while he's here."

On-screen: `GET THE WHOLE LIST DONE`

**Hook 3 — the observation** *(most natural, least salesy)*
> "Most people call us out for one thing, then remember four more while we're standing there."

On-screen: `ONE JOB TURNS INTO FIVE`

<details><summary>Optional hooks 4–5, to fill the five-ad-set structure</summary>

**Hook 4 — the switchboard**
> "If your switchboard still has ceramic fuses in it, that's the one on the list worth moving up."

On-screen: `STILL GOT CERAMIC FUSES?`
⚠️ Confirm with Matt that he wants switchboard upgrade enquiries — it's a bigger job than the rest of the list and may deserve its own campaign rather than a hook.

**Hook 5 — the straight answer**
> "Half the stuff on your list is a ten minute job. The other half isn't. We'll tell you which is which."

On-screen: `WE'LL TELL YOU STRAIGHT`
</details>

## Body — 0:03–0:15

> "We're MVX. Licensed electricians, Gold Coast and Northern NSW.
> Fans, lights, powerpoints, switchboards, safety switches, data — all of it's us, so it all gets done in the one visit.
> And we'll tell you straight what needs doing and what doesn't."

**Why these words:** *licensed* and the geography do the qualifying work in plain speech. The service list is deliberately the small-jobs list, not the full company capability — an ad that mentions medical fitouts and aviation reads as commercial and loses the homeowner. **Data is in there on purpose**: the ACRS Master Cabler licence is real and almost no domestic sparky leads with it. *"What doesn't"* is the trust line — it's the only sentence in the ad that costs MVX money to honour, which is exactly why it lands.

## CTAs — 0:15–0:20

**CTA A — the list, returned** *(pairs best with Hooks 1–3)*
> "Send us your list. We'll come back and tell you what it takes."

On-screen: `SEND US YOUR LIST` · Button: **Get Quote**

**CTA B — lower friction**
> "Tap below, tell us what's on the list, we'll get back to you."

On-screen: `TELL US WHAT'S ON THE LIST` · Button: **Get Quote**

# Campaign 2 — New Builds & Renos

## The angle

Already established and correct in [BUILD-NEWBUILDS.md](../mvx-meta-rebuild/BUILD-NEWBUILDS.md): nobody Googles "electrician" the day they decide to renovate, but they scroll Facebook for months while planning it. Planned work, ten times the job value of a service call.

The video's job is to sound like the electrician who has done this before and knows where it goes wrong — which is **the timing of the electrical decisions**, not the price of them.

## Hooks — 0:00–0:03

**Hook 1 — the bit left too late** *(lead with this one)*
> "If you're building or renovating, electrical is the bit people leave too late."

On-screen: `LEFT TOO LATE`

**Hook 2 — before the plasterer**
> "Work out where your power and your lights are going before the plasterer turns up. Not after."

On-screen: `BEFORE THE PLASTERER`

**Hook 3 — the cheap decision**
> "Building a house is about a hundred decisions. This is one of the cheapest ones to get right early."

On-screen: `CHEAPEST ONE TO GET RIGHT EARLY`

<details><summary>Optional hooks 4–5, to fill the five-ad-set structure</summary>

**Hook 4 — the regret, stated without fear**
> "The thing people say after a reno is never 'too many powerpoints'."

On-screen: `NOBODY SAYS "TOO MANY POWERPOINTS"`

**Hook 5 — works off plans**
> "Send us the plans. We'll tell you what the electrical actually involves before you're committed to anything."

On-screen: `SEND US THE PLANS`
</details>

## Body — 0:03–0:15

> "MVX does the electrical for new builds, renos and extensions. Gold Coast and Northern NSW.
> Rough-in, lighting and power layout off your plans, data, switchboard, right through to fit-off.
> We work in with your builder, and we turn up when we say we will."

**Why these words:** *off your plans* signals this is a planning-stage conversation, which pre-qualifies for the planned-work frame the whole campaign rests on. *Rough-in* and *fit-off* are trade words on purpose — they cost nothing with a homeowner and they signal competence to a builder or an owner-builder, who are the highest-value viewers here.

⚠️ **Two lines need Matt's sign-off before they're spoken:**
- **"we turn up when we say we will"** — the single strongest line available to any trade, and worthless the first time it isn't true. It's a conduct claim, not a timing guarantee, so it clears the no-promises rule in [README.md](README.md) — but Matt has to actually stand behind it.
- **"lighting and power layout"** — deliberately *layout*, not *design*. Don't upgrade this to lighting design unless Matt genuinely offers it as a service.

## CTAs — 0:15–0:20

**CTA A — plans** *(pairs best with Hooks 1–3, 5)*
> "Send through your plans and we'll come back to you with a quote."

On-screen: `SEND THROUGH YOUR PLANS` · Button: **Get Quote**

**CTA B — earlier stage, wider net**
> "Tap below for a free quote on your build. Even if you're still just planning it."

On-screen: `FREE QUOTE ON YOUR BUILD` · Button: **Get Quote**

CTA B deliberately invites people who haven't started. Those are worse leads *today* and the right ones for a campaign whose whole thesis is catching people during the planning months — so if it wins on volume but the leads have no timeline, that's the trade working as designed, not a failure. Judge it at day 14, on the timeline question, per BUILD.md.

## Takes

| # | Take | Setup | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | General Electrical hooks 1–3 | A | Optional: hooks 4–5 |
| 4 | General Electrical body | A | |
| 5–6 | General Electrical CTA A + B | A | |
| 7–9 | New Build hooks 1–3 | B | Optional: hooks 4–5 |
| 10 | New Build body | B | |
| 11–12 | New Build CTA A + B | B | |

**12 takes, 16 with the optional hooks.** Two setups. Shoot all of setup A before moving.

- **Setup A — General Electrical:** in front of the van or at a switchboard. Domestic, tidy, recognisable.
- **Setup B — New Builds:** an actual site — frame, bare studs, exposed rough-in. ⚠️ **This is the shoot's one real dependency: does Matt have site access Monday?** If not, setup B falls back to Matt with plans on a ute tailgate, which is weaker but workable. Worth confirming before Monday, not on Monday.

**Shoot every take vertical 9:16**, framed with the top and bottom thirds clear so captions and the Meta chrome don't cover his face. Same shirt across both setups so takes stay interchangeable.

## Light b-roll

Six to eight shots, 3–5s each, no camera moves — this is texture under the body, not a film.

**General Electrical:** hands screwing a powerpoint faceplate · fan starting to spin · switchboard door opening · testing a safety switch · downlight going in · van door closing with the tool bag.

**New Builds:** cabling run through bare studs · hand marking a stud with a pencil · plans held up in frame · drill going into a joist · switchboard rough-in on a bare wall · wide of the frame with someone working in it.

**Reuse what already exists** before shooting anything twice — `photos/42995.jpg` (gloved hands wiring a powerpoint into bare wall) and `photos/2203.jpg` (ceiling fitting, unfinished room) already cover two of the new-build shots as stills.

## Post

- Burned-in captions, whole 20s. Sound-off is the default viewing condition.
- Hook text on frame for the full 3s, not animated in — motion competes with the scroll instead of stopping it.
- Green on the CTA only, ≤10% of frame.
- **Advantage+ creative enhancements OFF** on every ad — see [BUILD.md](../mvx-meta-rebuild/BUILD.md). Meta will otherwise re-crop the frame and rewrite the copy, and re-cropping a 9:16 with deliberate caption safe zones is exactly the damage that setting does.
- Export one cut per hook, body and CTA held constant within a campaign.
