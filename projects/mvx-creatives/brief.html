Two ads. One about the general electrical work around a house — the small jobs people put off. One about new builds and renos. Matt talks to camera, with close-ups of real work cut over the top.

Each ad gets filmed in three pieces and cut together afterwards: an opening line, a middle, and a sign-off. We film three different opening lines for each ad, because the first three seconds is what decides whether anyone stops scrolling. Same middle every time. That way we can run all three, see which opening works, and keep the winner.

Twelve takes all up. Nothing here takes more than about twenty seconds to say.

02Saying it

  • Talk to the camera like you're talking to a customer at the front door. Same voice.
  • Keep it short and stop. No need to fill the silence.
  • Look at the lens, not the screen.
  • We'll do each one three or four times. The first one's always the worst — that's normal.
  • If you fumble a word, keep going. Don't start again, we'll cut around it.
  • Nothing here is longer than twenty seconds.

04Camera, sound, light

Camera

  • 4K 16:9, framed for a centre 9:16 crop. Cropping 4K to vertical still lands above 1080×1920, so we keep the resolution and can reframe later. Crop guides on — don't eyeball it.
  • 25fps for talking, 50fps for close-ups so they can be slowed.
  • S-Log3, usual pipeline. Lach's grading it to match the rest of the MVX work.
  • Matt sharp from ear to ear. Shallow, not silly.
  • Eyes on the upper third, and keep the top 15% and bottom 20% clear — that's where Facebook's buttons and our captions land.

Sound — the one that ruins the day

Outdoors, next to a van, on a site. Wind is the actual risk, not the camera.

  • Lav on Matt, deadcat on it, cable hidden.
  • On-camera mic running as backup, always.
  • Headphones on all day. Check between spots, not at the end.
  • Plane, drop saw or truck over a take — call it and go again straight away. Five seconds now, unfixable later.

Light

  • Avoid roughly 11am–2pm. Morning or late arvo, or find shade.
  • Open shade, or backlit with a bounce. No hard sun on his face.
  • Indoors at a switchboard: small LED to lift his face, let the practicals do the rest.

07The edit

Round one: three cuts per ad. Six all up.

One per opening line, all using the same middle and sign-off A. That's it.

Hold sign-off B back. We test the opening lines first; once one wins, sign-off B becomes the next thing we test against it. Cutting all twelve combinations now means six of them never run.

Every cut

  • 20 seconds, 1080×1920, H.264.
  • Captions burned in the whole way through. These get watched on mute — that's the default, not the exception.
  • Opening line on screen as text too, held the full three seconds. Static, not animated in — movement competes with the scroll instead of stopping it.
  • Brand green on the sign-off only, no more than about 10% of the frame.
  • Close-ups cut under the middle. Matt on screen for the opening line and the sign-off.
  • One grade across all six so they look like a set.
On upload

Turn Advantage+ creative enhancements off on every ad — both text improvements and visual improvements. Meta will otherwise re-crop the frame and rewrite the copy, and re-cropping a vertical we framed deliberately for caption safe zones is exactly the damage that setting does.
