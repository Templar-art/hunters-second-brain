### What it tells us (patterns)
1. They run **one core idea in many near-identical versions** (4 copies of the same scam-answer ad). Cheap volume of variants, not new ideas.
2. **Long runners** (running 8-10+ months) are plain offer ads: "viral/#1" hook + feature ticks + big discount + guarantee. Boring and profitable.
3. **Objection-handling is their hook** ("scam?", "5 reasons NOT to buy").
4. They use **advertorial landing pages** (/sp/...) per ad angle, not the product page.
5. **One-body-area ads** (arms) for sub-audiences.
6. **Calendar offers** each month (Halloween, BF, Xmas).
7. They rely on **a doctor persona, trial stats, fat and cellulite claims, fake "was" pricing and fake scarcity**. Not safe for us.

## PART 4: Persona and "trial", what we can truthfully do
- **Persona (OK):** Hunter as the founder with a true story and no fake credentials, OR a named original animated brand character (G/H characters are already original).
- **Persona (NOT OK):** an invented doctor or expert, or a fake named customer presented as real. ACCC + Meta ban it, and the store is the one that gets hit.
- **Trial (OK):** a REAL one. Ship 20-30 units at cost (~A$900-1,350), 30 days of use, short survey, publish the real result with the sample size ("In our first trial, X of Y women said..."). About 5-6 weeks. Recruit through an ad: "Join our first 50 testers".
- **Trial (NOT OK):** invented percentages or "clinical trial" wording. Even "trial" needs to be true.
