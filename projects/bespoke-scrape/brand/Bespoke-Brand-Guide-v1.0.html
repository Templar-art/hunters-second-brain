05, Photography & Video

Photography Style

Photography is the primary brand signal on social and digital. Every image should feel intentional, premium, and real, not staged or filtered into a different reality.

At a property, in context
Natural light, warm, clean
Candid-leaning, Gold Coast lifestyle

Photography Do's

  • Shoot the agent in context, at a property, walking a site, in conversation
  • Prioritise natural light; warm but neutral colour temperature
  • Smart casual to business casual wardrobe depending on context
  • Candid-leaning compositions, show real moments
  • Lifestyle framing for property, show how spaces are lived in
  • Clean, uncluttered backgrounds that support the subject
  • Jason should appear in the majority of brand content

Photography Don'ts

  • No stiff, overly formal headshots as primary content
  • No cluttered or distracting backgrounds
  • No heavy filters or over-edited skin tones
  • No generic stock-style photography
  • No harsh artificial lighting or flash shadows
  • No environments that don't reflect the Gold Coast lifestyle
  • No artificially perfect or overly staged compositions
06, Photography & Video

Video Guidelines

Video is the primary trust-building tool, it is where personality and expertise come through. Every piece should feel intentional and well-considered. Treat every frame as a brand statement.

Hook Rule
2s
Hook must land within the first 2 seconds. Deliver value before the 5-second mark.
Reels / Stories
9:16
Vertical format for all Reels, Stories, and TikTok-style content.
Repurpose / Web
16:9
Landscape for YouTube, website embeds, and long-form repurposing.

Video Do's

  • Prioritise audio quality above all else, reshoot if audio is poor
  • Steady, well-framed shots, no unplanned shaky footage
  • Colour grade: warm, clean, consistent across all edits
  • Agent speaks direct to camera where appropriate
  • On-screen text reinforces the spoken word, never replaces it
  • Consistent lower-third style, fonts, and colour grade across all edits
  • Cinematic but not overproduced, intentional, not flashy

Video Don'ts

  • No unplanned shaky footage presented as primary content
  • No clickbait titles or thumbnails
  • No music that contradicts the brand tone
  • No content where the agent looks unprepared, reshoot
  • No overproduced, flashy edits that feel artificial
  • No inconsistent colour grades across the content library
  • No text that overwhelms or replaces the spoken content
Colour Grade Reference: All Bespoke video should share the same grade, warm shadows, lifted blacks, clean highlights with a slight green tint in the shadows to reflect the brand palette. Avoid heavy LUTs, over-saturated colours, or colour casts that feel like Instagram filters. Aim for the aesthetic of a premium architectural photography video.

10, Copywriting

Tone of Voice

Bespoke communicates like a trusted local expert, someone who knows the Gold Coast market inside out but doesn't feel the need to prove it. Confidence comes through knowledge, not volume.

Relatable
People-first, speaks to the person
Connectable
Warm without being casual
Informative
Lead with insight, not hype
Expert
Results and knowledge do the talking
Grounded
Professional without being cold

Trust-Transfer Language

Phrases that connect Jason's authority to the Bespoke brand

"I've sold properties in this street twice, I know what buyers are looking for here and what they'll pay."
"Most agents will tell you to renovate before selling. Most of the time, they're wrong. Here's why."
"The Gold Coast market isn't one market, it's 30 different micro-markets. Let me show you what's happening in yours."
"I don't just sell properties. I manage the sale so my clients don't have to think about it."

Voice Do's

  • Speak to the person, not the property
  • Lead with insight, share what you know
  • Use plain language, no unnecessary jargon
  • Let results and expertise do the talking
  • Warm tone, professional delivery
  • Short paragraphs, one idea per paragraph
  • Use first person (Jason's voice) for organic content

Voice Don'ts

  • No boastful, flashy, or self-congratulatory language
  • No corporate, stiff, or impersonal tone
  • No desperate, promotional, or click-baity copy
  • No jargon, if you need to explain a term, simplify it
  • No passive voice when active voice is available
  • No vague superlatives, "amazing", "incredible", "best"
  • No em dashes, use commas, colons, or short sentences instead

Hook Framework

Every piece of content, organic or paid, needs a hook that stops the scroll within the first 2 seconds. These templates are starting points. Always adapt to the specific property, suburb, or market context.

Type Template Psychological Trigger
Pattern Interrupt
Stop the scroll
 "Most people think the Gold Coast market is [X]. Here's what's actually happening right now." Challenges assumptions. Creates a knowledge gap the viewer wants to close.
Direct Callout
Audience identification
 "If you're thinking of selling your home in [Suburb] this year, watch this before you do anything else." Immediate relevance. The viewer self-selects in, this is for them.
Myth Buster
Curiosity + authority
 "No, you don't need to renovate before selling. Here's why most agents get this wrong." Contradicts conventional wisdom. Positions Jason as the smarter choice.
Data Shock
Credibility + urgency
 "[X]% of properties in [Suburb] sold above asking in the last 90 days. Here's what that means for you." Real numbers create authority and urgency. Hard to ignore.
Demonstration
Show don't tell
 "I walked through 3 properties last week. Here's the honest breakdown, what they're worth and why." Builds trust through transparency and real access. Jason as the insider.
Social Proof Open
Risk reduction
 "We had 4 offers by the first open home. Here's exactly what we did differently." Proven results reduce perceived risk. Seller imagines their own outcome.
