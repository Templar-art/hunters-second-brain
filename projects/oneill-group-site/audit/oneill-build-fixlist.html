Does this look AI-generated? No.

That should be said first, because it is the hardest part to get right and this site has it. The palette is committed and uncommon — sage, cream and basalt, no pure white and no pure black. Real photography carries the design. No gradient text, no glass cards, no hero-metric row, no grid of identical icon-and-heading tiles. The copy has a voice: "A family name, not a call centre", "Your address is not a line on a board". Someone made decisions here.

Two house rules do get tripped. A kicker sits above nearly every section — ABOUT US, HOW WE WORK, INSIGHTS, GET IN TOUCH, LEGAL, WHAT WE LOOK FOR. One named kicker is voice; a kicker as section grammar is scaffolding. And 01 / 02 / 03 appears on things that are not sequences. The numbering on "How we work" (listen, care, deliver) is legitimate and should stay. Buy / Sell / Lease and "four things we look for" are parallel lists and should lose it.

The automated scan returned 97 findings across the 9 pages. The substantive ones: the body face is Inter and Elementor is still pulling Roboto; the header animates width and height on scroll, which thrashes layout on a fixed element during the most frequent interaction on the site; and 20 build comments ship in the production source, including "Inert. Nothing here is wired up yet." and "Three tiles, one photo, three crops." Anyone who views source reads the to-do list.

Overall impression

This is a good-looking site that has not yet been asked what it is for.

The craft is real. The interior page heroes are the best thing on it — "PUT YOUR NAME TO IT" over a dusk exterior with a three-column fact strip underneath is a confident, composed piece of design that most Gold Coast agencies could not produce. The restraint is genuine rather than timid.

But the homepage opens with 756 px of dark video that says nothing, and the site never once asks for a phone call above the footer. An agency website has two jobs: convince a vendor to list, and let a buyer find stock. Right now the design is optimised for a third thing, which is being admired. The biggest opportunity is not visual. It is deciding what the visitor should do, then letting the design carry them there.

What's working

The interior page heroes. Our People and Careers both open with a display headline, a supporting paragraph offset right, and a hairline-divided fact strip along the bottom, all on a dusk photograph. Asymmetric without being arbitrary, and the fact strip does real work — three short answers to the three questions a visitor actually has. This is the pattern the homepage should be borrowing.

The colour discipline. Three grounds, used consistently, alternating for rhythm rather than decoration. Uncommon for the category, which is exactly why it reads as considered. The only failures are places where sage is asked to carry text it cannot support — a token-application problem, not a palette problem.

The copy voice. Plain, Australian, unsentimental, free of agency cliché. "We're not a franchise, and we're not a boutique that only opens the door for the top end of the market" does more positioning in one sentence than most agency about-pages manage in a page.

Priority design issues

  1. P0
    The first fold makes no argument

    A prospect sees a dark abstract video, six nav items, and nothing else. No headline, no proposition, no search, no number, no action. The mission statement that would do this work sits below the fold, and on mobile the video crops to unreadable shapes. Every other page opens with a headline and a supporting paragraph — the homepage is the only one that opens with silence, and it gets the most traffic.

    Fix: bring the mission statement onto the video using the Careers hero pattern — display headline, one supporting line, a fact strip or single primary action along the bottom. Video stays as ground, not content. Command: /impeccable shape

  2. P0
    There is no path to a phone call

    This is an agency; the conversion is a conversation. The header carries six nav items and zero calls to action. There is no phone number anywhere above the footer, and the footer number is a placeholder. The one real conversion point requires a free-text message before it will submit. The homepage CTA band then offers five exits at once — Buy, Sell, Lease, Contact Us, Request a Property Quote — with no primary among them.

    Fix: a persistent call action in the header with the real number. One primary action per section, visually dominant. Message field optional. Command: /impeccable shape

  3. P1
    The scroll wall spends the page's whole attention budget on four sentences

    Six full viewports to deliver four short statements, sitting between the About block and the testimonials, with scroll speed hijacked throughout. It is also where the art direction fails: "WE LISTEN" works because the type sits on the dark left third of its frame; "WE CARE" puts cream type on a white kitchen and is unreadable, as are the closing two frames. There is no rule governing type placement against photo brightness, so each frame works or fails by luck.

    Fix: cut the dwell to three viewports or fewer. Add a brightness-adaptive scrim behind the type block, tuned per frame. Command: /impeccable distill

  4. P1
    The three commercial pages are the emptiest on the site

    Buy It, Sell It and Lease It are structurally identical: one headline, the shared listings grid, the shared CTA, nothing between. Sell It is the highest-value page on the site — a vendor choosing an agency is the decision that pays for everything — and it contains one sentence of unique content. The grid isn't even filtered per page, so Lease It shows Sold listings.

    Fix: each page needs one section only it could carry. For Sell It that is the campaign process, what the fee covers, and proof. Filter the grid per page. Command: /impeccable shape

  5. P1
    Two type voices, and only one has character

    The display face carries the whole brand and is the reason the site looks expensive. The body face is Inter — the most common typeface on the web — so all the personality lives in the headings and none survives into the paragraphs. The display face is also Sinhala MN, an Apple system font repurposed with metric overrides and shipped as a 322 KB unsubset WOFF. The identity rests on a font that was never licensed for this.

    Fix: a licensed display serif of the same voice, subsetted. Replace Inter with a body face chosen against it on a real contrast axis rather than defaulted to. Command: /impeccable typeset

Walked through as three visitors

Jordan, first-timer, arrived from a For Sale board. Lands on the homepage, sees a dark video and no message, has no idea whether this agency covers their suburb or price bracket. Scrolls to the grid and finds nine cards reading "00 Placeholder Street". Tries the search; nothing happens, because it is inert. Sees badges saying "Own It" and "Preparing It" and cannot tell which means available. Gives up before the About section.

The Vendor, interviewing three agencies — the decision that matters. Goes straight to Sell It. Finds one headline, placeholder listings, and a form. No process, no fee structure, no recent sales, no named agent. Goes to Our People to see who they would deal with and finds six cards showing the same stock photograph of the same man, named PLACEHOLDER ONE through SIX, sharing one phone number. The page meant to prove there are real people behind the family name currently proves the opposite.

The Buyer, on a phone, on the couch. The hero video crops to abstract dark rings. The header shows three inline links about 40×20 px plus a hamburger — two navigation systems competing in one strip. Scrolls, and the white nav pill sits on top of whatever they were reading. Reaches the testimonials and the quotes are sliced mid-word at both edges. Wants to call; there is no number until the footer, and it reads 07 0000 0000.

Smaller notes

20 HTML build comments ship in production source. "PROPERTY IS PERSONAL" is used twice on the homepage. The kicker hairline bleeds off-viewport on interior heroes but is inset on the homepage — pick one. The ON monogram tile straddles a section boundary and reads as mispositioned. A small empty rectangle sits beside the scroll wall's progress dashes. Careers leaves a large empty column beside the "Four things" heading. Bracketed contact details read as unfilled template slots, especially while the values inside them are placeholders. The listing status vocabulary extends the Buy It / Sell It / Lease It naming somewhere it does not work — nav labels can be voice, status badges need to be scannable.

Questions worth answering before the next build pass

If a vendor could only see one page before choosing an agency, which would you want it to be, and does that page currently earn the choice?

The interior heroes are better than the homepage hero. What stops the homepage from using the same pattern?

The scroll wall is the most expensive thing on the site in attention. Cut to three frames — which one goes, and does anyone notice?

What is the site's single most important action, and where is it on the screen right now?

The restraint is real. Is the silence on the first fold restraint, or an unmade decision wearing restraint's clothes?

Technical quality pass: accessibility, performance, responsive behaviour, broken wiring. Organised by surface. Scored separately from the critique above.

Site-wide

Applies to every page. Fix once, fixes nine.

  1. P0
    15 freight-and-logistics demo surfaces are still live

    The theme kit was never cleaned out. Everything below returns 200 and carries an "… - O'Neill Group" title:

    1 page. /services/ — "Freight & Logistics Services", Road / Air / Sea / Rail Freight cards with emoji in the headings, a stock aeroplane-and-shipping-container hero, a "Customized Pricing for Professional Logistics Operations" table, 12 empty headings, and lorem copy beginning "Was justice improve age article between."

    7 posts. /blog-logistic-v1/ plus /how-modern-logistics-improves-efficiency/, /managing-project-cargo-successfully-today/, /how-technology-transforms-supply-chains/, /reducing-costs-through-smarter-logistics/, /how-customs-brokerage-simplifies-trade/, /building-resilient-supply-chain-networks/. All lorem, filed under categories "Insights, Shipping, Trucking", dated December 2025, and each showing "1 Comment" — so comments are open and taking spam.

    7 orphan templates in wpr_templates-sitemap.xml, all named logistic-v1: header, footer, 404, single-post, single-page, blog archive, category/tag archive. The 404 template is the one serving the broken /news/ link.

    Fix: delete all 15, confirm each slug 404s, delete the Royal template kit, then clear the freight stock imagery out of the media library. Regenerate the sitemap and confirm it lists only the 8 real pages.

  2. P2
    The author sitemap exposes the admin username

    author-sitemap.xml publishes /author/admin_bj1b251x/. Handing an attacker a valid username halves the work of a brute-force attempt on wp-login.

    Fix: disable author archives in Yoast, and rate-limit or restrict wp-login.php.

  3. P2
    No favicon.ico

    The icon links are declared as JPGs, which browsers accept, but /favicon.ico itself 404s — some crawlers and older clients request it directly.

    Fix: add a real favicon.ico and export the icon set as PNG rather than JPG.

  4. P2
    The 404 page is the theme default

    "Error 404 / Oops, page was not found… / Back to Home" — generic, with an ellipsis, no search, no suggested links, and none of the voice the rest of the site has. It matters more than usual here because the footer's broken /news/ link sends people to it from every page.

    Fix: write it in the site's own voice and offer the three service pages plus contact as exits.

  5. P0
    The staging site is fully indexable

    No <meta name="robots">, no X-Robots-Tag, robots.txt has an empty Disallow:, and Yoast is publishing a sitemap. Everything above — including the freight page and every "00 Placeholder Street" — is crawlable, and will compete with the real domain later.

    Fix: site-wide noindex, nofollow plus HTTP auth on the staging host until launch.

  6. P0
    No meta description on any of the 9 pages

    Yoast is installed but the field is empty everywhere, so og:description falls back to a dump of the whole page. The homepage's is several thousand characters long and contains "00 Placeholder Street" nine times. That is what appears when anyone shares the link in Slack, WhatsApp or Facebook.

    Fix: write a 150–160 character description per page. Set a proper og:image (currently a 2560 px PNG).

  7. P1
    The display serif is an Apple system font, self-hosted

    SinhalaMN.woff, 322 KB — the single heaviest asset on the site after the hero photo. It is Sinhala MN, bundled with macOS, re-served with ascent-override hacks and a Sinhala MN Web alias. It is unsubset (the entire Sinhala script rides along, unused) and it is WOFF, not WOFF2. There is a licensing question here as well as a weight one.

    Fix: swap for a licensed display serif with the same voice, subset to Latin, served as WOFF2. Expect 30–50 KB. This changes every heading on the site, so do it early.

  8. P1
    Unused Google Fonts loading on every page

    Elementor's defaults are pulling Roboto and Roboto Slab at all 18 weights each, italics included, and none of it is used. Inter is requested from Google ten separate times.

    Fix: set Elementor's default fonts to the real stack so the Roboto requests stop; consolidate the Inter requests into one.

  9. P1
    Reveal animations hide content until JavaScript runs

    Sections ship as opacity: 0 and only become visible when a script adds .is-in — for example .onp-tile { opacity: 0; transform: translateY(30px) }. If the observer doesn't fire (prerender, a crawler, a background tab, a JS error) the section ships blank. There is a prefers-reduced-motion override that forces them visible, which is good, but it doesn't cover the failure case.

    Fix: invert it. Content visible by default; the script adds a .will-reveal class that sets the start state, so a page with no JS is a page with no animation rather than a page with no content.

  10. P1
    No <main> landmark and no skip link

    A keyboard or screen-reader user tabs through the whole header on every page before reaching content. Two of the seven <nav> elements also have no aria-label.

    Fix: wrap the page body in <main id="main">, add a visually-hidden skip link as the first focusable element, label the remaining navs.

  11. P2
    Locale is US

    <html lang="en-US"> and og:locale = en_US on an entirely Gold Coast business.

    Fix: en-AU in both places.

  12. P2
    No canonical tags

    Fix: enable Yoast canonicals, pointing at the production domain once it exists.

  13. P2
    Homepage is 1.48 MB over 66 requests

    671 KB images, 369 KB fonts, 190 KB scripts, 149 KB CSS. Five plugin bundles (Elementor, Royal Addons, ElementsKit, Essential Addons, Formidable) plus jQuery and jQuery-migrate. Largest single asset: 47-Somersert-Drive-_-Web-16-scaled.jpg at 511 KB.

    Fix: serve WebP at the size actually rendered, audit which of the five addon plugins are genuinely in use, and dequeue the rest per page.

Header

One global header, position: fixed, 141 px tall, z-index: 1000. Present on all 9 pages, so every item here is a nine-page fix.

  1. P0
    The header covers content at almost every scroll position

    Once you scroll, the nav collapses to a white pill and floats over whatever is beneath it. It is not an occasional clash, it is constant. On the homepage alone it covers: the first line of the "YOUR GOLD COAST PROPERTY JOURNEY" mission statement, the OWN IT / PREPARING IT badges on the listing cards, the "MARKET UPDATE" heading, and the top of the CTA band. Same on mobile, worse — the pill is proportionally larger.

    Fix: two parts. Add scroll-padding-top to :root and scroll-margin-top to section anchors so jump links land clear. Then either shrink the pill materially on scroll or hide it on scroll-down and reveal on scroll-up, so it is never resting on top of a heading.

  2. P0
    Nav text is white with no dark variant, so it disappears on light pages

    On /privacy-policy/ — a cream page with no hero image — the nav measures 1.38:1 against the background. The wordmark goes with it. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1. The same problem shows on the light-hero pages and over the pale sections of /contact/, /our-people/ and /careers/.

    Fix: add a dark header state (basalt text, no white pill) and apply it on any page without a dark hero, plus on scroll once past a dark hero. Measure both states.

  3. P1
    Mobile header shows an inline nav and a hamburger

    At 390 px the header carries the wordmark, "BUY IT SELL IT LEASE IT" as inline links, and a hamburger. The three inline links sit roughly 8 px apart and measure about 40×20 px each. Two navigation systems, competing, both cramped.

    Fix: pick one. Either the hamburger holds everything, or the three primary links stay and the hamburger goes.

  4. P1
    Every nav link is under the 44 px tap-target minimum

    Desktop links measure 78×32 px (97×32 for "OUR PEOPLE"). Mobile is smaller again. Between 20 and 37 sub-44 px targets per page, and the header accounts for six of them on all nine.

    Fix: increase vertical padding on the anchor itself, not the wrapper, so the hit area grows without moving the text.

  5. P1
    The current page is marked visually but not programmatically

    The active item gets a CSS underline. There is no aria-current="page" anywhere on the site, so a screen reader has no idea where it is.

    Fix: add aria-current="page" to the active item and drive the underline off that attribute rather than a separate class.

  6. P1
    The search form has no visible focus ring

    Tabbing through the homepage: the seven header links all show a focus outline correctly, then focus enters the property search and the ring disappears. Measured outline: 0px none on the text input and on the SEARCH button, and outline-style: none on all four selects. A keyboard user loses track of where they are inside the one interactive component on the page. WCAG 2.4.7.

    Fix: a :focus-visible ring on every field and the button, using the same treatment the header links already have.

  7. P1
    Keyboard focus lands underneath the fixed header

    Same root cause as the overlap issue above, confirmed with the keyboard: once focus moves past the header, focused elements scroll to the top of the viewport and sit under the 141 px bar. The user cannot see what they have focused.

    Fix: scroll-padding-top on :root matching the header height. Same fix as the overlap issue, so do them together.

  8. P2
    Header height changes at every breakpoint

    141 px at 1440, 137 px at 1024, 94 px at 768. Whatever offset gets used to fix the overlap has to track all three rather than being a single hard-coded value.

    Fix: drive the offset from a CSS custom property set per breakpoint, or measure the header at runtime.

  9. P2
    Logos are 2560 px PNGs in 82 px and 159 px slots

    Oneill-Property-White-Logo-scaled.png is 2560 px wide rendering at 159 px; Oneill-Property-Icon-Basalt-scaled.png is 2560 px rendering at 82 px. About 155 KB of PNG for two marks, on every page.

    Fix: use the SVGs. They already exist at projects/oneill-group-site/preloader/assets/oneill-lockup.svg and oneill-mark.svg.

Footer

One global footer on all 9 pages. Same multiplier as the header.

  1. P0
    "NEWS" links to a 404

    /news/ returns 404 and there is no news page in the sitemap, but the link is in the footer on every page — nine broken links.

    Fix: build the news index (the homepage already has a "VIEW ALL" that needs the same destination) or remove the item until it exists.

  2. P0
    All three social links are dead

    Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn all have an empty href. On every page. Clicking does nothing.

    Fix: real URLs, target="_blank" rel="noopener", and an aria-label on each since they are icon-and-word links.

  3. P0
    Placeholder phone and a wrong email domain, sitewide

    The footer shows [ 07 0000 0000 ] wired to tel:+61700000000, and hello@oneillgroup.com.au. Every other email on the site uses oneillproperty.com.au — the footer is the only place using oneillgroup. Address is just "Gold Coast, Queensland".

    Fix: confirm the real number, email and street address with the client and use one set everywhere. See the Contact page item below — right now there are three competing versions.

  4. P1
    Text over an unscrimmed photo

    The footer sits on a property photo with only a light overlay. Where the photo is bright — the upper-left on mobile especially — the white column headings and links lose contrast against it. Contrast varies pixel to pixel, which means it cannot be measured or guaranteed.

    Fix: either a flat basalt ground, or keep the photo and put a solid scrim over it at an opacity that holds white text everywhere in the frame.

  5. P2
    Square brackets around the phone and email read as unfilled placeholders

    [ 07 0000 0000 ] and [ hello@... ]. With placeholder values inside them, the brackets look like a template that was never filled rather than a design device. On the privacy page the same bracket convention is an unfilled placeholder, which muddies it further.

    Fix: keep the brackets only if they survive next to real details; otherwise drop them.

  6. P2
    2560 px logo PNG again

    Fix: same SVG swap as the header.

Home — /

12,797 px tall on desktop, 13,831 px on mobile. The longest and most ambitious page on the site, and where most of the remaining work sits.

Structure

  1. P0
    No <h1>

    The page begins at H2 ("OUR PROPERTIES"). The homepage of the site has no top-level heading at all.

    Fix: make the mission statement — "Your Gold Coast property journey, guided by O'Neill Property" — the H1. It is already the first real statement on the page and it reads as one.

  2. P0
    Two empty <h2> elements

    Both in the "HOW WE WORK" scroll section. They register in the outline as headings with no text.

    Fix: give them their real text or change them to non-heading elements.

  3. P1
    "PROPERTY IS PERSONAL" is used twice

    Once as the About section H2, then again as the closing statement of the scroll wall a few thousand pixels later.

    Fix: pick one home for it. The wall outro is the stronger placement.

Hero

  1. P0
    The first screen carries no message and no call to action

    756 px of dark video with the nav floating over it. No headline, no CTA, no property search. A visitor's entire first fold says nothing beyond the logo. The statement doesn't appear until they scroll.

    Fix: this is a design decision, not a bug, so it needs a call. Either pull the mission statement up onto the video, or commit to the silent-video opener and make the scroll cue unmistakable. Right now it is neither.

  2. P1
    The hero video has no poster frame

    1920×1080, preload="metadata", poster="". Until it buffers there is nothing to look at.

    Fix: add a poster — the first frame, exported as a small WebP — so the fold is never empty.

  3. P1
    The ghost O'NEILL watermark is cut off at both edges

    The oversized wordmark across the bottom of the hero runs past the left and right edges mid-letter. It reads as a rendering error rather than a deliberate bleed, and it is worse on mobile where only two or three letters remain.

    Fix: either scale it to fit with intentional margin, or bleed it far enough that it obviously reads as a texture. Halfway is the problem.

  4. P1
    The video crop is unreadable on mobile

    At 390 px the 16:9 frame crops to a portrait sliver of abstract dark rings. It is not identifiable as property, interiors, or anything else.

    Fix: a separate portrait crop for narrow viewports, or a still image on mobile instead of the video.

Listings block

  1. P1
    All 9 listing cards have an empty href

    Expected while the content is filler, but the cards are already marked up as links, so the wiring is the outstanding job.

    Fix: point them at the listing CPT once it exists.

  2. P1
    The search field has no label and is 12.5 px

    "Suburb or address" is a placeholder only — once someone types, the field's purpose disappears. The four <select>s are labelled; the text input is not. All five are 12.5 px, and anything under 16 px makes iOS Safari zoom the page on focus.

    Fix: add a visually-hidden <label>, and lift the font size to 16 px on mobile.

Buy / Sell / Lease triptych

  1. P1
    All three tiles are the same photograph

    One image, 47-Somersert-Drive-_-Web-8-scaled.jpg, used three times and zoomed 1.55–1.62× with different offsets to fake three shots. The zoom makes each crop soft, and the crops land on the same architectural lines, so the row reads as mismatched bands of one blurry photo. The source even carries the note: "Three tiles, one photo, three crops. When you have a separate image per service, change each src."

    Fix: three real photographs, one per service, and delete the inline --z / --tx / --ty zoom values so each centres normally.

  2. P1
    01 / 02 / 03 on a set that is not a sequence

    Buy, sell and lease are three parallel services. Numbering them implies an order that doesn't exist, and it repeats a pattern already ruled out on 10x's own pages.

    Fix: drop the numerals. The hairline under each title already carries the cadence. The numbering on "HOW WE WORK" (listen → care → deliver) can stay, because that one genuinely is a sequence.

"How we work" scroll wall

  1. P0
    "WE CARE" is unreadable — cream type on a near-white kitchen

    The panel behind it is a bright white kitchen. The heading and the body copy beneath it are both cream, with no scrim, landing on white cabinetry and white tile. Effective contrast is around 1.2:1. The same failure hits the "PROPERTY IS PERSONAL" outro and the closing logo frame, both of which sit on a bright bathroom.

    Fix: an adaptive scrim behind the type — a gradient sized to the text block, with peak opacity tuned per frame. The bright interiors need roughly twice the scrim of the dark ones. This is the same brightness-adaptive scrim rule 10x already applies to type on footage.

  2. P1
    The section is 5,400 px tall for four statements

    Six full viewports of pinned scrolling on desktop, more on mobile, to deliver four short lines. It is the single longest section on the site and it sits between the About block and the testimonials, so a visitor heading for the contact CTA has to scroll through all of it.

    Fix: tighten each statement's dwell so the whole wall lands in three viewports or fewer.

  3. P2
    A stray empty box next to the progress dashes

    A small unfilled rectangle sits to the right of the four progress dashes at the bottom of the wall, roughly 30×16 px, with no visible content.

    Fix: identify and remove, or give it whatever content it was meant to hold.

Testimonials

  1. P1
    Quotes are chopped mid-word at both edges

    The marquee container is overflow: visible with no mask, so the first and last cards are sliced through the middle of words — "…eeks, for / …hout a" is what a visitor actually reads at the left edge. It looks like a clipping bug rather than a scroller.

What this audit did not cover

Listed so nobody assumes a clean bill on things that were never tested. Worth a second pass once the items above are done.

  1. P1
    Form submission was never exercised

    Nothing was submitted, so validation behaviour, inline error messages, the success state, the notification email, and whether entries actually land anywhere are all unverified. Given the form is the site's only conversion point, this is the most important untested thing on the list.

    Test: submit valid and invalid entries, confirm the notification email arrives at a real inbox, confirm the honeypot or spam guard is on.

  2. P1
    Safari and iOS were not tested

    Everything here is Chrome. Safari differs on the things this site leans on hardest: video autoplay policy, aspect-ratio, scroll-driven animation, backdrop blur, and 100vh handling with a dynamic toolbar. The pinned scroll wall is the most likely thing to behave differently.

    Test: real iOS Safari on a handset, not a simulator, with particular attention to the hero video and the scroll wall.

  3. P2
    The mobile menu drawer was only partly checked

    The trigger itself is well built — 46×46 px, aria-label="Open menu", aria-expanded="false", aria-controls="op-menu", which is better than most of the site. But the probe could not confirm the panel opening, so the open state, focus trapping, Escape-to-close, and background scroll-lock are all unverified.

    Test: open it on a real device; confirm focus moves into the panel, Escape closes it, and the page behind does not scroll.

  4. P2
    No screen reader pass and no real Core Web Vitals

    Accessibility findings here are measured (contrast ratios, heading structure, tab order, tap targets) but nothing was tested with VoiceOver or NVDA. Performance figures are transfer weight and request counts, not throttled LCP, CLS or INP — and the pinned scroll wall plus the header's width/height transition are exactly the kind of thing that hurts INP.

    Test: Lighthouse on mobile throttling, plus one VoiceOver pass over the homepage and the contact form.

  5. P2
    Hover and active states, and the print stylesheet

    Hover transitions are declared in the CSS and look considered, but were not exercised. No print styles were checked — relevant only because people do print property pages.

Contrast figures are computed WCAG 2.1 ratios measured against the element's actual rendered background. Ratios are not given for type sitting directly on photographs, because those vary across the frame and cannot be stated as a single number — those are flagged by description instead. Tested in Chrome at 1440×900, 1024×768, 768×1024 and 390×844.
